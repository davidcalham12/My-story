# Chapter 1 — The Seventh Step

The water went off the causeway at twenty past two and Ada Rowe went down the stair behind it, one hand on the wet rope, counting steps she had not needed to count in eleven years.

Three petitions on the seventh step. Two weighted with stones, one with a scrap of net float, grey, the twine gone to fur.

Nell Harker stood four steps below with her boots in the standing water and did not come up. She never had. She looked at the papers in Ada's hand and then out at the shoal, where the Reach lay flat and brown and the drowned street showed its gable ends.

"Low water again tomorrow," Ada said.

"If it's written," Nell said.

Ada climbed. Behind her the stair dripped and the Corbie Light stood unlit above it, waiting on the sun.

In the lamp room she laid the three papers out and read them in the order she had lifted them. A hand she knew: *Sweet Providence, smack, lost off the Ness, my grandfather's.* She wiped the nib on the inside of her left cuff, which was black to the elbow and had been for years, and wrote: *2.41, east by north, Sweet Providence, smack.* Iron-gall, one line, the hour of the sighting and the hour of the writing the same hour. She read it back. It stood.

The second she entered likewise. The third she did not.

The third said *Marigold* in Nell Harker's square, patient letters, and Ada set it flat at the desk's edge with the net float on it and left it there, unwritten, as she had left it every low water for two years.

Thomas Creed came up the causeway at four with his case under his arm and salt to his knees. He took the last quarter's returns out and went down the unmatched names at half voice, the way he did everything — "*Sweet Providence*, no return; *Kestrel*, no return; *Ann Bligh*, no return" — and stopped when she set the cup down beside him, and started the column again from the top.

"Spring tide in two days," he said. "Dawn of the sixteenth. I'll audit the book page by page and the oil against the hours."

"It'll be open."

"Mistress Rowe." He squared the returns. "The *Marigold* stands twice in your hand. Enter her a third time and she goes to the Drowned Page, and I will countersign her there myself, and the name is closed. Never entered again. Not by you, not by anyone after you."

Ada looked at the water beginning to feel its way back along the causeway, filling the ruts first.

"You'd best go down," she said, "while there's road."

He went. The flood took the stones behind him a length at a time, and by the time he reached the Ridge there was no causeway, only the Reach, and the seventh step under two feet of it.

The petition lay flat on the desk under its net float. The name on it stood nowhere in the book. The tide clock went on and on, louder than the sea.
