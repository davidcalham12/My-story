# Chapter 3 — The Drowned Page

Creed came up at first light with the returns under his arm, and Ada put the Light out at sunrise and opened the Register on the desk for him. He read at half voice as he wrote: four gallons against fourteen hours burned, five against seventeen, the numbers going down his column like water finding its level. Two valid entries of the *Marigold* stood in the book, Walter Byrd's of eleven years ago and her own of nine. The line that had come in the night counted toward nothing and had been recopied and witnessed at the step. Ada stood with her left cuff black to the elbow and let him count.

At page 91 he stopped mid-sum. He put one finger below the squared, backward-leaning hand with the hour written in full, and asked her where the names came from that he could not match to any return.

"Off the seventh step," Ada said. "The petitions. I copy the name and enter it within the hour, in my hand, at a bearing I choose."

"Every one of them."

"Every one." She offered nothing after it.

Creed laid his own return beside the page and smoothed it flat. The *Marigold* had sailed at ten of the clock on the third of March. Walter's line gave three of the clock that same morning, nineteen hours before. At the turn of the tide Ada read both aloud in the lamp room as the rule required, the hour on the page and the hour on the return, and the sound of them went out into the salt and did not come back with anything. Creed did not offer a reason. Neither did she. They stood in it a moment, and then it was simply read, and standing.

Ada took up the pen and wiped the nib on the inside of her cuff. Nell Harker's petition of the fourteenth lay where she had set it two years' worth of refusal ago, the scrap of net float still knotted through it, and she copied it out at last within the hour of taking it up: *Marigold, ketch, sou'-sou'-west.* The third. Then she turned to the back of the book, to the Drowned Page, and wrote the name there herself, because no other hand might. Creed took the pen from her and countersigned beneath the line. It cost him. She watched it cost him, a vessel he could match to nothing at all, and he wrote his name under her anyway rather than leave it open.

She carried the book down to the head of the stair. Nell stood on the seventh step and did not climb, and Ada read the Drowned Page line down to her, the name and the date and the countersignature, while the chimneys of the drowned street came up out of the ebb below them.

"*Marigold*," Nell said, once, aloud, over the water.

Ada shut the Register. She wiped a clean nib on her cuff, out of habit, on a cuff that would never write that word again.
