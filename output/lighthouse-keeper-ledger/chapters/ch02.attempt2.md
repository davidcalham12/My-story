# Chapter 2 — A Hand Not Mine

At sunset Ada Rowe set the character: two flashes and a dark, every half minute, the clockwork wound and the oil topped and the glass wiped free of salt. She stood the first hours of the watch with nothing passing. Then, wanting the date of the second *Marigold* entry, she took the Register down from its shelf and turned back through nine years of her own lines to page 91.

Beneath Walter Byrd's entry there was a new line on the page, and by the rule a line in a hand not the keeper's counts toward nothing at all.

*Three of the clock, sou'-sou'-west, Marigold, ketch.* Squared, backward-leaning, the hour written out in full where she would have written 3.00. The ink was iron-gall and it was dry. The page had been clear beneath that entry when she closed the book at the turn of the tide; she was certain of it the way she was certain of the stair under her feet.

She did not touch it. No entry may be struck out, and a line in a hand not the keeper's voids the whole page. What the rule required was not judgement but labour: recopy the page entire, the offending line with the rest, carry the copy down to the seventh step, have it signed there by two Ridge witnesses before the next high water. She set the book open, weighted the leaf, and went to fetch a clean sheet.

Behind her the lamp lost its character.

She counted. The dark held past a full minute. Ada went out to the Causeway Bell and rang it, without pause, her arm going and going while the weather came off the water, until the clockwork caught and the two flashes and the dark came back over the drowned street.

Somebody was on the stair. Nell Harker came up out of the dark to the seventh step and no further, her shawl black with spray.

"The *Marigold* stands written on page 91," Ada called down. "In a hand that is not mine."

"If it's written," Nell said. And then, into the wind: "The *Marigold*."

Eleven years, and the name gone out of her at last.

Ada went up and wrote. She wiped the nib on the inside of her cuff before every line and copied page 91 entire, every vessel, every bearing, and then the line she had not made — *three of the clock, sou'-sou'-west, Marigold, ketch* — in her own upright hand, the hour in words because the copy must be faithful.

Before high water she carried the fair copy down to the seventh step and no further. Nell Harker signed, and another from the Ridge signed beneath her, the stone wet under the paper.

Ada held the sheet flat against the spray. The *Marigold* stood in her own handwriting, as it had these nine years, but for the first time in a line she had not composed.
