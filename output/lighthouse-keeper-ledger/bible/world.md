The Corbie Light stands in Sallowmere Reach, above the drowned street. Its causeway is a road four hours in every twelve. Passing vessels are written into the Register of Passage.

## Factions

- **The Commissioners of the Lower Light** — want every vessel accounted for; they audit the Register against their returns and countersign the Drowned Page.
- **The Ridge Households** — want their drowned named in the Register; they leave petitions on the seventh step and witness recopied pages.

## Instruments

- **The Corbie Light** — occulting: two flashes and a dark, sunset to sunrise.
- **The Register of Passage** — numbered pages, iron-gall ink, one line per vessel.
- **The Causeway Bell** — rung only when the character fails.

## Rules

- Only the keeper on watch may write in the Register, and only within the hour of the sighting. A sighting is any report of a vessel's passing that reaches the keeper, whether seen from the lamp room or carried up as a petition, and the hour is counted from the moment the keeper receives it. A line in any other hand voids the whole page; the page must then be recopied by the keeper, carried down to the seventh step and signed there by two Ridge witnesses before the next high water.
- No entry may be struck out. A correction is written beneath the original, and both lines are read aloud in the lamp room at the next turn of the tide. A voided page is recopied entire, the offending line with the rest.
- The Light must show two flashes and a dark from sunset to sunrise. If it fails for a full minute, the Causeway Bell is rung without pause until the character is restored.
- Nobody from the Ridge may climb past the seventh step of the causeway stair. Petitions are left on that step at low water and are answered only by an entry in the Register; a petition the keeper does not enter goes unanswered and lapses, though nothing forbids the keeper speaking down the stair of what already stands written.
- A vessel entered three times in the keeper's own hand, which the Commissioners cannot match to any return, goes onto the Drowned Page at the back of the Register, and its name may never be entered again. A line voided under the first rule does not count toward the three. The keeper writes the Drowned Page line; the attending Commissioner countersigns beneath it, and that countersignature is the only hand other than the keeper's that the Register admits.

## Texture

Salt, iron-gall ink, lamp oil. The Ridge watches the stair; the keeper watches the water.
