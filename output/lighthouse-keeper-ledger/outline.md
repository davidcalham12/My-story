# Outline — Sallowmere Reach

## Acts

**Act I (Chapter 1) — the situation and its cost.** Low water, 14 March. Ada Rowe lifts the petitions off the seventh step and keeps writing the drowned into a record the Commissioners cannot match, and keeps refusing the one name that would end itself by being written. Thomas Creed gives notice of the spring-tide audit and names the price of a third entry.

**Act II (Chapter 2) — the turn.** Night, 15 March. The uncanny arrives as a discrepancy in the record: a line on page 91 in a hand that has been in the sea for eleven years. The Light fails; the Causeway Bell brings Nell Harker as far up the stair as she is allowed; and the voided page must be recopied and witnessed before high water, which puts the *Marigold* into Ada's own handwriting for the first time.

**Act III (Chapter 3) — the irreversible close.** Spring tide, dawn, 16 March. Creed audits, Ada answers him plainly, two impossible hours are read aloud at the turn of the tide, and the *Marigold* goes to the Drowned Page, where the name is admitted and closed in the same stroke.

## Promises

1. **Who are the ships the Commissioners cannot name?** — planted Chapter 1 (Nell's petition on the step, Ada's line in the book an hour later), paid in full Chapter 3, when Creed asks outright and Ada answers without defence.
2. **Whose hand wrote the *Marigold* on page 91, nineteen hours before she sailed?** — planted Chapter 2, paid Chapter 3 when the two hours are read aloud together. The book names the hand and explicitly refuses to explain it away.
3. **What has Nell Harker been asking for at every low water, and will Ada give it to her?** — planted Chapter 1 (Ada sets the petition aside unwritten), paid Chapter 3 when Ada makes the third entry knowing it closes the name forever.
4. **What does the written line oblige the living to do?** — advanced Chapter 2 (the Light fails and the Bell must be rung without pause; a voided page recopied and carried down to the seventh step to be witnessed), paid Chapter 3 when Creed must countersign a name he does not believe in, and Nell may at last say a word she has held for eleven years.

## Chapters

### Chapter 1 — The Seventh Step

- **Tension**: 4/10 — low and pressing; the ordinary procedure of the place, with one refusal held inside it and a date set for the reckoning.
- **POV / when**: Ada Rowe. 14 March, present — low water, afternoon, the causeway open.
- **Promise advanced**: 1 and 3 (planted); 4 (opened).
- **Beats**:
  1. At low water Ada goes down the causeway stair, collects the petitions from the seventh step, and climbs back past Nell Harker, who stands below the step and does not speak the name written on her own paper.
  2. In the lamp room Ada reads the petitions, enters a boat from one of them into the Register of Passage in her own hand within the hour — hour, bearing, name, rig — wiping the nib on her cuff first, and lays Nell's petition aside unwritten with its scrap of net float still on it.
  3. Thomas Creed comes up the causeway, reads last quarter's unmatched names out at half voice, and gives notice that the spring-tide audit falls in two days.
  4. Creed tells her that a third entry of the *Marigold* puts the name on the Drowned Page and that he will countersign it himself; Ada neither agrees nor denies, and the flood takes the causeway behind him as he goes.
- **Ends on**: Nell's petition flat on the desk under its net float, the name on it still unwritten anywhere in the book, and the tide clock going on louder than the sea.

### Chapter 2 — A Hand Not Mine

- **Tension**: 8/10 — the discrepancy in the record, then the Light failing and the Bell obliging her to call the Ridge to the foot of her own stair.
- **POV / when**: Ada Rowe. 15 March, present — night, between the flood and the small hours.
- **Promise advanced**: 2 (planted); 4 (pressed hard).
- **Beats**:
  1. Ada sets the character at sunset — two flashes and a dark, every half minute — and opens the Register to turn back to page 91 to check the date of the second *Marigold* entry.
  2. Beneath the entry she finds a line she did not write, in a squared backward-leaning hand with the hour written out in full — *three of the clock, sou'-sou'-west, Marigold, ketch* — on a page she is certain she left clear, and she does not touch it, because no entry may be struck out and a line in another hand voids the whole page under the first rule; the rule as amended requires the keeper to recopy the page entire and carry the copy down to the seventh step to be signed by two Ridge witnesses before the next high water.
  3. The lamp loses its character and stays dark past a full minute; Ada goes to the Causeway Bell and rings it without pause until she has the two flashes and the dark back.
  4. Nell Harker comes up out of the dark to the seventh step and no further; Ada calls down that the *Marigold* stands written on page 91 in a hand that is not hers, and Nell says "if it's written" and then says the boat's name aloud for the first time in eleven years.
  5. Before high water Ada recopies page 91 entire in her own hand — Walter Byrd's impossible line included, because no entry may be struck out — and carries the fair copy down to the seventh step and no further, where Nell Harker and a second Ridge witness sign it on the wet stone.
- **Ends on**: the fair copy held down against the spray on the seventh step, the *Marigold* standing for the first time in Ada's own handwriting, in a line she did not compose.

### Chapter 3 — The Drowned Page

- **Tension**: 9/10 — opens in the slack of first light, the audit proceeding as arithmetic, then closes on the one act that cannot be undone.
- **POV / when**: Ada Rowe. 16 March, present — spring tide, dawn, the Light put out at sunrise and the tide on the turn.
- **Promise advanced**: 1, 2, 3 and 4 (all paid).
- **Beats**:
  1. Creed comes up at first light with his returns and begins the audit quietly, counting oil against hours burned aloud, page by page, while Ada stands at the desk with her cuff black to the elbow and the tally of valid *Marigold* entries standing at two — Walter Byrd's of eleven years ago and her own of nine, the voided line of last night counting toward nothing.
  2. He reaches page 91, stops mid-sum at the second hand, and asks Ada outright where the names he cannot match come from; she tells him plainly that she copies them off the petitions left on the seventh step and enters them within the hour, and offers no defence.
  3. Creed lays his own return beside the page: the *Marigold* sailed at ten of the clock on 3 March, nineteen hours after the line that records her passing, and at the turn of the tide the two hours are read aloud together in the lamp room, and neither of them can account for it and neither of them pretends to.
  4. Ada takes up the pen, wipes the nib on her cuff, and enters the *Marigold* a third time in her own hand within the hour — Nell Harker's petition of 14 March, set aside unwritten, copied out at last — then turns to the Drowned Page at the back of the Register and writes the name there herself, because only the keeper may write in it; Creed countersigns beneath her line, the only hand other than the keeper's the Register admits, and it costs him to put his name under a vessel he cannot match to any return.
  5. Ada carries the open book down to the head of the stair and reads the Drowned Page line down to Nell Harker, who has not climbed past the seventh step, and Nell says the name once more, aloud, over the water where the drowned street's chimneys are coming up out of the ebb.
- **Ends on**: the Register shut on a name that is at last admitted and at last finished, and Ada wiping a clean nib on a cuff that will never write that word again.
