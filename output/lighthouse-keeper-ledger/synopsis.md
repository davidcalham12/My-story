Ada Rowe has spent eleven years keeping a book that isn't quite true. As keeper of the Corbie Light, she alone may write in the Register of Passage, and only within the hour of a sighting — but a sighting, by the letter of the rule, includes a petition carried up from the drowned street below, where the Ridge households leave the names of boats no return will ever confirm. So Ada copies the dead onto the page as if they still passed the light, one line at a time, in ink that cannot be struck out. It is not quite forgery. It is not quite mercy, either.

One name stands twice already: the *Marigold*, entered by the keeper before her, an hour that does not fit the tide that drowned him with it. A third entry would close the name forever — and Nell Harker, who has left a petition on the seventh step at every low water for nine years, wants nothing else. But closure means admission, and admission is what Thomas Creed's spring-tide audit is built to punish. Then, the night before the Commissioners arrive to make the book agree with the sea, a new line appears on page 91, written in a hand that drowned eleven years ago. Ada must decide whether to let it stand, knowing what the Register does to a name entered three times — and what it costs the keeper who writes it.

For readers of *The Little Stranger* and *Jonathan Strange & Mr Norrell*.
