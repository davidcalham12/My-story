Harrow Bench is a shelf of good ground above a river, a stone bridge at one end, a half-feral orchard at the other. Forty-odd people live in the houses that were already here. Whatever ended is called the quiet, and nobody explains it, because everyone old enough to explain was there. What is left is a chapel with no priest, a ledger with no money behind it, a well that works, and one argument about what the place is for.

## Factions

- **The Orchard Side** — wants Harrow Bench to feed itself and nothing else: ground planted in slow things, apples and walnut and winter wheat. They hold the seed store, and say a tree planted for a stranger is a tree the valley does not eat from.
- **The Road Side** — wants Harrow Bench to be somewhere people arrive. They keep the bridge road clear and beds aired in the old inn, trade seed for news of other valleys, and say a place that only feeds itself has already agreed to end.

## Means

- **The seed store** — the chapel vestry: shelves of labelled jars, everything the valley can plant next year and nothing spare.
- **The ledger** — a hand-drawn book on the vestry table: names, rows, debts of work. No money in the valley; the ledger is the money.
- **The upper well and its pipe** — the one water that runs without carrying, feeding the flats where anything grown for trade must go.

## Rules

- Nobody plants a row on the flats before their name is entered in the ledger; an unentered row is pulled up by whoever finds it.
- The seed store is opened only after the Sunday meeting, and never by one person alone — two must be in the room, and both sign.
- Anyone who walks in over the bridge is fed at the first house they reach and may sleep three nights; on the fourth morning the meeting decides whether they stay.
- Nobody asks about the quiet of someone who lived through it. Questions go to those born after, who answer with what they were told.
- The road is kept clear only as far as the bridge. Past the bridge nothing is mended, and nobody proposes mending it.

## Texture

It smells of woodsmoke, wet stone and bruised windfall apples. Work is unhurried, and people talk while doing something else with their hands. There is no monument; grief looks like an old habit nobody has broken, a plate set out, a door left unlatched. Things grow back unevenly, and the people watching them are busy with something else.
