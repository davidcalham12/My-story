Ruth Vance walks into Harrow Bench at dusk with a stoppered jar of seed, and the valley's oldest rule takes over before anyone asks her name: she is fed at the first house she reaches, given three nights in an aired bed, and told that on the fourth morning a meeting will decide whether she stays. It is a fair rule, and an old wound. The last stranger who ate at this table left with a jar of Harrow Bench's own seed and never sent it back, which is why Martha Gale, who holds one of the two keys to the seed store, has not signed for anyone since. Tom Leary, born the spring after the quiet ended and the only person in the valley anyone is allowed to ask about it, finds a short unentered row growing where his water runs — against the rule that says an unentered row is pulled up by whoever finds it. He does not pull it up. What grows in that row, what is actually in Ruth's jar, and whether Martha will put her name beside a stranger's again come due on Sunday morning, when the meeting has to decide not who is right, but what this place is for. A small, patient novel about a village, a well, a ledger, and the slow work of deciding who gets to stay.

For readers of *A Gentle Creature* and *Station Eleven*, and viewers of *Local Hero*.
