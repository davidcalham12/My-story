Saturday dawn. Tom Leary clears the upper well pipe, follows the water down and finds a short row on the flats — drilled, firmed, watered from his pipe, entered nowhere in the ledger. The rule says whoever finds an unentered row pulls it up. He leaves it standing. Ruth Vance comes up with an empty can, does not apologise, and names the seed: skirret, something Harrow Bench had and lost, enough for one row. Tom asks Martha Gale why she stopped signing; she tells him about last autumn's walker and the jar that never came back, and that holding the walnut ground is part grief, part spite. He must say both things aloud tomorrow, and works out what to ask for.
