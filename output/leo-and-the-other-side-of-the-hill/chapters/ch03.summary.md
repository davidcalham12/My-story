Mid-July. Iris catches Leo outside the village shop and tells him his face has already decided: he stares at the hill every day, he asks everyone what is there, and he will not go because he is scared. Leo says he is not scared of a hill and hears himself promise to go that afternoon at two. Iris insists on coming — she wants to see which it will be.

They ride out at two on the old bicycle Alice lent him, Iris on her own, Bruno trotting alongside and delighted. The path narrows, the grass grows taller than Leo's head, and he realises he has not brought the torch.

The path stops at a green gate. Beyond it the hill begins. Mr. Pike is standing there. He does not wave and does not say hello. "Off," he says. Then: "Off my land. You. The boy. The dog. The bicycles. Off. Now." He commands; he does not ask. He never threatens and never touches anyone, and he is terrifying anyway. Bruno's ears go flat and he stops at the fence on his own, looking at Leo rather than at the gate.

Leo turns the bicycle around. They ride home in silence. Leo's mouth opens twice and nothing comes out. Where the roads split, Iris stops, looks at him properly, and walks her bicycle home without saying goodbye.

WHAT CHANGED: Leo has been turned back at the gate and has told nobody. He makes no new plan.
WHO KNOWS WHAT: Iris knows he tried and failed, and what she will do with that is not yet known. Alice and Samuel know nothing. Nobody knows about the torch.
STILL OPEN: what is on the other side; whether Iris tells; whether Mr. Pike would really do anything.
