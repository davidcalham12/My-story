# Chapter 3 — The Challenge

Iris caught him coming out of the village shop with a bottle of lemonade and his thinking face on.

"You're going to try it," she said.

"Try what?" Leo said, but he knew.

"The hill. You've finally decided you're going."

"I haven't decided anything."

"Your face decided," Iris said. She was smaller than Leo by half a head, which made the pointing worse. "You stare at it every day. You ask people what's there. And you won't go yourself because you're scared."

Leo's whole face went hot. "I'm not scared of a hill."

"Then go now," Iris said. "Go right now. Go today at two o'clock and prove it."

Leo didn't answer. Bruno had walked over and fallen asleep on his foot.

"You won't," Iris said. "That's the thing. You're so scared you won't even try."

"I will," Leo said. The words came out before he could catch them. "This afternoon. Two o'clock. I'm going."

"I'm coming with you."

"No."

"Yes," Iris said. "I want to see which one it is—you turning back, or you being brave. I want to see."

They cycled out at exactly two o'clock. The blue bicycle was fast. Iris had the red one. Bruno trotted alongside them, tongue out, happy about the whole plan for no reason Leo could work out. The path was smooth and worn from other people's feet. It went past the edge of the village and then it kept going.

"How much further?" Iris called.

They had been cycling for three minutes.

"Almost there," Leo said, which was not true, but it made him feel braver.

The path got narrower. The grass on both sides grew taller and wilder. Leo's stomach felt strange. His legs felt like they belonged to someone else.

Then the path stopped at a gate.

The gate was green. The fence was real. On the other side, the path went on and the hill began. Leo could see the place he wanted to go. It was right there, just past the fence.

A man stood beside the gate.

He was tall the way that filled up space. His shirt was brown and dirty. His face was the colour of old brick. When he saw them, he didn't move or say hello.

"Off," he said.

Just that one word.

"We were just—" Leo tried.

"Off my land," the man said. His voice was flat and certain. "You. The boy. The dog. The bicycles. Off. Now."

Leo understood that this was Mr. Pike the way you understand things in dreams. Just knowing. The farmer. The one who owned.

Bruno had stopped at the fence. He didn't bark or push against it. He just stopped. He could smell what a fence meant. Bruno understood boundaries. Boundaries were real to Bruno.

Leo turned the bicycle around.

"Go on," Mr. Pike said.

They cycled back the way they had come. Leo's hands were shaking on the handlebars. Bruno trotted alongside them without protest, the way he always trotted, like this had been the plan all along. His tongue was still out. He looked happy.

Iris didn't speak. She didn't laugh. She didn't tell him he'd been right about the scared part. She just rode beside him with her face very serious and very still, and when they got back to the village, she got off her bicycle and walked it home without saying goodbye.

Leo put the blue bicycle away in the shed. He walked to the cardboard observatory in the garden. He sat down inside it with Bruno. He didn't tell anyone what had happened. He didn't make a plan to come back. He didn't say anything at all.

Bruno fell asleep against his leg. Leo sat in the dimness under the cardboard walls and waited for supper time.
