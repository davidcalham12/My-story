Early July, a week after the birthday. Hunting in the stuck kitchen drawer for matches to light the observatory's oil lamp, Leo finds an old torch pushed to the back — dented metal casing, scratched lens, red button. The bulb flickers twice and holds a thin yellow glow. The battery is dying and the light is barely brighter than a candle, but it works. He keeps it. He does not tell Alice.

Three days later Bruno grabs the hem of Leo's shorts at the sink and hauls backwards; Leo's knee knocks the light switch and the kitchen goes black. Leo cannot see his own feet and his hand shakes properly. He finds the torch, presses the button, and the thin beam finds his feet. The shaking stops — not at once, not like magic. Then he says aloud, "Captain Crunch. That's what I found. At the beach. That fossil. I found that myself." This is the first time the naming happens, and it works.

Through the first week of July Leo takes the torch to the near slope every afternoon, never past the village edge, always home well before dusk, and tests the beam against the long shadows. Seven times it comes on. Bruno refuses to come on the fourth afternoon and lies down in a hole he dug for no reason, then wakes and leads.

WHAT CHANGED: Leo has the torch, and has done the naming once, in the dark, and it worked. He now half believes he is the one who is supposed to go.
WHO KNOWS WHAT: nobody knows about the torch — not Alice, not Samuel.
STILL OPEN: what is on the other side; how long the torch has.
