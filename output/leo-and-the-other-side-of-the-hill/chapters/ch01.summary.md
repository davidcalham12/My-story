June 23rd. Leo turns ten. He wakes holding Captain Crunch, the spiral fossil he found himself among the rocks at Cadaques one summer ago and named on the spot. Bruno wakes him by standing on his chest about breakfast.

At breakfast Alice and Samuel sing the birthday song badly, and Bruno barks once in the middle as though conducting. Leo asks the question he always asks: what is on the other side of the hill? Alice says more countryside. Samuel says fields, another village, the road that goes to it. Leo asks whether anyone they know has been. Alice stops at the sink, and Leo watches her realise she cannot answer and then stop trying. "No one has bothered," she says. Samuel says the best things are usually close to home.

Leo does not ask again, but he has seen the not-bothering in their faces and understands that the grown-ups stopped wondering long ago.

That afternoon he sits in the cardboard observatory he and Samuel built in the garden two summers ago — soft at the corners now, the roof sagging at the seam — with Bruno asleep against his leg. He sorts his stones on the shelf and looks through the observation slot at the hill.

WHAT CHANGED: nothing outwardly. Leo has decided nothing yet.

WHO KNOWS WHAT: Alice and Samuel know Leo keeps asking, and both think it does not matter. Nobody knows he is afraid of the dark — he admits it only to himself, and tells neither of them, nor Bruno.

STILL OPEN: what is on the other side; whether Leo will go. No torch yet.
