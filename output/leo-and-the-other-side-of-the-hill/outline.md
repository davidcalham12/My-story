## Promises

1. What is on the other side of the hill?
2. Will Leo's torch last long enough?
3. Will the farmer harm a trespasser?
4. Can Leo overcome his fear of the dark?
5. Will Iris become Leo's ally or his betrayer?

## Chapters

### Chapter 1 — The Question
- **POV:** Leo
- **Tension:** 5/10
- **Promise advanced:** "What is on the other side of the hill?" and "Can Leo overcome his fear of the dark?"
- **Beats:**
  1. Leo wakes on his tenth birthday; the cardboard observatory still stands in the garden where he and Samuel built it two summers ago.
  2. At breakfast, Alice and Samuel sing the birthday song, and Leo asks the question he always asks: what is on the other side of the hill?
  3. The grown-ups give him their usual answers—nobody has bothered to look, it doesn't matter, it's just more countryside—and Leo sees in their faces that they stopped wondering long ago.
  4. That afternoon in the garden, Leo climbs into the observatory and studies the hilltop; Bruno curls at his feet, sleeping; Leo admits to himself—but only to himself—that he is afraid of the dark that falls between here and there.

### Chapter 2 — The Torch
- **POV:** Leo
- **Tension:** 4/10
- **Promise advanced:** "Will Leo's torch last long enough?"
- **Beats:**
  1. A week later, searching through a kitchen drawer for matches to light the observatory's oil lamp, Leo finds an old torch with a fading battery buried at the back.
  2. He tests it in the darkening kitchen with the lights off; the beam is dim but it works, and his hand stops shaking once the light is on.
  3. Leo carries the torch to the hilltop every afternoon in early July, studying the slope and testing the beam against the long shadows, growing braver with each day.

### Chapter 3 — The Challenge
- **POV:** Leo
- **Tension:** 7/10
- **Promise advanced:** "Will the farmer harm a trespasser?" and "Will Iris become his ally or his betrayer?"
- **Beats:**
  1. Iris corners Leo by the village shop and challenges him: if he is so curious about the other side, why hasn't he gone there already—because he's afraid, she says, and Leo will not admit it.
  2. Leo tells Iris he will go that afternoon; she insists on coming; they cycle to the base of the hill with Bruno trotting alongside.
  3. The farmer's gate appears at the boundary; Mr. Pike stands there, warns them sharply to get off his land, and tells Leo to take his dog and his bicycle and turn around; he does not ask, he commands.
  4. Leo and Bruno turn back while Iris watches, silent; neither of them says anything on the ride home.

### Chapter 4 — The Rules
- **POV:** Leo
- **Tension:** 6/10
- **Promise advanced:** "Can Leo overcome his fear of the dark?"
- **Beats:**
  1. Alice finds out about the encounter with Mr. Pike and sets firm rules: Leo must stay in the village, must be home before dusk, must not attempt the farmer's land again.
  2. Samuel finds Leo sitting in the observatory and asks him directly what he wants to know; Samuel tells him that brave people are afraid too—they just don't stay afraid of the same thing for long.
  3. Leo borrows Alice's old bicycle, which she gives permission for, though not for where he intends to use it; he hides it in the shed and tells himself this is different from lying.

### Chapter 5 — The Way Around
- **POV:** Leo
- **Tension:** 7/10
- **Promise advanced:** "Will the farmer harm a trespasser?"
- **Beats:**
  1. Leo cycles to the hill on a warm afternoon when Alice thinks he is playing in the village; he leaves Bruno at the house deliberately, telling himself it is safer, though the truth is he wants to move faster.
  2. He finds a path that skirts the farmer's gate by a quarter-mile, following a stream that runs downhill; the fence is lower and the land here is wild, not grazed.
  3. Leo climbs through a gap in the fence—no latch, no gate—and discovers the farmer is nowhere to be seen; he realizes the farmer only guards the one gate, not the boundary itself.
  4. He walks back toward the village as the sun starts to lower, his heart loud in his chest; the farmer does not appear, and Leo is alone on this new side of the line.

### Chapter 6 — The Discovery in the Garden
- **POV:** Leo
- **Tension:** 3/10
- **Promise advanced:** "Can Leo overcome his fear of the dark?"
- **Beats:**
  1. Leo spends an afternoon at home with Bruno, sitting in the grass near the observatory, sorting his stone collection by colour and weight, trying to calm the excitement and fear in his chest.
  2. Bruno digs up a smooth stone Leo has never seen before—darker, heavier than most, with a hole worn clean through it by the stream—and refuses to give it back, tossing it in the grass and pouncing.
  3. Leo chases Bruno, laughing despite everything, and keeps the stone, adding it to his collection and marking the place in the garden where Bruno found it.

### Chapter 7 — The Preparation
- **POV:** Leo
- **Tension:** 6/10
- **Promise advanced:** "Can Leo overcome his fear of the dark?"
- **Beats:**
  1. Three days later, Leo tells Samuel he is going to ride the bicycle to the far edge of the village before dusk and wants to bring the torch in his pocket, just in case.
  2. Samuel asks no questions but walks with Leo to test the bicycle on the lane and check the torch's beam one more time in the fading light; the battery is noticeably dimmer than it was a week ago.
  3. Leo cycles home as the light thins, the torch off, riding by the last of the sun; at the edge of the village he turns it on to light just the path ahead, and the beam is so thin he has to trust his memory of the slope.

### Chapter 8 — The Darkness
- **POV:** Leo
- **Tension:** 8/10
- **Promise advanced:** "Can Leo overcome his fear of the dark?"
- **Beats:**
  1. The next afternoon, Leo cycles to the gap in the fence with the torch in his pocket and Bruno at home; the light is already gold and growing thin, and he knows he will reach the other side in near-darkness.
  2. As he climbs the slope beyond the fence, the sun drops below the horizon and the world goes gray; Leo lights the torch with shaking hands, and its thin beam shows him the path but no more—the fear rises in his throat.
  3. Leo stops in the dimming light, shaking, and speaks aloud to the dark: "I found a stone in the garden that the stream wore a hole through, and I did not know the stream was strong enough to do that"—his voice sounds small but true—and the fear becomes something he can walk through.
  4. He climbs the slope with the torch in front of him, the beam barely reaching the next steps, but it reaches them, and he keeps moving.

### Chapter 9 — The Other Side
- **POV:** Leo
- **Tension:** 9/10
- **Promise advanced:** "What is on the other side of the hill?" and "Will Leo's torch last long enough?"
- **Beats:**
  1. The slope opens into a wide meadow that runs down toward a stream; the stream curves away between stone walls, and Leo realizes it is the same stream that runs near the village, but here it is wild and wide and moving fast.
  2. Near the far edge of the meadow stands an old stone shelter with a roof long gone to moss; as full darkness comes on, Leo lights the torch and pauses at the entrance; he names aloud the roar of the stream—how it thunders here where it is wild and wide, nothing like the quiet water near the village—and then creeps inside; there he finds carved initials and dates on the walls going back more than a hundred years—shepherds, maybe, or travelers who needed to know they were not alone.
  3. Leo sits on the shelter's threshold and watches the dusk deepen, the torch growing dimmer by the minute, and realizes that the world beyond the village is not a mystery to be solved but a real place that was here before he was born and will be here long after—he is just the first of his family to see it.
  4. The torch flickers once, steady again, and Leo knows he must climb back down before it fails entirely; he marks the path with stones in his pocket, one for each turn, so he will remember it.

### Chapter 10 — The Telling
- **POV:** Leo
- **Tension:** 4/10
- **Promise advanced:** "Will Iris become his ally or his betrayer?"
- **Beats:**
  1. Leo cycles home in near-total darkness, the torch beam barely a glow, arriving after nightfall—breaking Alice's rule to be home before dusk—his hands shaking with cold and urgency; he hides the bicycle in the shed.
  2. Samuel is waiting in the kitchen with a cup of warm milk, asks Leo nothing, and listens while Leo tells him about the meadow and the stream and the shepherd's shelter with initials a hundred years old.
  3. The next morning, Leo finds Iris sitting by the village shop; he tells her he went to the other side, and she listens without interrupting, her eyes wide with the hunger for discovery he felt before he went.
  4. Leo walks home with Bruno beside him, the stone with the hole worn through it in his pocket, understanding that the summer changed him and that the world is bigger than he thought, and that the best discoveries are the ones you tell to people who understand why they matter.
