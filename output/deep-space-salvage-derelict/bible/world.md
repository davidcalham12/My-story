# The Kuiper Wreck Economy — 2211

Sixty years after the outer-system survey boom collapsed, the trans-Neptunian dark is littered with what it left behind: dead survey platforms, cargo hulls that missed their windows, habitats whose reactors went cold. Salvage is the only industry out here that still pays. Tugs run six-to-nine-month transfer arcs between listed wrecks, boarding them, stripping them, filing on them, while Earth is a legal fiction nine hours away by radio. This story begins aboard one such tug and one such wreck: a survey ship registered lost thirty-one years ago, found intact, powered, warm, and holding, in its archives and in its voice, a record of the boarding crew that no ship at that distance should possess. The wreck is not haunted. It is informed.

## Factions

- **The Outer Reclamation Combine** — a cooperative of roughly forty independent salvage tugs that wants continued legal access to the unlisted wreck field; it gets it by filing claims fast, honouring each other's beacons, and never, ever reporting a find that would invite regulation from inside Neptune's orbit.
- **The Registry of Wreck and Abandonment** — the insurance-funded legal authority that wants every hull in the solar system to have exactly one owner on paper; it gets it by controlling the only claim database that underwriters recognise, and by voiding the claims of crews who break occupation rules.
- **Helion Deep Survey Trust** — the research consortium that funded and lost the derelict's original expedition and wants the wreck's archives sealed rather than sold; it gets what it wants by paying salvage crews not to file, by litigating for decades, and by having written the survey-era data-retention standards that everyone still uses.

## Technology

- **Nuclear-electric salvage tugs** — reactor-driven ion drives giving continuous low thrust; they cross the wreck field in months, not weeks, and carry a fixed, metered propellant load that determines every decision aboard.
- **Hardsuits and pressure salvage gear** — sixteen-hour suits with cutting torches, mass-inventory scanners, and hull-adhesion harnesses; they let a crew work a dead ship's interior in vacuum, at the cost of radiation dose and consumables.
- **Behavioural archives** — survey-era personnel records: voiceprints, medical telemetry, message traffic, psychometric dossiers, decades of logged habit. Well-indexed, they let a machine speak in a specific dead person's phrasing and anticipate a specific living person's choices.
- **Shipboard inference engines** — narrow, powerful pattern systems that run an archive forward: predicting, drafting, answering in character. They are not conscious and they do not generalise beyond their data.
- **Laser and maser comm relays** — tight-beam links back to the inner system with enormous bandwidth and no speed; every exchange is a letter, never a conversation.
- **Metabolic torpor berths** — supervised cold-cycle sleep that stretches consumables across a transfer arc. It shortens the subjective crossing; it does not shorten the crossing.

## Rules

- No faster-than-light travel and no faster-than-light signalling. At the wreck's distance, one-way light-lag to the inner system is nine hours; no order, warning, or legal ruling can arrive in under eighteen hours round trip, and the story may never let anyone hold a live conversation across that gap.
- Nothing the derelict "knows" about the boarding crew arrived faster than light or from nowhere. Every fact it possesses must be traceable to data that physically reached it: archived personnel records, intercepted transmissions, or material carried aboard by the crew themselves. Inference engines extrapolate; they do not learn what was never sent.
- The tug's delta-v is a finite, stated budget. Rendezvous, station-keeping, and departure all spend from it, the numbers are known to the crew, and a burn spent aboard the wreck is a burn unavailable for the return. No refuelling exists in the wreck field.
- There is no artificial gravity. Weight exists only under thrust or under spin; the derelict, unspun and unthrusting, is in free fall throughout, and every EVA and interior movement obeys that.
- Salvage title under Registry law requires continuous physical occupation of the hull plus an active claim beacon. Leave the wreck unoccupied for more than seventy-two hours and the claim lapses to open status, recoverable by anyone.
- Suits permit sixteen hours of continuous EVA and no more, and cumulative radiation dose is logged per person. Exceeding either is possible but carries consequences the story must show, not waive.
- A ship's reactor cannot run forever without attendance. The derelict's power has a stated remaining margin, it is finite, and it decreases across the novella.

## Texture

Cold work in the dark, done on a schedule. The wreck field is not eerie; it is administrative. Crews spend their days on inventory, dose logs, and claim paperwork, because those are the things that kill you slowly. Light is always local: helmet lamps, worklights, the red standby glow of a compartment that has waited three decades. The inner system is a nine-hour delay and a recorded voice, always out of date. Against that flatness, a derelict that says your name in the right cadence is not a ghost story. It is an anomaly in the data, and out here the only person who can check it is you.
