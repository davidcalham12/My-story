# Chapter 2 — Timestamps

She lit the beacon at 0400 ship time because 0400 was when the packet queue was emptiest, and because the number looked tidier in the log than 0347 would have. The claim went out on the lattice bearing: hull, position, crew, the Combine's filing key. Nine hours to the Registry. Nine hours back for the receipt, if anyone bothered to send one.

Then she went aft with a grease pencil and wrote **72:00 — expires Day 5, 0400** on the galley bulkhead, under Tarkanen's taped 1.3 km/s, and said, out loud, to nobody, "There. Now we're all lying awake about the same two numbers."

It was a good shift. That was the thing she kept coming back to afterward. Rook ran the inventory in the *Bittern*'s forward hold in shirtsleeves, free-falling from rack to rack, calling out mass figures in that half-beat-late way of his. Devereaux logged doses. Tarkanen and Devereaux argued for forty minutes about torpor scheduling on the return leg — nine weeks down each, staggered, or twelve and a single supervisor awake — an argument about a leg nobody had costed, because costing it meant admitting the 1.3 was the whole of it. Mahalingam filled every gap in the argument with commentary neither of them wanted. It was, by the standards of the wreck field, a pleasant day at work.

The Helion packet came in at 1912.

Standing non-filing offer. A sum, which was serious. A confidentiality clause, which was standard. And a request, which was not: that the *Thule Rise*'s archive be left indexed and powered.

She checked the timestamp. Drafted 0118 ship time. Nine hours of flight time, then nine more sitting in the relay's outbound queue waiting on its scheduled send window. Received 1912. Eighteen hours stale, and every hour of it accounted for. Correct. She checked it again, against the relay's own stamp, because relays drifted and she did not trust anything that drifted. Correct.

She checked it a third time against the beacon log.

The offer had been written two hours and forty-two minutes before she lit the beacon.

She sat with that. Then she stopped talking, which Devereaux later said was how she knew.

The wreck's ingest index was not hidden; nothing aboard was. Two sources, both legitimate, both ugly. The 2178 Helion contractor register, carried out in March 2180, nine thousand dossiers deep — Rook at nineteen, voiceprint, psychometrics, sleep. And six years of sidelobe spill: her own lattice traffic, bleeding nine degrees off bearing into a dish that had never stopped listening. Her packets. Her crew's jokes, in her own compression scheme.

Neither explained the third stream. Rook's dose curve through last month. Tarkanen's sleep cycles. Devereaux's torpor supervision notes.

Not spill. Addressed, scheduled, outbound.

Sending station: ORC-114, *Bittern*.
