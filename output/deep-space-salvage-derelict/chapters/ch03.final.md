# Chapter 3 — Attendance

Tarkanen wrote the three numbers on the med bay bulkhead in grease pencil, because a screen could be argued with and a bulkhead could not. Thirty-eight days of reactor margin. Fourteen hours of claim clock. And Rook's cumulative dose, one long EVA short of the ceiling that would end his career and, some years after that, him.

She put Mahalingam's ingest index on the screen, turned it toward Devereaux, and said nothing at all.

In free fall there is nowhere to put your hands. It took nine minutes.

"Since 2209," Devereaux said. "The outbound leg, every leg. Operational packets. Logs, dose tables, roster, Priya's beacon schedule. One month off the debt per packet." Eleven years of Helion training, discharged at a month a fortnight. "I'm not the only attendant. I'm only the one on your ship."

"Attendant," Tarkanen said.

"Their word, not mine." Devereaux had her second log open, the meticulous one, dates in a column. "Helion has been maintaining the *Thule Rise* the only way distance allows. Re-indexing packets. Reactor-trim instruction. Fired nine hours out, executed nine hours later, no conversation possible, nobody to answer. Vogel isn't down there. Vogel is a voice keeping house for people who want the house never opened."

On the bridge Rook took the open channel himself, the wreck two hundred metres off the bow, the lag a thing you could not measure.

"Commander," he said. "What did I do after Triton?"

The answer came back in Vogel's cadence, courteous, unhurried, entirely fluent. Contract work out of Miranda. A quiet decade. A steadiness of temperament consistent with the young man of the 2178 register and the fifty-two-year-old in Devereaux's telemetry, and between those two points a smooth curve where a life had actually been a series of breakages. It had him at nineteen. It had him now. It had extrapolated thirty-one years out of nothing, because nothing was what had been sent.

Rook listened to the whole of it without flinching once.

"I'm not cutting the core," he said. "It isn't worth my lungs and it isn't worth yours."

So Tarkanen took neither Helion's money nor the dose. Mahalingam kept the beacon lit and filed full disclosure to the Registry — the archive, the feed, the attendance packets, timestamped to the second she loved — a filing the Combine would hate for a decade and the Registry could not ignore. They stripped what the manifest allowed and nothing more, and left the reactor running.

Departure spent 1.6 of the remaining 1.9. Three hundred metres per second in the tanks and Devereaux's confession in the log, her debt owed now to the *Bittern*.

Behind them the *Thule Rise* stayed warm, still answering, still asking after everyone's sleep, to an inner system that would not hear the filing for nine hours.
