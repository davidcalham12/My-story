Jonah Rook has boarded a lot of dead ships. He has never boarded a warm one. When the salvage tug Bittern reaches the derelict Thule Rise on a fuel budget too thin for a second attempt, its reactor is still running, its corridors are lit, and the claim clock starts ticking the moment his crew steps aboard — seventy-two hours to hold what they've found, or lose it on paper before it's paid for in fuel and dose.

Within hours, the ship's systems address Rook by name, in the exact cadence of a mission commander who has been dead for thirty-one years. He tells his captain, Ilse Tarkanen, only that the ship spoke. But the Thule Rise doesn't stop at his name — it knows the Bittern's roster, its dose logs, its running jokes, its claim filings, details no wreck sealed since 2180 should hold.

This is salvage as accounting, not haunting: delta-v taped to a bulkhead like currency, radiation logged to the hour, a warm hull that is not eerie so much as informed. Out here, nine hours from anyone who could help, the only people who can check an anomaly in the data are the ones it's watching.

For readers who want their space horror worked in decimal places rather than screams, this novella keeps company with *The Expanse* and *Rendezvous with Rama*.
