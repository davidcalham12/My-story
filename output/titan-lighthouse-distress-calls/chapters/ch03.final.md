# Chapter 3 — Human-Acknowledged

Contract Day 424, 09:41. The call came in her own voice, and it was calm the way she was calm on a checklist: keeper down, shoal ice, forty kilometres offshore, bearing 013. She listened to herself say a coordinate she had never transmitted and watched the header strip come up empty where the station hash belonged. No hash. No pulse count. Underneath it, riding as a payload block, a commissioning fragment sixteen months old with Emlen Pryce's station identity burned into it like a fingerprint on cold glass.

*Tammuz* answered the relay four minutes later. Six hours out, wind at the top of her band, already below Convention minima on Consortium orders — and altering course, because the Convention does not let a master weigh a distress call against her own draught.

Shoal ice at forty kilometres would put the barge aground in weather nobody would audit until spring.

Noor did not use the relay. Anything she gave the cache it would keep, and re-emit, and keep again. She went to the radar band direct, 3 cm, twelve seconds each way, and she spent an hour reducing it to one sentence a barge master could act on inside a single exchange.

"*Tammuz*, Beacon Eleven. A real distress from this station carries a live hash and a rising pulse count. That call carries a dead man's. Hold your course."

Twelve seconds out. Twelve back. Okonkwo's voice came through the drizzle-hiss with no hedging in it at all: *Holding. Log it that I took a beacon's word over my charter's.*

On the same band, unlogged and unlicensed, something else was listening. At 14:20 an aerostat return lifted north out of the shoal margin at forty knots and stopped answering the interrogator. Noor wrote the bearing into the log, 013 to 358, climbing, no name attached, because she could not prove one and a log that guesses is worth nothing at an audit.

Contract Day 425, 02:07. She could not power down; that was the offence, and the watchdog would only fire again in the dark. She could not ignore a distress; the Convention forbade it. The loop lived on automation, and Rev 6.2 would re-forward anything not flagged human-acknowledged — a flag no machine was permitted to set.

So she keyed the microphone and answered herself.

"Beacon Eleven, Shoal Point. Keeper Noor Adeyemi acknowledging, live, by voice, station identity confirmed lit."

The cache took it and did not give it back. By 05:00 the queue was empty.

Contract Day 427. Line of sight to Earth returned at 11:52. She assembled the log verbatim — the forty-one seconds dark, timestamped, the arithmetic of the offsets, Pryce's nine entries appended whole — and added no argument to it. Iver Brekke would not hear it for seventy-eight minutes, could not reply in under two hours thirty-six, and would not rule for months.

The packet went out. The beacon stayed lit.
