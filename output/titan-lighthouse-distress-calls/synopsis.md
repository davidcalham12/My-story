# The Titan Beacon Chain

On Titan's Shoal Point, keeper Noor Adeyemi has fourteen months left on an eighteen-month contract that requires her to acknowledge, log, and never silence a single distress call — even the one now arriving in her own voice, reporting a position she never gave. The station's clear-sky relay window opens once every sixteen days; the rest of the time, everything on Kraken Mare's sea-lanes routes through cache satellites nineteen years frozen and unable to tell a live signal from a replayed one. The calls keep coming, each stamped further ahead of station time than the last, and when Noor does the arithmetic on the drift, it points backward — to a forty-one-second gap in her own power log, and further still, to the final entries of Emlen Pryce, the predecessor who died at this beacon and was convicted, after the fact and on evidence months late, of abandoning it. Bound by a Convention that treats every acknowledged distress as gospel and forbids a keeper from ever going dark, Noor cannot simply refuse the call. She has twelve seconds each way to prove what she suspects before a barge under way in weather minima changes course for coordinates that may be nothing but her own voice, echoing forward through machinery that was never built to know the difference between rescue and repetition. A hard-science procedural about instruments, law, and the last data you can trust.
