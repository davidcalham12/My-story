# Outline — The Titan Beacon Chain

## Act Structure

**Act I (Chapter 1) — The receipt arrives.** Beacon Eleven, blind half, oxidiser at 6%. A distress call in Noor Adeyemi's own voice comes down off the relay stamped 1:18 in her future. She logs it verbatim because the Convention allows nothing else.

**Act II (Chapter 2) — The arithmetic and the dead man.** The call returns twice more at 2:36 and 3:54. Noor divides by 78 minutes, finds 41 seconds of dark radar in her own log, and finds Emlen Pryce's last nine entries saying the cache keeps things.

**Act III (Chapter 3) — The splice and the microphone.** A keeper-down call with no station hash puts *Tammuz* on a course into shoal ice. Noor broadcasts the discriminator direct on radar band, then answers her own distress live to set the flag Rev 6.2 will not re-forward. Packet away, beacon lit.

## Promises

- **Why is Noor Adeyemi receiving distress calls in her own voice?** — she recorded it herself at commissioning and a dead-man watchdog used it exactly as designed.
- **Why do the calls arrive stamped in her future — 1:18, then 2:36, then 3:54?** — the offset is a cycle counter: 78 minutes of Earth-lag correction reapplied per pass.
- **What actually killed Emlen Pryce, and was the abandonment verdict wrong?** — the same loop, with nobody to do the arithmetic; he died of his own compliance 1.2 km from a lit beacon.
- **Who is putting Noor's voice on coordinates she never transmitted?** — Sorrel Vane, splicing a bought archive of keeper voice banks to strand a barge into salvage.
- **How do you stop a loop the law forbids you to interrupt?** — obey the law completely: acknowledge the distress live, by voice, and set the human flag no automation may set.

## Chapters

### Chapter 1 — Forty-One Seconds

- **POV:** Noor Adeyemi, inside Beacon Eleven's log room at Shoal Point, Contract Day 422, blind half, oxidiser tally at 6%
- **Tension:** 5/10
- **Promise advanced:** Why is Noor receiving distress calls in her own voice
- **Beats:**
  - Noor wakes at 15:20 into the tail of the predicted five-hour magnetopause blackout, resets the forty-minute alternation between radar and habitat heaters by hand, and writes the oxidiser figure on the sheet under Emlen Pryce's handwriting, which is still the line above hers.
  - At 15:40 the first clear relay pass drops an inbound distress into the station. It carries Beacon Eleven's station hash. It is spoken in her own voice, flat, phrase-bank cadence, giving a position report she has never made.
  - She checks the clock twice because she trusts the clock more than herself: the packet is stamped 1 hour 18 minutes ahead of station time. The station has already auto-acknowledged and forwarded it before she reaches the console.
  - She logs it verbatim as the Convention requires, adds no interpretation, and notes that she cannot power down the beacon to think about it. Outside, methane drizzle on the roof; the heat exchanger is the loudest thing in the room.
- **Canon established:** Beacon Eleven's layout, the 600 W(e) cap and the radar/heater alternation; the 6% oxidiser stock; the Convention duty to log, acknowledge and forward verbatim; the blind half means everything routes through the Relay Trust cache; the voice is unmistakably Noor's own.

### Chapter 2 — Divide by Seventy-Eight

- **POV:** Noor Adeyemi, log room and archive locker, Contract Day 423
- **Tension:** 4/10
- **Promise advanced:** Why the calls arrive stamped in her future; what killed Emlen Pryce
- **Beats:**
  - The same call returns at 2:36 ahead, then again at 3:54 ahead — identical audio, identical hash, only the stamp moved. Noor lays the three offsets in a column and sees 78 minutes between them, the season's one-way lag to Earth, added once per handling.
  - She divides, counts back three passes, and lands on 04:12 Contract Day 422. In her own power log she finds it: the alternation slipped and the radar drew nothing for 41 seconds. The call from the future is a receipt for a gap she slept through.
  - She sends a query up to Jun Halloran at the Relay Trust and gets back a cached packet a pass and a half old saying only that cache behaviour is proprietary and that Rev 6.2 has operated to specification for nineteen years. It reads like a lie and is merely a muzzle.
  - In the archive locker she pulls Pryce's final nine entries. He logged the same climbing offsets, wrote that the cache "keeps things," and then logged a keeper-down report at coordinates on the ice apron — and acknowledged it, and suited up. Nine days later somebody else closed his log. Iver Brekke's abandonment verdict sits in the same folder.
- **Canon established:** the 78-minute correction is reapplied per pass and the offset is a cycle counter; the 41-second radar outage at 04:12 on Day 422; the dead-man watchdog speaks in the keeper's commissioned 312-phrase voice inventory; Pryce walked out to a corrected timestamp and died 1.2 km from a beacon that never went dark; Brekke's verdict is right on evidence and wrong in finding.

### Chapter 3 — Human-Acknowledged

- **POV:** Noor Adeyemi, log room and radar console, Contract Day 424, closing on Contract Days 425 and 427
- **Tension:** 9/10
- **Promise advanced:** Who is putting Noor's voice on coordinates she never transmitted; how do you stop a loop the law forbids you to interrupt
- **Beats:**
  - A new call arrives in Noor's voice: keeper down, shoal ice, 40 km offshore. It carries no live station hash and no pulse count — only a replayed block from Pryce's dead commissioning record. Marta Okonkwo's *Tammuz*, six hours out and already below weather minima, signals that she is altering course to it under Convention obligation.
  - Noor works the discriminator into one sentence — genuine beacon calls carry a live hash and a rising pulse count, splices carry a corpse's — and puts it on the radar band direct to *Tammuz*, twelve seconds each way, nothing routed through the relay for the cache to hold and re-emit. Okonkwo takes a beacon's word over her charter's and holds her course.
  - On the same band, unlogged and unlicensed, Sorrel Vane is listening; *Second Thought* lifts north and stops answering. Noor writes the aerostat's bearing into the log without a name attached to it, because she cannot prove one.
  - She cannot power down — that is the offence, and it would only refire the watchdog. She cannot ignore the call — the Convention forbids it. So she takes the microphone and acknowledges her own distress herself, live, by voice, with her station identity, setting the human-acknowledged flag Rev 6.2 will not re-forward. The loop stops. (Timeline: Contract Day 425.)
  - On Contract Day 427, line of sight to Earth returns. She assembles the verbatim log for Iver Brekke: the 41 seconds dark, timestamped, the mechanism attached, Pryce's file appended, no argument added. The packet goes out and the beacon stays lit, and no answer can exist for 2 hours 36 minutes and no verdict for months.
- **Canon established:** the splice tell is structural (live hash and pulse count) rather than acoustic; the Drift's bought archive contains keeper voice banks; direct radar-band traffic bypasses the cache entirely; the human-acknowledged flag is the only thing Rev 6.2 will not re-forward and no automation may set it; the novella closes with the packet away, Noor's choice known and Brekke's decision not.
