# Timeline: The Kraken Mare Light

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| 2131 | Marit Anselm registers her voiceprint with the Mare Authority and signs custody for survey pod Wick-3; by Authority rule the model binds to every beacon she has signed for, permanently and non-transferably. |
| 2138, winter transit | Anselm, then survey lead out of Station 7, sends Halvard Ness in Wick-3 to re-anchor buoys in Nicholson Shoal. A ballast tank seal fails; Wick-3 sinks in roughly 180 m of methane-ethane and is preserved intact at 94 K. |
| 2138, same hour | Ness composes a distress text as the pod goes under. The beacon synthesises it in Anselm's registered voice and Buoy 47 takes custody — but Buoy 47's uplink horn is tholin-fouled, so it can accept bundles and never hand one off. It re-transmits into an empty arc for nine years. |
| 2138, +72 hours | Authority search finds no signal and no wreck. Salvage cooperatives never hear the call, because nothing ever leaves Buoy 47. Wick-3 is logged missing, not distressed. |
| 2139 | Inquest signed by Corvin Ladd rules that Wick-3's beacon failed to transmit. Anselm, who certified that beacon, is censured and accepts the posting to Station 12. Buoy 47 is written off as a dead node and left where it floats. |
| 2146 | Automation retires Station 11. Station 12 becomes the last light on Kraken Mare with a human in it, kept only by the charter clause requiring a human custodian for the distress network. |
| 2147, day −30 | Ladd opens the decommissioning audit of Station 12, arguing the buoy chain can now carry custody unaided. Six usable Huygens passes a day; Delphine Sarr begins metering Anselm's evidence by the kilobit. |
| 2147, day −6 | Oyuna Brecht runs the *Sorrel Draught* through Nicholson Shoal on a falsely filed weather bundle. Her anchor fouls derelict Buoy 47 and drags it about 14 km east — inside the 17 km line of sight of Station 12's 60 m mast. |
| 2147, day 1 | Buoy 47 finally finds a listener and begins discharging nine years of custody. The first distress bundle reaches Station 12 in Anselm's own voice, custody-stamped 2138. She logs it as a duplicate fault and cannot make the timestamps lie. (Chapter 1) |
| 2147, day 2 | A further bundle arrives stamped this week: Wick-3's radioisotope-fed beacon is still calling every forty minutes from the seafloor. Tomas Weir's cooperative logs the same open-band call and the seventy-two-hour salvage clock starts. (Chapter 2) |
| 2147, day 3 | Anselm buys uplink seconds from Sarr and burns nine minutes a pass to file custodial acknowledgement and the full 2138 custody chain, browning out the habitat heaters to do it. Ladd receives a file he signed shut nine years ago. (Chapter 2–3) |
| 2147, day 4 | Anselm triangulates Wick-3 from Buoy 47's custody stamps and Brecht's real track, and trades the coordinates to Weir for a lift: the cooperative raises the pod and Ness's body. The proven nine-year custody hole keeps Station 12 crewed, and keeps Anselm in it. (Chapter 3) |
