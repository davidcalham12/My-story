# Mysteries: The Kraken Mare Light

- **Why is Marit Anselm receiving distress calls spoken in her own voice?** (CENTRAL — must resolve by the end of Chapter 3.) *Answer (canon):* she signed custody for survey pod Wick-3 in 2131, so that pod's beacon carries her registered voice model forever; the words are Halvard Ness's, composed by him as Wick-3 sank in 2138 and synthesised in her voice by the beacon. The voice is hers, the distress never was. *Seeded:* Chapter 1, first bundle, when she recognises her own vowels and her own throat-clearing tic. *Paid off:* Chapter 2 in mechanism (she pulls the beacon-registration record and finds her own signature from 2131), Chapter 3 in full when the raised pod gives up its author.

- **Why has a nine-year-old call arrived only now?** *Answer (canon):* Buoy 47's uplink horn was fouled with tholin sludge in 2138; it could take custody but never hand off, so it re-transmitted the bundle into empty sea for nine years, unheard and outside every line of sight. Six days before Chapter 1, Oyuna Brecht's anchor fouled the derelict buoy during an illegal shoal shortcut and dragged it about 14 km east, into the 17 km horizon of Station 12's mast. The network did not fail on day one — it failed for nine years and then was repaired by accident, by a crime. *Seeded:* Chapter 1, in the mismatch between Brecht's filed weather and the Light's own radar track. *Paid off:* Chapter 2, when Anselm reads the custody stamps backwards to a hop that never happened and puts the buoy's drift against the *Sorrel Draught*'s real course.

- **Who is inside Wick-3, and can anything still be alive in it?** *Answer (canon):* Halvard Ness, dead in under ninety seconds when the seal went at 94 K, his body and the pod preserved unchanged on the floor of Nicholson Shoal at roughly 180 m. The bundles stamped this week are genuine new emissions from a radioisotope-fed beacon still calling every forty minutes into 500 kg/m³ of methane — a machine keeping a promise, not a survivor. Nothing Anselm can do in a six-hour hardsuit could have reached him in 2138 or reaches him now. *Seeded:* Chapter 1 end, with a bundle whose custody stamps are only days old. *Paid off:* Chapter 2 end (she proves the beacon's power source, not a person, explains the fresh stamps) and Chapter 3 (Tomas Weir's cooperative raises the pod).

- **Why did the 2139 inquest rule that the beacon never transmitted?** *Answer (canon):* no one read Buoy 47's custody log, because a buoy that has never handed off a bundle reports as a dead node, and dead nodes were excluded from the audit set that the automation schedule depended on. Corvin Ladd signed the finding because a transmitting beacon meant a chain that swallows distress traffic, and that finding would have frozen eighteen station retirements. Not a conspiracy — an incentive, and a default in a query filter. *Seeded:* Chapter 2, when Anselm requests the 2139 evidence list through Delphine Sarr's metered pass and sees which node classes were queried. *Paid off:* Chapter 3, when Ladd answers across a 79-to-89-minute Earth-link lag he cannot shorten and does not deny it.

- **Will Station 12 be decommissioned, and does Anselm want to stay?** *Answer (canon):* it stays crewed. Anselm's custodial acknowledgement — bought at nine minutes of uplink per pass and a browned-out habitat — becomes the Authority's own proof that the buoy chain can hold a distress bundle for nine years without a human to notice, which is precisely the insurability argument the charter clause exists to protect. Ladd's audit is reversed by the evidence Ladd's audit provoked. Anselm stays, which is both her vindication and her sentence: she keeps the lamp because the lamp was proved necessary by the one call she failed to hear. *Seeded:* Chapter 1, with the audit notice already on the board. *Paid off:* Chapter 3, final beat.
