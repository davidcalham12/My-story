# Outline: The Kraken Mare Light

## Acts

- **Act I (Chapter 1) — The call that cannot exist.** A nine-year-old distress bundle reaches the last crewed light on Kraken Mare, spoken in the custodian's own voice. The timestamps refuse to be a fault.
- **Act II (Chapter 2) — The arithmetic of a dead node.** Not a hunt but an audit: registration records, custody stamps read backwards, a metered uplink, and the cold finding that the fresh calls are a machine, not a survivor.
- **Act III (Chapter 3) — Nine minutes a pass.** Anselm spends her heaters to file the truth, trades coordinates for a lift, and the evidence that clears her is the same evidence that keeps her on the rock.

## Promises

1. **Whose voice is on the distress call, and why is it hers?** Marit Anselm hears her own vowels and her own throat-clearing tic in a message she never composed. The book owes an explanation that is mechanical, not supernatural.
2. **Why did a nine-year-old message arrive today?** The network did not break on day one. Something moved, recently, and the reader is promised the specific physical act that moved it.
3. **Is anyone still alive inside Wick-3?** Some bundles are stamped this week. The book owes a definite answer about what is on the floor of Nicholson Shoal and whether it can be reached.
4. **Who decided the beacon never transmitted, and why did that finding hold?** A 2139 inquest closed this. The reader is promised the mechanism of the error and the face attached to the signature.
5. **Does Station 12 survive the audit, and does Anselm want it to?** A decommissioning notice is already on the board. The reader is promised a resolution that costs her something either way.

## Chapters

### Chapter 1 — Custody Stamp 2138

- **POV:** Marit Anselm
- **Location:** Station 12 habitat and radar gallery, Seldon Peninsula, Kraken Mare, Titan — 2147, day 1. She is the sole human custodian of the last crewed light of nineteen; the other eighteen are automated. 94 K outside, 1.5-bar nitrogen sky, methane drizzle, Saturn motionless above the same patch of horizon.
- **Tension:** 7/10
- **Promise advanced:** Promises 1, 2, 3 and 5 — opens all four; resolves none.
- **Beats:**
  - Routine maintenance: she scrapes tholin sludge off the radar dome feed and logs acknowledgements nobody reads, with Corvin Ladd's decommissioning audit notice (opened thirty days ago, arguing the buoy chain can carry custody unaided) sitting unanswered on the board behind her.
  - The short-range buoy link delivers a distress bundle. The voiceprint beacon synthesises it in her registered voice — her vowels, her throat-clearing tic before a hard consonant — but the words are not hers and never were: a plainspoken report of a failing ballast seal, composed by someone who is going under.
  - She files it as a duplicate fault, then opens the custody chain and cannot make it lie: bundles carry the signed timestamps of every node that held them, and this one was composed in 2138 and has been in the custody of Buoy 47 ever since — a node the Authority wrote off as dead and left where it floats.
  - Working the problem sideways, she pulls her own radar track for the past week against the Kraken Freight Guild's filed weather and finds the *Sorrel Draught*, barge master Oyuna Brecht, six days ago on a course through Nicholson Shoal that the filed weather does not justify. She notes the discrepancy without yet knowing it is the answer.
  - Closing beat: a second bundle arrives in the same voice, and its custody stamps are only days old.
- **Leaves open:** everything. The reader must exit believing the beacon might still have a living person behind it. Do not explain the voice, the buoy, or Brecht's course; do not name Halvard Ness.

### Chapter 2 — Dead Node, Live Signature

- **POV:** Marit Anselm
- **Location:** Station 12 habitat, at the console, over roughly thirty hours spanning 2147 days 2–3. A single sustained scene of forensic work. Carry forward from the previous chapter: yesterday a distress bundle arrived in her own voice, composed 2138, held all nine years by derelict Buoy 47; a second bundle is stamped this week.
- **Tension:** 5/10 — deliberately the book's trough. This is cold procedure, not crisis: the terror is arithmetic, and the chapter's worst moment is a subtraction.
- **Promise advanced:** Promises 1, 2, 3 and 4 — answers 1 mechanically, answers 2 fully, closes 3's false hope, opens 4's machinery.
- **Beats:**
  - She pulls the beacon-registration record and finds her own signature from 2131: she registered her voiceprint and signed custody for survey pod Wick-3, and by Authority rule the model binds to every beacon she has signed for, permanently and non-transferably. The voice is hers. The distress never was. The registered wearer that winter was Halvard Ness, survey technician, lost 2138 on a buoy re-anchoring run she ordered.
  - She reads the custody stamps backwards hop by hop and finds a handoff that never happened: Buoy 47 took custody in 2138 and never passed it on — its uplink horn tholin-fouled, so it could accept bundles and only re-transmit them into an empty arc, out of every line of sight, for nine years. Then she overlays the buoy's drift on the *Sorrel Draught*'s real track and sees Brecht's anchor foul it and drag it roughly 14 km east, inside the 17 km horizon of her own 60-metre mast. The network was repaired by accident, by a crime.
  - She buys uplink seconds from Delphine Sarr of the Huygens Ring Consortium, who meters by the kilobit and is transactional to the second, and spends them requesting the 2139 inquest evidence list. What comes back is a query filter: dead node classes excluded from the audit set. Nobody read Buoy 47's log because a buoy that has never handed off a bundle reports as dead.
  - On the open distress band she hears Tomas Weir's Mayda Insula cooperative log the same unanswered call. Titan salvage custom gives any beacon seventy-two hours before it is abandoned property. The clock is running and she is not the only listener.
  - Closing beat, the cold subtraction: she traces the fresh stamps to their source and proves them genuine new emissions from Wick-3's radioisotope-fed beacon, still calling every forty minutes from roughly 180 m down in 500 kg/m³ of methane-ethane. A seal at 94 K kills by conduction in under ninety seconds. Ness died in 2138. The machine has simply kept its promise for nine years.
- **Leaves open:** whether she can prove any of this to anyone who matters, and whether Corvin Ladd — who signed the 2139 finding — will answer. Closes the question of survivors: no one is alive down there, and the chapter must say so plainly.

### Chapter 3 — Nine Minutes a Pass

- **POV:** Marit Anselm
- **Location:** Station 12 — console, then the radar gallery with the high-gain uplink running, 2147 days 3–4. Carry forward: she now knows the caller is Halvard Ness, dead since 2138 in pod Wick-3 on the floor of Nicholson Shoal; that her own 2131 custody signature put her voice on his beacon; that derelict Buoy 47 held the call for nine years until Oyuna Brecht's illegal shoal shortcut dragged it into her horizon; and that Corvin Ladd's 2139 inquest missed it because dead nodes were filtered out of the evidence set. Tomas Weir's cooperative has a seventy-two-hour salvage clock running on the same call.
- **Tension:** 9/10
- **Promise advanced:** Promises 1, 3, 4 and 5 — all closed. Introduces nothing new.
- **Beats:**
  - She commits the station's power: the high-gain uplink draws 7 kW against a 5 kW plant, so she burns the full nine minutes a pass, three passes running, to file her custodial acknowledgement of a 2138 distress bundle plus the entire custody chain that proves it. The habitat heaters brown out to do it and the cold walks in from the edges of the room while she works in her liner.
  - She triangulates Wick-3's position from Buoy 47's sequence of custody stamps cross-referenced against Brecht's real track — the drag vector run backwards to where the anchor first fouled — and gets a fix she can defend to a metre.
  - Ladd's answer comes back across the Earth-link lag he cannot shorten, 79 to 89 minutes one way, a one-sided voice that has already stopped speaking by the time she hears it. He does not deny the filter default. He explains it: a transmitting beacon meant a chain that swallows distress traffic, and that finding would have frozen eighteen station retirements. Not a conspiracy. An incentive, and a default in a query filter.
  - She trades Weir the coordinates for a lift. The arithmetic is honest and he takes it: his cooperative raises Wick-3 intact, preserved unchanged at 94 K, and gives up its author — Halvard Ness, and the text he composed with his own hands while the pod went under, which her beacon has been speaking in her voice ever since.
  - Final beat: the acknowledgement she bought with her heaters becomes the Authority's own proof that the buoy chain can hold a distress bundle for nine years with no human to notice — exactly the insurability argument the charter clause exists to protect. Ladd's audit is reversed by the evidence Ladd's audit provoked. Station 12 stays crewed, which means Anselm stays: her vindication and her sentence in the same signature, keeping the lamp because the lamp was proved necessary by the one call she failed to hear.
- **Leaves open:** nothing structural. Close the voice, close Wick-3, close the inquest, close the audit. The only thing left hanging is her own choosing — end on her back at the console, logging the next acknowledgement nobody will read, with Saturn exactly where it was.
