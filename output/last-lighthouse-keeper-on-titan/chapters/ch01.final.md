# Chapter 1 — Custody Stamp 2138

The tholin came off the radar dome in flakes the colour of old tobacco, and Marit Anselm scraped at it with the edge of a nylon spatula because metal scratched the radome and a scratched radome scattered. Ninety-four kelvin. Four hours on the suit clock, two remaining before the heaters lost their argument with the nitrogen. The wind pushed at her back, thick and slow, the way a crowd pushes.

Inside, out of the shell, she logged the work nobody would read. Behind her on the board, thirty days old and unanswered, Corvin Ladd's decommissioning audit notice glowed its patient argument: *the buoy chain can carry custody unaided.*

The short-range link chimed at 0412 station time. A distress bundle, inbound off the buoy chain, flagged for custodial acknowledgement within one relay window.

She opened it and her own voice came out of the speaker.

Not a recording. Synthesis — the flat Authority cadence of a voiceprint beacon — but her vowels, the way she shortened the *a* in *ballast*, and the small dry catch in the throat before a hard consonant that she had been told about at nineteen and had never once managed to hear in herself. It said: *Ballast seal on the aft tank is going. Rate is increasing. I am setting the pumps to full and I do not expect it to be enough. Position is Nicholson Shoal, bearing—*

The words were plain and ordered and someone else's. She had never said them. She had never been in Nicholson Shoal in a pod with a failing tank. The voice was hers the way a fingerprint on a glass is yours after you have put the glass down.

Duplicate fault, she typed. Beacon echo, source unresolved. Filed.

Then she opened the custody chain, because a bundle carries the signed timestamps of every node that ever held it, and bundles cannot be forged or deleted, only delayed.

Composed 2138.

She read it four times and it did not change, because it could not. The origin stamp was nine years old and the custody stamp under it belonged to Buoy 47 — a node written off, a node the Authority's own tables listed as dead and left floating wherever it had drifted. After that, every stamp was Buoy 47 again, and none of them older than thirty days. After that, Buoy 47 again, and again, and again, the chain doubling back through the same dead node for nine years, and not one stamp in it older than thirty days. She scrolled to the bottom and back to the top. It was not possible and it was signed. She wrote no theory down.

She went at it sideways, because the front of it was a wall. She pulled her own radar track for the past week and laid it against the Kraken Freight Guild's filed weather. Seven days back: the *Sorrel Draught*, barge master Oyuna Brecht, cutting a line straight through Nicholson Shoal on a sea state her filing did not justify. Marit noted it, without knowing what it was worth, the way you note a number before you know it is the answer.

At 0631 the link chimed again.

The same voice. Her voice. And the custody stamps were four days old.
