# Chapter 3 — Nine Minutes a Pass

She spent the heaters first, because the heaters were hers to spend.

Nine minutes a pass, three passes running: seven kilowatts drawn against a five-kilowatt plant, the battery bank sagging, the habitat browning out from the walls inward. Marit Anselm worked in her liner with her hands going stiff and the cold arriving the way everything on Titan arrived, from the edges and late. Uplink one carried the custodial acknowledgement of a distress bundle composed in 2138. Uplink two carried the custody chain entire — every stamp Buoy 47 had laid on it for nine years, unhandoffed, unread. Uplink three carried the fix.

The fix she got on day four. Buoy 47's stamps were a position log nobody had asked to read: each one a timestamp against a drift the buoy had not chosen. She laid them over Oyuna Brecht's real track through Nicholson Shoal — not the filed weather, the track — and ran the drag vector backwards ten days to the point where the anchor had first fouled the derelict. One hundred eighty metres of methane-ethane under it. She could defend that fix to a metre, and she wrote down how, twice.

Corvin Ladd's answer came back through the Ligeia Head dish by way of the Authority's Earth office, which meant eighty-some minutes out and eighty-some minutes home, a lag he could not shorten and had not tried to. A one-sided voice that had stopped speaking before she heard a word of it.

He did not deny the filter. He explained it. Dead nodes excluded from the audit set; a buoy that has never handed off reports dead; and a transmitting beacon meant a chain that swallowed distress traffic, and that finding would have frozen eighteen station retirements. *Not a conspiracy,* he said, into the hour and a half between them. *An incentive. And a default.*

Tomas Weir took the coordinates for a lift, because the arithmetic was honest and he was scrupulous about custom. His cooperative raised *Wick-3* intact on day four, seals unbroken where they had not broken, the whole of it preserved unchanged at ninety-four kelvin, and gave up its author. Halvard Ness, nine years dead, steady on the record. The text was still in the beacon's buffer in his own composition, plainspoken, ordered, finished. Her voice had been saying his words since before she was censured for the silence.

The acknowledgement she had bought with her heaters became the Authority's proof that the chain could hold a distress bundle nine years with no human to notice it. That was the charter clause exactly. Ladd's audit reversed on the evidence Ladd's audit had provoked. Station 12 stayed crewed, which meant she stayed.

Vindication and sentence in one signature. She brought the heaters up, sat back at the console, and logged the next acknowledgement nobody would read. Saturn was where it had been.
