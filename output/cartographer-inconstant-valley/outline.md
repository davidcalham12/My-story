# Scarrowdale — Outline

## Acts

Three movements across eight chapters. **Act I (Chapters 1–3)** sets the instruments against the dale and lets the survey appear to work: Hale arrives, takes his first chained line, and proves to his own satisfaction that Scarrowdale's reputation is slander — the false summit, and the book's deepest lull sits at its top. **The first turn falls at Chapter 4**, when Hale rides out over Sile Ford and comes back to find the same line moved by the width of a field; from there **Act II (Chapters 4–6)** is the dismantling — the theodolite left overnight, the Raven Hause entry in his own hand that he cannot remember making, and Hannah Rigg naming, over tea, exactly what the estate is buying with the dale's silence. **The second turn falls at the end of Chapter 6**, when Rigg asks to be numbered and tithed. **Act III (Chapters 7–8)** is reconstruction and payment: Fowler's admission, the theodolite read correctly at last, and Rogation Monday, where the plan closes on the only three things that measure the same for everybody and the dale is written down to save one widow's hay.

## Promises to the reader

- **Will the plan be sealed, and will Hale be paid?** A man with a note falling due at Midsummer and instruments bought on it must get a first-class plan under seal, out of a parish that has defeated four surveyors. Asked in Chapter 1, pressed in Chapters 4 and 5, paid in Chapter 8.
- **Why does no two plans of Scarrowdale agree?** Four honest surveys, four different dales. Suspected in Chapter 2, apparently disproved in Chapter 3, proved with its exception in Chapter 4, understood completely in Chapter 7, and used in Chapter 8.
- **Who took the Raven Hause entry that Hale cannot remember making?** An entry in his own writing, lawfully witnessed, that fixes the estate's line. Discovered in Chapter 5, reconstructed in Chapter 6, admitted in Chapter 7, and lawfully undone in Chapter 8.
- **What does Hollins Hall gain by the bound falling at Raven Hause, and what will it cost to stop it?** Three intakes of good grass and a widow made a trespasser on her own hay. Named plainly by Rigg in Chapter 6, and paid in Chapter 8 at the price of the dale being written down at last.
- **Why does a theodolite left overnight face the church tower by dawn?** Not malice and not a warning. The event in Chapter 5, read correctly in Chapter 7, and obeyed in Chapter 8, where the columns sum twice alike for the first time.

## Tension curve

- Chapter 1 — a courteous arrival with the wrong number under it — tension 3/10
- Chapter 2 — the chain works and nobody will say a figure — tension 5/10
- Chapter 3 — the lull: Sunday, good weather, arithmetic that repeats — tension 2/10
- Chapter 4 — the same line has moved by the width of a field — tension 6/10
- Chapter 5 — an entry in his own hand he has no memory of making — tension 8/10
- Chapter 6 — tea at the highest farm, and the trap said out loud — tension 5/10
- Chapter 7 — Fowler's mark explained and the instrument understood — tension 9/10
- Chapter 8 — the bound walked, the plan sealed, the dale written down — tension 7/10

## The chapter entries

### Chapter 1 — The Ford Stone

- **Day** — Day 1, Thursday 11 May 1843.
- **POV and place** — Edmund Hale. Sile Ford at the dale mouth, the lane up to Nether Scarrow, the church, and the inn parlour that evening.
- **Tension** — 3/10.
- **Promise advanced** — Will the plan be sealed and Hale be paid; why no two plans agree.
- **Beats**
  1. Hale comes in over Sile Ford with chain, theodolite and two beacon poles on a hired cart, and measures the ford stone as a check against the four plans in his satchel, finding it agrees with all four — the only thing in the parish that does.
  2. Going up the lane he asks a woman carrying milk how far it is to Nether Scarrow and is told how long the walk takes and who last walked it, and he asks the same question three more ways before he stops.
  3. At the church Amos Dent unlocks the chest, puts the parish perambulation book into Hale's hands, and declines to read a single line of it aloud, telling him instead the length of the Rogation walk in the saying of the Creed and the name of the man who last walked it.
  4. Alone in the inn parlour Hale lays the estate, enclosure, turnpike and county plans side by side and finds the Sile running four different courses, then makes his first field-book entry in iron-gall ink and reckons the days he has before the note on his instruments falls due at Midsummer.
  5. Silas Brock rides down from Hollins Hall after dark, buys the surveyor's supper, offers himself as a standing witness for any entry at any hour, and mentions that the estate has always understood the head of the parish to be at Raven Hause.
- **What this chapter must plant** — The three constant marks begin with the ford stone; the perambulation book is in Hale's keeping and unread; Brock's line is Raven Hause and his method is availability.
- **What the reader knows by the end** — Four plans disagree, one stone does not, nobody born here will say a distance aloud, and the man paying for the survey already knows the answer he wants.
- **Ends on** — Brock's lantern going back up the dale, and Hale writing *check ford stone again on leaving* in the margin.

### Chapter 2 — Ten Arrows

- **Day** — Days 2–3, Friday 12 and Saturday 13 May.
- **POV and place** — Edmund Hale. The low pastures above Nether Scarrow, the chained line along the Sile, and the church porch at dusk.
- **Tension** — 5/10.
- **Promise advanced** — Why no two plans agree; will the plan be sealed.
- **Beats**
  1. Hale runs his first chained line with Nat Fowler leading and the hired Ingleford lad following, and when he calls for the count Fowler draws the ten arrows, sets them in Hale's open palm, and tells him that is what is in his hand and he will not say it for him.
  2. Standing at the station Hale writes the entry in ink and asks the two farmers watching from the wall to witness it; both decline courteously and go on watching, so the entry is signed by Brock, who has arrived early with a pen, and by the Ingleford lad, before Hale leaves the station.
  3. Hale walks the same line against the enclosure plan and finds it short by more than the plan's error could account for, and finds no field corner where the estate plan puts one.
  4. At the church porch that evening Dent tells him, without naming a direction, that the man who drew the enclosure plan in 1836 never walked the bound and never came back, and that nobody ever told him why his plan was not confirmed.
- **What this chapter must plant** — Fowler's refusal to speak a figure and his willingness to be near the book; Brock's signature as the first of many; the 1836 surveyor who left and never returned.
- **What the reader knows by the end** — The dale answers every question and refuses none, and the only two hands willing to witness the survey are the estate's and a boy hired from outside.
- **Ends on** — Hale counting the signatures already in his book and finding Brock's on every page but the first.

### Chapter 3 — Identical to the Link

- **Day** — Days 4–5, Sunday 14 and Monday 15 May.
- **POV and place** — Edmund Hale. Nether Scarrow church, the fell tops at Raven Hause and Sile Head, and the low pasture of the Day 2 line.
- **Tension** — 2/10.
- **Promise advanced** — Why no two plans agree (the false answer).
- **Beats**
  1. At Sunday service Hale notices that Dent preaches a whole sermon on the Rogation without once naming a direction or a distance, and that the congregation follows it perfectly.
  2. Hale sets the two beacon poles, one on Raven Hause and one on Sile Head, takes the angles between them in good weather with Brock and the Ingleford lad signing the entry at the station before he leaves it, and comes down to find tea poured for him at three farms in turn.
  3. Alone on the Monday, Hale re-measures the whole Day 2 line with his own hands and it comes back identical to the link, and he walks it a second time to be sure.
  4. He writes in the margin of the field book, in ink and in his own hand, that the dale's reputation for crooked measure is slander and that every surveyor before him was careless — and Fowler, coiling the chain, watches him write it and says nothing.
- **What this chapter must plant** — The margin note Hale will have to read again; the two beacon poles standing; Fowler having watched the one measurement that repeated.
- **What the reader knows by the end** — Hale has proved the dale honest to his own satisfaction, and the proof is perfectly true and means the opposite of what he thinks.
- **Ends on** — A dry evening, a column that sums, and Hale sleeping well for the only time in the book.

### Chapter 4 — The Width of a Field

- **Day** — Days 6–7, Tuesday 16 and Wednesday 17 May.
- **POV and place** — Edmund Hale. Out over Sile Ford to Ingleford and back; then the Day 2 line in the low pasture on the Wednesday afternoon.
- **Tension** — 6/10.
- **Promise advanced** — Why no two plans agree (the true answer, with its exception); will the plan be sealed.
- **Beats**
  1. Hale rides out over Sile Ford to Ingleford to post his interim return to Somerset House, and looks back from the far bank at a dale that lies flat and ordinary in the afternoon light.
  2. At Ingleford he has his Gunter's chain compared against the standard and it is found true to the link, and the certificate is written out and put in his pocket.
  3. He rides back in on the Wednesday afternoon, takes up the same chain on the same line with Fowler leading, and the figure comes back longer than his own Day 2 figure by the width of a field.
  4. He runs the line three more times before the light goes, gets three more figures, and then sits at the station with the book open and finds the column will not sum twice alike however he adds it.
  5. Fowler, who was on the chain for every run, coils it, sets the arrows down beside Hale's hand, and walks down to Nether Scarrow without having said one word about the figures.
- **What this chapter must plant** — Hale has now left the dale and returned, so nothing he re-measures will ever repeat again; the certificate proving the fault is not in the chain; Fowler's silence as knowledge, not ignorance.
- **What the reader knows by the end** — The rule and its exception: the dale gives the same answer to the hand that first asked, until that hand goes out over the ford and comes back a stranger.
- **Ends on** — Hale reading his own Chapter 3 margin note about slander by candlelight, and not crossing it out because nothing in his book may be struck through.

### Chapter 5 — First Light on Raven Hause

- **Day** — Days 8–9, Thursday 18 and Friday 19 May.
- **POV and place** — Edmund Hale. The inn parlour, a night out on Sile Head beside the standing theodolite, the Raven Hause station at first light, and the inn again by evening.
- **Tension** — 8/10.
- **Promise advanced** — Who took the entry Hale cannot remember; why the theodolite faces the tower.
- **Beats**
  1. In the inn parlour on the Thursday evening Brock, pouring for everyone, remarks to Fowler that his father's yearly tenancy comes up for renewal at Martinmas and that Hollins Hall likes a man who is useful, and Hale half-hears it and thinks nothing of it.
  2. Rather than lose an unfinished sight, Hale breaks the dale's one plain custom and leaves the theodolite set up on Sile Head overnight, sleeping out beside it in his coat with the cloud coming down.
  3. At first light Brock comes up the fell with the field book under his arm and a lantern already blown out, and Hale is awake and walked across to the Raven Hause station while the light comes onto the fell — and afterwards he can account for the cold, the wet bracken and Brock's lantern, and not for the pen.
  4. Returning to Sile Head in the middle of the morning, Hale finds the theodolite standing exactly where he left it and turned to face the church tower at Nether Scarrow, with no mark in the grass around its feet.
  5. Reading his own book at the inn that evening he finds a Day 9 entry in his own honest hand, an angle taken on the boundary oak, placing the head of the parish at Raven Hause, witnessed by Fowler's mark and Dent's signature — with Brock's name nowhere on the page — and when he reaches for the pen to strike it through he remembers that a correction is a new entry and needs two witnesses of its own who stood at that station.
- **What this chapter must plant** — The lawful entry that now has to be undone lawfully; Brock's absent signature; the theodolite's direction as an unread instruction; Martinmas spoken where Fowler could hear it.
- **What the reader knows by the end** — Nothing was forged and no rule was broken, which is exactly why the estate's line is now the law of the parish.
- **Ends on** — Hale writing out the rule for corrections in full on a loose sheet, and understanding that he cannot lawfully touch his own book until two people who will sign stand with him at the oak.

### Chapter 6 — What Is Written Can Be Taken

- **Day** — Day 10, Saturday 20 May.
- **POV and place** — Hannah Rigg, at the highest farm under Raven Hause and among her three intakes above the beck. **This chapter earns the switch**: the trap is hers, she worked it out before anyone, and the only way the reader can see the dale's courtesy from inside it — and see Hale as a man being politely handled — is to stand behind the eyes of someone who will not say a number to save her own hay.
- **Tension** — 5/10.
- **Promise advanced** — What Hollins Hall gains and what it costs to stop it; who took the Raven Hause entry.
- **Beats**
  1. Rigg watches Hale come up the fell track with his book under his arm, has the kettle on before he reaches the yard, and pours his tea while answering his question about the extent of her ground with how long it takes her sons to mow it.
  2. She tells him, without a distance or a direction anywhere in it, that Mr. Brock was abroad before the light on the Friday morning with a lantern he had no use for coming back, because her elder son was up with a sick ewe and saw him pass twice.
  3. She walks Hale out among the three intakes, names each by its name and not its bearing, and says plainly that if the head of the parish is put at the oak and the tops entered as waste of the manor, her hay becomes Hollins Hall's rough grazing at Lady Day and she is a trespasser on it.
  4. Hale tells her that the only remedy is to have the intakes numbered, and that numbered means tithed and written down forever, and Rigg — who has kept her family's ground safe for a century by being on no plan at all — says number them.
- **What this chapter must plant** — Rigg's willingness to be tithed; her eyewitness account of Brock on the Friday dawn; her refusal to ask Dent any favour, which is why the chest stays shut until Rogationtide.
- **What the reader knows by the end** — The estate needs no lie and no threat, only the dale's own silence, and the one person who can break it can only do so by giving up what the silence was protecting.
- **Ends on** — Rigg going back to the kettle, and the intakes standing in the last of the light with nothing written on them anywhere in England.

### Chapter 7 — Durations and Dead Men

- **Day** — Day 11, Sunday 21 May.
- **POV and place** — Edmund Hale. The inn parlour over the perambulation book, the fell up to the boundary oak with Fowler, and the church at dusk.
- **Tension** — 9/10.
- **Promise advanced** — Who took the Raven Hause entry; why the theodolite faces the tower; why no two plans agree.
- **Beats**
  1. Hale reads the perambulation book through for the first time and finds a bound recorded entirely in durations and the names of dead men, which goes out wide round three intakes above the beck and has done so in every hand for a hundred and forty years.
  2. He takes Fowler up to the boundary oak and asks him four different ways what happened at first light on the Friday, and Fowler, looking at the oak and not at Hale, says that he put his mark to what was set in front of him, that the angle on the oak was true, and that Mr. Brock had spoken of Martinmas the night before.
  3. Hale sets the theodolite on the boundary oak and sights the church tower and the ford stone in turn, and the three angles close on themselves to the second — the first thing in eleven days that has agreed with itself — and he understands that the instrument facing the tower at dawn was not a warning but an instruction.
  4. Brock climbs up to him with a fair copy of the apportionment already engrossed, the upper pastures entered as waste of the manor, and offers warmly to save Hale the Rogation walk and the delay, and Hale declines and gives no reason.
  5. At the church at dusk Hale tells Dent that he will take a new entry at the Raven Hause station on the Monday and will need two witnesses who have stood there, and Dent answers that he will sign a bound he has actually walked and will sign it once.
- **What this chapter must plant** — The triangulation on tower, ford stone and oak as the method that will close the plan; Dent's condition for signing; Brock's engrossed copy waiting for a seal it is not going to get.
- **What the reader knows by the end** — Every piece is now in Hale's hands, and the only lawful way to use them is to walk the bound with the whole dale on Rogation Monday.
- **Ends on** — Dent locking the church and remarking, without naming a direction, that the walk begins when the light is on the fell and not before.

### Chapter 8 — The Walked Bound

- **Day** — Days 12–13, Rogation Monday 22 and Tuesday 23 May.
- **POV and place** — Edmund Hale. The Rogation perambulation from Nether Scarrow up the dale to Raven Hause, the boundary oak and its station, then the church and Sile Ford on the Tuesday.
- **Tension** — 7/10.
- **Promise advanced** — What it costs to save the intakes; who took the Raven Hause entry (undone); will the plan be sealed; why the theodolite faces the tower.
- **Beats**
  1. The dale walks the Rogation bound behind Dent, who reads the perambulation book aloud in durations and dead men's names without once saying a distance, and at the top of the dale Hannah Rigg takes the walk wide, out round her three intakes, and every soul following turns with her and nobody contradicts it.
  2. At the boundary oak Rigg lays her hand flat on the trunk and holds it there, and Hale takes the angle off her hand because the oak measures true for everybody and she will not say a bearing aloud.
  3. Standing at the Raven Hause station with the light still on the fell, Hale writes a new entry correcting the Day 9 entry — the head of the parish at the oak by the bound as walked, the intakes inside it — and Rigg and Dent, who both stood there through the taking of it, sign before he leaves the station.
  4. On the Tuesday Hale rebuilds the whole triangulation on the church tower, the ford stone and the boundary oak, numbers every field inside the walked bound including the three intakes, and adds the columns twice and gets the same total twice for the first time since he came into the dale; Dent signs the bound, and the plan goes to Somerset House under seal.
  5. Brock is the first man to congratulate him, shakes his hand without a word of complaint, and pays the survey's bills to the penny; and Hale rides out over Sile Ford with his fee assured, his note answerable at Midsummer, and the certain knowledge that the three intakes are written down forever and that if he ever comes back into Scarrowdale not one line he drew will measure the same again.
- **What this chapter must plant** — Nothing forward; this chapter closes the book.
- **What the reader knows by the end** — The plan is sealed and honest, Rigg keeps her hay because it is now numbered and tithed, and the dale that stayed off paper for a century is on paper at last because being written down was the only thing that could save anybody.
- **Ends on** — The ford stone measuring exactly what it measured on Day 1, and Hale not looking back at the dale behind him.
