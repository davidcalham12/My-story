# Scarrowdale — Mysteries

Four questions. Each answer below is canon; the chapters must not invent a different one.

---

## 1. Why does no two plans of Scarrowdale agree?

**The reader is asked.** Four plans exist — estate, enclosure, turnpike, county — and they disagree on the beck's course, the number of fields and where the parish stops. Is this fraud, incompetence, or something in the ground?

**What is true.** Neither. It is lawful and it is consistent. Only three things in the dale measure the same for everyone in every survey ever made: the church tower at Nether Scarrow, the ford stone at Sile Ford, the boundary oak at Raven Hause. Everything else returns the same figure only to the one hand that first took it, and only while that hand has not passed out of the dale and come back. Four plans were made by four surveyors, each of whom left; so there are four dales on paper, each of them honest. The dale is not lying. It is answering the person asking, and it has never once refused to answer.

**Where answered.** Suspected from Day 2 (Chapter 2), proved to Hale by his own hand on Day 4 when his re-measurement matches to the link (Chapter 3), and confirmed as a rule — with its exception — when he returns from Ingleford on Day 7 and the same line has moved (Chapter 4).

---

## 2. Who took the Raven Hause entry that Hale cannot remember making?

**The reader is asked.** On Day 9 there is an entry in the field book, in Hale's own hand and ink, signed with Nat Fowler's mark and Amos Dent's signature, placing the head of the parish at the boundary oak. It is properly witnessed, properly timed, unimpeachable — and Hale has no memory of it. Who forged it?

**What is true.** Nobody forged anything, and that is the horror. Hale spent the night of Day 8 on Sile Head to keep his sight. Silas Brock came up at first light with the field book, the lantern already out, and walked him across to the Raven Hause station while the light was coming onto the fell. Hale was awake, the station was real, the entry is his own honest writing of an angle on the oak — which measures true for everybody. He remembers the cold and not the pen. Fowler signed because Brock had mentioned his father's tenancy at Martinmas the evening before; Dent signed because the entry recorded the oak and the oak cannot be wrong. Every rule was obeyed. The entry stands, and under the rules a correction to a signed entry is itself a new entry needing two witnesses of its own — which is why it takes Rogation Monday to undo.

**Where answered.** Discovered in Chapter 5, reconstructed piece by piece across Chapters 6 and 7, with Fowler's admission the last piece.

---

## 3. What does the Hollins Hall estate gain by the bound falling at Raven Hause?

**The reader is asked.** Brock is helpful past the point of plausibility. He pays for the survey he could obstruct. What is he actually buying?

**What is true.** Hannah Rigg's three intakes above the beck. They have been walked inside the Rogation bound for a hundred years and appear on no plan at all. If the head of the parish is fixed at Raven Hause and the upper pastures are entered as waste of the manor, the intakes fall outside the numbered fields, become rough grazing of Hollins Hall, and Rigg is a trespasser on her own hay at Lady Day. Brock does not need a false figure to get it; he needs only that the dale go on saying nothing, which is what the dale has always done. The estate's interest and the dale's own silence are pointing the same way, and only Rigg has worked that out.

**Where answered.** Named plainly by Rigg in Chapter 6; its cost paid in Chapter 8, where saving the intakes means getting them numbered and tithed — the dale written down at last, which is exactly what the dale walked the bound for a century to prevent.

---

## 4. Why does a theodolite left overnight face the church tower by dawn?

**The reader is asked.** No local will leave an instrument standing after sunset. Hale does, on Day 8. At dawn it is turned to the church tower, no mud disturbed, no hand on it.

**What is true.** Left to itself with an unfinished sight, the instrument settles on the only direction in the dale that is stable for everyone. The church tower is one of the three constant marks. Nothing carries it and nothing needs to; the dale refers an open question to the thing that cannot be measured wrong, the way water finds the beck. It is not a warning and it is not malice. It is, once Hale understands it, an instruction: hang the survey on the tower, the ford stone and the oak, and the plan will close.

**Where answered.** The event in Chapter 5; Hale reads it correctly in Chapter 7 and rebuilds the whole triangulation on the three constants in Chapter 8, at which point the columns sum twice alike for the first time.
