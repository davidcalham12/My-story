Days 2–3, Friday 12 and Saturday 13 May. Hale ran his first chained line along the Sile, Nat Fowler leading and the hired Ingleford lad Coulter following. Asked for the count, Fowler turned Hale's hand palm up and laid the ten arrows into it: "That's what's in your hand, sir. I'll not say it for you." Ten chains and fifty-three links, taken by Hale himself. Two farmers watched from the wall from the second change and courteously declined to witness — not hostile, and they did not leave. Silas Brock had been there since six, having come down from Hollins Hall in the dark with a lantern, and signed at the station; Coulter signed under him before Hale stepped off it. In the afternoon Hale checked the line against the enclosure plan of 1836: the plan gives rather more than eleven chains where he has ten and fifty-three, a difference no surveyor's error covers, and there is no field corner where the plan puts one. At dusk Dent told him the 1836 surveyor never walked the bound, went out and never came back, and was never told why his plan was unconfirmed. Brock's hand is on every page of Hale's book but the first.
