Day 11, Sunday 21 May. Hale finally read the perambulation book. There are no figures in it at all: the bound is kept in recitations — a psalm, the Creed said through, the Lord's Prayer twice — and in the names of dead men, and in every hand back to 1703 the walk goes out wide round the three intakes above the beck and returns to the oak. A hundred and forty years of it, signed each time by the incumbent. He took Fowler up to the boundary oak and asked four ways what happened at first light on the Friday. Fowler, looking at the oak, said he put his mark to what was set in front of him, that the angle on the oak was true, and that Mr Brock had spoken of Martinmas the night before. Then Hale set the theodolite over the oak and sighted the church tower and the ford stone, and the round closed to the second, three times — the first thing in eleven days to agree with itself. He understood the instrument turned to the tower as an instruction, not a warning. Brock came up with a fair copy already engrossed, the upper pastures as waste of the manor, and was refused without a reason. At the porch Hale told Dent he will take a fresh entry at the station at the oak tomorrow, needing two witnesses who stood there; the Friday signatures count for nothing. Dent will sign a bound he has walked, once. The walk starts when the light is on the fell.
