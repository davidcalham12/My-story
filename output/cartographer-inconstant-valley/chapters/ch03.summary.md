Days 4–5, Sunday 14 and Monday 15 May. At service Amos Dent preached the whole Rogation bound from the pulpit — where they stop, who stopped there, how long each stretch takes — without once naming a direction or a distance, and the congregation followed it perfectly. Hale took it for a charming country manner and was glad of it. He set the two beacon poles, one under the boundary oak at Raven Hause and one on Sile Head, and took the angle between them in clear weather; Brock and Coulter signed at the station before he stepped off it. Coming down he was given tea at three farms, and at each his question about how far was answered with how long it took and who last did it. On the Monday, alone and with no witness, he re-measured the Day 2 low pasture line twice: ten chains, fifty-three links both times, identical to the link. He wrote in the margin of the field book, in ink, that the 1836 plan is forty-seven links in error, that the dale's reputation for crooked measure is slander, and that every man who came before him was careless. Fowler watched him write it and said nothing. The column summed. He slept the whole night.
