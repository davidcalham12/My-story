Days 8–9, Thursday 18 and Friday 19 May. In the inn parlour Brock, pouring, remarked to Nat Fowler that his father's yearly ground comes up at Martinmas and that Hollins Hall likes a useful man. Hale half-heard it. Rather than lose an unfinished sight he left the theodolite set up on Sile Head overnight — a thing no local does — and slept out beside it in cloud. At first light Brock came up with Hale's field book and a lantern already blown out, and walked him across to the Raven Hause station with the light standing on the fell. Fowler was there with the flag and Amos Dent a little apart. Hale remembers the cold, the wet bracken and the lantern. He does not remember the pen. Mid-morning the theodolite stood exactly where he left it, turned to face the church tower, no mark in the grass. That evening, reducing the week, he found an entry in his own honest hand: an angle oak to tower, taken at the Raven Hause station, carrying the head of the parish to Raven Hause — witnessed by Fowler's mark and Dent's signature, with Brock's name nowhere on the leaf. He cannot strike it out. A correction is a new entry needing two witnesses who stood for the taking of the correction itself.
