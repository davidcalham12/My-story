Days 6–7, Tuesday 16 and Wednesday 17 May. Hale rode out over Sile Ford to Ingleford, posted his interim return to Somerset House, and looked back from the far bank at a dale that lay flat and ordinary — a green wedge, no stranger than any valley he had chained. At Ingleford the borough surveyor Hetherington compared his Gunter's against the standard and found it true to the link, and wrote him a certificate, which Hale put in his breast pocket. He came back over the ford on the Wednesday at three and ran the low pasture line again, Fowler leading: eleven chains, seventy-two links — one chain nineteen links longer than his own entered figure of ten chains fifty-three links. He ran it three more times before the light went off Raven Hause and got ten chains ninety-six, eleven chains forty, twelve chains eleven. Four figures, no two alike. He would not enter them in the field book, having only one witness and no wish to seal a contradiction, and set them on the back of the certificate instead. They sum and mean to nothing on the ground. The chain is proved true. Fowler, on the chain for every run, coiled it, set the arrows by Hale's hand, and went home without one word about the figures. Hale read Monday's margin note by candlelight at the inn and could not strike it out: a correction is a new entry needing two witnesses of its own.
