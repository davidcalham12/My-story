Day 1, Thursday 11 May 1843. Edmund Hale, tithe surveyor from Lancaster, came into Scarrowdale over Sile Ford. He measured the ford stone against his four plans — estate, enclosure of 1836, turnpike, county — and it agreed with all four in relation to the church tower. Nothing else on them agrees; the Sile runs four different courses. Going up the lane he asked a woman with milk cans how far Nether Scarrow was, four ways, and got durations and the last man to walk it, never a number or a bearing. At the church Amos Dent unlocked the chest, put the parish perambulation book into Hale's hands to keep, and would not read a line of it aloud. Hale made his first field-book entry in iron-gall that evening at the inn. His note on the theodolite and chain falls due at Midsummer: forty-four days to get a plan sealed. Silas Brock of Hollins Hall rode down after dark, offered to witness any entry at any hour, and said the estate has always understood the head of the parish to be at Raven Hause. Hale has found nobody else who will sign.
