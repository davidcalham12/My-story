# Mysteries

- **Why has no resident countersigned a sheet in nine years?**
  - True: Rosa Vane signed the last one. Sam Ord's eleventh sheet was accurate the day it was drawn, and the moment it was carried out of the valley it went ordinary and the ground let go — but the acreage on it stayed filed. The households have paid rents and drainage levies for nine years on land the valley no longer grants. The refusal is not superstition; it is an invoice.
  - Planted: Chapter 1, Rosa Vane declines to sign while being perfectly hospitable, and the grant file's nine-year figure sits in Hartley's brief beside it.
  - Lands: Chapter 2, Rosa explains it to Hartley in acres, levies and years, without once calling the valley strange.

- **Who is the wall-mender who knows the stones by number, and what happened to the eleventh sheet?**
  - True: he is Sam Ord, the cartographer contracted before Hartley. He filed the eleventh sheet and kept Notebook 11 rather than submitting it, so the sheet reached the registry clean, defensible and unaccompanied by the corrections that would have shown it moving. He stayed in the valley because the signature he talked Rosa Vane into is being paid off in levies by people who still feed him.
  - Planted: Chapter 2, Ord corrects Hartley's chainage without looking up from the wall, and Notebook 11 turns out to be in the valley and not in the file.
  - Lands: Chapter 3, Ord tells Hartley what he left out of his submission and why, and hands over Notebook 11 to be filed nine years late.

- **Can a map that tells the truth about this valley also be a map the Survey will accept?**
  - True: yes, but only by claiming less. Form 9 has no box for uncertainty, so Hartley files a sheet that asserts only what the benchmark stones fix — the nine stones, the ridge, each reading named to its stone — and states no acreage at all. Nothing on it is false, nothing on it can be assessed for levy, and Rosa Vane will countersign that. The valley keeps everything the sheet declined to name, and goes on moving there.
  - Planted: Chapter 1, Hartley reads Form 9 and Penn's instruction for one sheet, signed, no annotations in the margin, and finds nowhere to write what she already suspects.
  - Lands: Chapter 3, the reduced sheet is drawn, countersigned and sent out with Notebook 12 and its struck-through corrections intact.
