# World

The valley lies four hours east of the district office, between a railway that was surveyed once and abandoned and a ridge everyone agrees is a ridge. It has been mapped eleven times in forty years and no two sheets agree. The Regional Survey holds a small standing grant for the work, reviewed every third autumn. This autumn's review falls in six weeks, and the file requires a final map: one sheet, signed, no annotations in the margin.

## Factions

- **The Regional Survey** — wants one defensible sheet it can file, because a grant that produces no map is a grant that ends; it gets it by sending a contracted cartographer out alone with a deadline, a per-diem, and a form that has no box for uncertainty.
- **The valley's tenant households** — want the ground to stay unmeasured, because rents and drainage levies are assessed from filed acreage; they get it by hospitality, by telling nobody anything, and by declining to sign.

## Means

- **The benchmark stones** — nine cut stones set by an earlier survey, the only things in the valley that do not move.
- **The field notebook** — bound and numbered, submitted with the map; strikethrough permitted, erasure not.
- **Form 9** — the countersignature page; without it the registry will not accept a sheet.

## Rules

- **R1.** The valley changes shape only in full dark, between the end of dusk and first light, and never while anyone can see it happen. Any reading taken while there is still light to read the instrument by — dusk included — holds exactly until dark.
- **R2.** A feature reads the same twice from the same benchmark stone and differently from any other; a reading that does not name its stone is not a measurement.
- **R3.** Nothing in the field notebook may be erased. Corrections are struck through and stay legible, and the notebook is filed with the map.
- **R4.** Whatever a map shows stops moving, but only while that map is inside the valley. Carried out, the sheet goes ordinary and the ground lets go.
- **R5.** The Survey files no sheet without a resident's countersignature, and no resident has countersigned in nine years.

## Texture

Strangeness arrives here as an administrative inconvenience. The light is ordinary, the rain is ordinary, the tea is offered twice; only the distances misbehave, and politely, overnight, where nobody has to watch. People speak of the valley the way they speak of a difficult relative — with patience, some fondness, and no intention of explaining it to the district office.
