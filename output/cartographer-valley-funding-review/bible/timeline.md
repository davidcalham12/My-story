# Timeline

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Forty years before the story | The first survey cuts and sets Stone One through Stone Nine and files the first sheet. Ten more sheets follow over the decades; no two agree, and the standing grant is written to be reviewed every third autumn. |
| Nine years before the story | Sam Ord measures the valley in daylight, gets Rosa Vane's countersignature on Form 9, and files the eleventh sheet — without Notebook 11, which he keeps. The sheet leaves the valley, goes ordinary, and the ground lets go. |
| The nine years since | Rents and drainage levies are assessed from Ord's filed acreage, which the ground stopped supporting the night the sheet left. No resident signs anything again. Ord does not go back to the district; he takes a tenancy and mends walls. |
| Six weeks before the review — Chapter 1 | Nell Hartley arrives with a per-diem, Form 9 blank and Notebook 12 unopened. She occupies Stone Three, takes a full day's readings that hold exactly until dusk, is given tea twice, and is declined a signature without being told why. |
| The following days — Chapter 2 | Hartley's dawn re-readings disagree with her dusk figures; she strikes through and keeps both. Rosa Vane explains the nine years in acres and levies. Ord corrects Hartley's chainage without looking, and Notebook 11 surfaces. |
| The last days before the deadline — Chapter 3 | Hartley draws a sheet that claims only what the nine stones fix and states no acreage. Rosa Vane countersigns Form 9. Sheet and Notebook 12 go out to the district office together, and at dusk the ground lets go of everything the sheet declined to name. |
