# Chapter 1 — Stone Three, Full Day

Four hours east of the district office the road stops pretending, and Nell Hartley got down with her case and opened Notebook 12 to the first page. She wrote the date. She wrote *fine, light southwest wind, cloud after noon*. Then she wrote *unopened*, because it had been until that moment, and a notebook that does not say when it began is an argument waiting to happen.

Arthur Penn’s covering letter ran to five numbered points. The fourth required one sheet, signed, no annotations in the margin. The fifth restated the review date, which she copied into the notebook under the weather, where it looked smaller than it was.

Form 9 she read twice. It had a box for the surveyor, a box for the resident, a box for acreage, and nowhere at all to say *this figure was true at the hour it was taken*. In the notebook’s margin — not on the form, the form allowed nothing — she wrote: *will need a box for uncertainty. None provided.*

The brief’s summary page gave her the figure she had half expected and underlined anyway: *no resident countersignature, nine years*.

She walked out at first light and found Stone Three where the eighth sheet said it would be, which she noted as a courtesy to the eighth sheet. She occupied it for a full day.

Nineteen readings. Stone Three, 412. Stone Three, 406. The stone named before the figure every time, because a reading that does not name its stone is not a measurement, and she had been doing this long enough to know which rules were pedantry and which were the whole job.

At dusk, with light still on the instrument, she went back through and took three of the nineteen again. Three identical figures. She wrote *holds exactly* beside them and felt, briefly, like someone who was being told something politely.

Rosa Vane met her on the track with a lamp she did not appear to need.

“You’ll have tea,” Rosa said. “Is it still raining in the district? They always say it is.”

“It wasn’t when I left.”

“It will be by the time you go back.” Rosa said nothing about the instrument, the case, or the nineteen readings, and asked instead whether the road had been bad at the bridge.

At the door she offered tea a second time. Hartley declined a second time and explained Form 9: the countersignature page, one signature, no acreage stated without it, no sheet filed at all.

“No,” said Rosa Vane, pleasantly, and did not say why. “Have you a dry coat?”

Walking back in the dark, Hartley did the arithmetic, which was the part of the day she trusted. Six weeks was thirty-one working days. She had spent one of them acquiring nineteen figures that held exactly, that named their stone, and that she could not file.
