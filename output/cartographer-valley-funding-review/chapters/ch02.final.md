# Chapter 2 — Struck Through, Kept Legible

At first light Hartley set up on Stone Three and re-ran the three bearings that had held exactly at dusk: the ridge notch, the fold at the beck, the corner of the lower wall. She took each twice, as instructed by nobody but herself.

Two disagreed. The ridge notch came back 417 against yesterday’s 412; the wall corner 398 against 406. She drew a single rule through each old figure, left it perfectly readable, and wrote the new one beneath. The beck held. She did not record relief, there being no box for it.

Counting up the page, she made it nine strikethroughs in two days. At the top of the sheet she wrote, in capitals, *Notebook 12 is filed with the map* — a note addressed to herself at some future hour when a clean page would look like a kindness.

She walked to Stone Seven and took the ridge notch again from there: 344. She entered all three with their stones named, Stone Three twice and Stone Seven once, because a figure without a stone is a rumour.

Below the beck a man was setting coping on a wall under repair. “Chainage two-eighty from the gate,” she said, aloud, the way one says a number to keep it.

“Two-ninety-one.” He did not look up. Then he did. “Sorry. Habit.”

His name was Sam Ord. She named stones at him — four, six, nine — and he gave their positions without pause, then the other six unasked. She asked who took the eleventh sheet. “Rained the whole of that week,” he said. “Cleared by the Thursday. You could see the top of the ridge for about an hour.”

“Where’s the notebook for it?”

“In the valley. In a drawer. Numbered eleven.” He weighed a stone in both hands. “These copings want laying flat side down. Everyone lays them wrong.”

Rosa Vane came down with tea on a tray and poured without asking. Hartley asked about Form 9 a second time.

“Filed acreage, upper holdings, four hundred and six acres,” Rosa said. “Drainage levy, one pound eleven the acre, assessed yearly. Nine years.”

“That’s—”

“Three thousand six hundred and some. On ground we’ve had the use of rather less of since the night that sheet went out.” She refilled Hartley’s cup. Difficult relative; fond; nothing for the district office.

Hartley opened Notebook 12 and wrote the levy figures on the facing page, opposite her own ruled-through columns, and looked at the two sets of numbers sitting there together. The refusal to sign was not superstition. It was an invoice, nine years outstanding, and no one had ever sent it anywhere.
