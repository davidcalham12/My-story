# Chapter 3 — What the Stones Fix

Four days before the deadline Hartley laid every reading in Notebook 12 out across the table in two groups: those named to a stone, and those tied to a landmark. Between them she ruled a single line. Below the line went bearings taken from the gate, the beck, the shoulder of the barn. Above the line, forty-one figures, each with a stone in front of it.

She drew the sheet from what was left. Stone One through Stone Nine, sited and numbered; the ridge, because the ridge is agreed; every reading attributed to the stone it came from. The acreage box she inked a border around and left empty, which took longer than filling it would have.

Sam Ord came to the door at eleven with a notebook under his coat and did not take tea. He said he had kept the corrections out of his submission — the dawn figures that disagreed with the dusk figures, nine years of them, the ones that would have shown the ground moving — so the eleventh sheet went to the registry clean.

“I stayed,” he said, “because the signature I talked Rosa Vane into is still being paid off in levies by people who feed me.” He put Notebook 11 on the table. He asked that it be filed nine years late.

Hartley wrote it down while there was light on the page: the admission, the date, his name. Then she turned back to the second week and drew one rule through *a tenant, name unknown*, leaving it legible, and wrote *Sam Ord, contracted cartographer, eleventh sheet* beside it.

Rosa Vane read the sheet at the kitchen table for a long time, tracing the empty box with one finger. “There’s no acreage here,” she said. “Nothing on this a man could assess.” Hartley offered the pen. Rosa Vane countersigned Form 9 and pushed it back without comment.

The covering note to Arthur Penn ran to numbered points, all of them negative. One: this sheet claims no acreage. Two: it claims no boundary not fixed by a benchmark stone. Three: Notebook 12 accompanies it with all corrections struck through and legible. Four: Notebook 11, submitted late by its author, is enclosed.

With the sheet still flat on her table she walked up to Stone Three in the last of the daylight and shot the ridge notch: 417. The same figure she had drawn that morning. The valley was holding, as it always held, until dark.

She carried sheet, Form 9, Notebook 12 and Notebook 11 down to the track end and gave the bundle to the carrier with both hands. She stood there until the bundle crossed the ridge, still lit.

After dark the ground let go of everything the sheet had declined to name. She heard nothing. There was nothing to hear. Inside the valley, with no instrument worth lifting until morning, she opened the spare pages and wrote: *19:40. Clear, no wind.* Then nothing else.
