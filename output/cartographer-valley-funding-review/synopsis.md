Nell Hartley has six weeks, a per-diem, and Notebook 12, and the arithmetic is unforgiving: thirty-one working days to produce the one thing the Regional Survey's grant now depends on — a single signed sheet, no margin annotations, before the funding review closes the file for good. Form 9 has boxes for surveyor, resident, acreage. It has no box for the fact that the valley will not hold still, and that any reading taken in daylight is honest only until dark. She spends her first day at Stone Three doing the work properly — nineteen readings, each named to its stone, three checked again at dusk and found, briefly, to agree. Rosa Vane, who holds the countersignature the Survey needs and has withheld it for nine years, offers tea twice, asks after Nell's coat, and says no without explaining why. By dawn the valley has already answered for her: two of yesterday's readings no longer match, the old figures struck through and kept legible, since the notebook permits correction and forbids erasure. A wall-mender who knows all nine stones by number corrects Nell's chainage without looking up, and mentions a notebook from the last survey that was never filed. Over a second cup of tea, Rosa Vane explains her refusal in figures rather than fear — levies, acreage, nine years of arithmetic. For readers who like their strangeness filed in triplicate: this is a book for anyone who has loved *Piranesi*, *Winter's Bone*, or *The Employees*.
