## Promises

1. **Why has no resident countersigned a sheet in nine years?** Rosa Vane pours tea twice and declines Form 9 without giving a reason — and the grant file says nobody has signed since the eleventh sheet.
2. **Who is the wall-mender who knows all nine stones by number, and what became of the eleventh sheet?** A tenant corrects a cartographer's chainage without looking up, and the notebook that should have been filed with that sheet is still in the valley.
3. **Can a map that tells the truth about this valley also be a map the Regional Survey will accept?** Form 9 has no box for uncertainty, and Arthur Penn's instruction is one sheet, signed, no annotations in the margin.
4. **Will Nell Hartley deliver a final map before the funding review at all** — with six weeks, a per-diem, and figures that disagree with themselves by morning?
5. **What does the valley do the night the sheet leaves?** Whatever a map shows stops moving, but only while the map is inside the valley.

## Chapters

### Chapter 1 — Stone Three, Full Day

- **POV:** Nell Hartley
- **Tension:** 4/10
- **Promise advanced:** 1 (planted), 3 (planted), 4
- **Beats:**
  1. Nell Hartley arrives four hours east of the district office and writes the date, the weather and the word *unopened* on the first page of Notebook 12.
  2. She reads Arthur Penn's covering letter, whose numbered points require one sheet, signed, no annotations in the margin, by a date she copies into the notebook.
  3. She turns to Form 9 and finds no box on it for uncertainty, and notes in the margin of the notebook — not the form — that she will need one.
  4. The brief's summary page gives a figure she underlines: no resident countersignature in nine years.
  5. She walks out at first light, finds Stone Three, and occupies it for a full day's work.
  6. She takes nineteen readings and writes each one with the stone named before the figure, as R2 requires.
  7. At dusk she re-reads three of the nineteen from Stone Three and gets three identical figures, and writes *holds exactly* beside them.
  8. Rosa Vane meets her on the track, offers tea, and asks about the weather in the district rather than about the readings.
  9. Rosa offers tea a second time, is told what Form 9 is for, and declines to sign it without saying why, then asks whether Hartley has a dry coat.
  10. Walking back in the dark, Hartley calculates that six weeks is thirty-one working days and that she has spent one of them getting figures she cannot yet file.

### Chapter 2 — Struck Through, Kept Legible

- **POV:** Nell Hartley
- **Tension:** 7/10
- **Promise advanced:** 1 (lands), 2 (planted), 4
- **Beats:**
  1. At dawn Hartley returns to Stone Three and re-runs the three readings that held exactly at dusk.
  2. Two of the three disagree with yesterday's figures, and she strikes each old figure through with a single rule and writes the new one beneath it, erasing nothing.
  3. She counts the strikethroughs already accumulating and writes *Notebook 12 is filed with the map* at the top of the page, as a reminder to herself.
  4. She moves to Stone Seven, takes the same bearing from there, gets a third figure, and records all three with their stones named.
  5. Passing a wall under repair, she says a chainage aloud to herself and a man setting stone corrects it by eleven metres without looking up, then apologises.
  6. He gives his name as Sam Ord, names all nine stones by number when she tests him, and answers her question about the eleventh sheet by describing the rain on the day he took it.
  7. She asks where the notebook for that sheet is, and he says it is in the valley, in a drawer, numbered eleven, and changes the subject to the coping stones.
  8. Rosa Vane brings tea out to the wall and, asked once more about Form 9, answers in figures instead: filed acreage, the drainage levy per acre, nine years.
  9. Rosa states the arithmetic of what the households have paid on ground the valley stopped granting the night the eleventh sheet went out, and does not once call the valley strange.
  10. Hartley writes the levy figures into Notebook 12 opposite her own struck-through readings, and understands that the refusal to sign is not superstition but an invoice.

### Chapter 3 — What the Stones Fix

- **POV:** Nell Hartley
- **Tension:** 6/10
- **Promise advanced:** 2 (lands), 3 (lands), 4, 5
- **Beats:**
  1. Four days before the deadline Hartley lays out every reading in Notebook 12 and rules a line under everything that is not named to a stone.
  2. She drafts a sheet that shows Stone One through Stone Nine, the ridge, and each reading attributed to its stone, and leaves the acreage box empty.
  3. Sam Ord comes to the door with Notebook 11 and tells her what he kept out of his submission: the corrections that would have shown the ground moving.
  4. He explains that he stayed because the signature he talked Rosa Vane into is still being paid off in levies by people who feed him, and asks that Notebook 11 be filed nine years late.
  5. Hartley writes Ord's admission into Notebook 12 in daylight, dated, and strikes through her own earlier note calling him *a tenant, name unknown*.
  6. Rosa Vane reads the sheet at the kitchen table, finds no acreage on it that can be assessed, and countersigns Form 9 with the pen Hartley offers.
  7. Hartley writes a covering note to Arthur Penn listing, in numbered points, what the sheet does not claim.
  8. With the sheet still on her table she walks to Stone Three in the last of the daylight, takes one reading, and finds it identical to the figure she drew that morning — the valley holding, as R1 promises, until nightfall.
  9. She carries sheet, Form 9, Notebook 12 and Notebook 11 down to the track end, hands them to the carrier, and watches the bundle go over the ridge while there is still light on it.
  10. After dark the ground lets go of everything the sheet declined to name, and Hartley — inside the valley, with nothing left to measure until morning — writes the time, the weather, and nothing else.
