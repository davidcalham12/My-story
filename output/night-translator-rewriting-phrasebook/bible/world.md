# The Post at Kolsa Pass

Kolsa Pass is the highest staffed crossing on the frontier: a block building, a barrier arm, a stove lit from October. Traffic stops after dark; the post does not close, because a crossing that closes has moved. Night shift is one duty officer and one translator. What happens here happens in writing.

## Factions

- **The Ministry of Interior Language Service** — wants the frontier to speak in approved words, so no traveller's answer can later be disputed. It issues and numbers the phrasebook, licenses translators, and requires every counter question to be read from the page.
- **The post's night staff** — want the shift to end cleanly, ledger closed, nothing raised to the district. They settle irregularities among themselves: a form recopied, a discrepancy unlogged, an oddity told to a colleague and never above.

## Means

- **The phrasebook** — a blue cloth-bound book of 212 numbered pages, issued by the Language Service, chained to the counter. Left column the state's language, right column the traveller's. Only the duty translator may hold it; only the Service may print in it. Officially the contents are fixed and identical in every copy; any reported change is a reader's error.
- **The night ledger** — the bound register of every crossing: time, name, purpose, phrase numbers used. Corrections struck once and initialled.
- **Form 9** — the only channel for reporting that an issued document is wrong. It goes to the district office and returns countersigned, or not at all.

## Rules

- **R1.** The phrasebook stays chained to the counter and may be held only by the translator on duty; a traveller who touches it is the duty officer's business.
- **R2.** A translator may say to a traveller only what is printed in the phrasebook, read with its page and phrase number; anything else goes in the ledger as an unlisted utterance.
- **R3.** No one at the post may write in the phrasebook. Marks, corrections and annotations are defacement of state property.
- **R4.** Every crossing is entered in the night ledger before the barrier lifts, in ink, and no page is torn out.
- **R5.** A change observed in an issued document exists officially only once Form 9 is filed and countersigned; until then the document reads as printed.

## Texture

Cold at the ankles. One strip light, the barrier's motor, wind on block. Hours of nothing, then headlights and four minutes of procedure. Language here is a numbered inventory, and being spoken to in it feels like being counted.
