# Characters

- **Irena Vasko** — night translator at Kolsa Pass for eleven years and the only person licensed to hold the chained phrasebook; keeps a private count of everything she is not allowed to say aloud, speaks to travellers only in page-and-phrase-number and finds her own unnumbered sentences physically difficult to produce
- **Tomas Brandt** — night duty officer on the same shift for those eleven years; settles irregularities below the district and would rather recopy a form than raise one, yet logs every unlisted utterance of Vasko's exactly as R2 requires because he believes accuracy is a kindness
- **Ana Petrik** — traveller who crosses the barrier three times in the night of the story with a valid card and a purpose that does not change; answers the counter questions patiently and in full each time as though the repetition were the point
- **Dora Lenk** — the night translator at Kolsa before Vasko; present in the book only as struck-and-initialled corrections in an old ledger volume and as a Form 9 that went to the district and never came back countersigned

## Register notes (describe; do not copy wording)

- **Vasko** reads aloud in the flat cadence of numbered text and speaks in her own words only in short reluctant fragments; her interior voice is dry and exact and counts things.
- **Brandt** talks in procedure and small domestic complaints about the stove; he softens bad news by describing the paperwork it will cause rather than the thing itself.
- **Petrik** is courteous, unhurried, faintly amused by the formality, and answers more than she is asked.
- **Lenk** is heard only through handwriting: initials, a struck line, the pressure of a pen.
