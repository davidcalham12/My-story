# Mysteries

- **What is the phrasebook doing — who is putting new phrases into a book nobody at the post may write in?**
  - True: the phrasebook is compiling. The Language Service builds approved phrasing out of the unlisted-utterance entries that every post's ledger returns each quarter; Kolsa's returns are unusually complete because Brandt logs Vasko honestly where other officers do not, so across eleven years Vasko has become the frontier's largest single source of unapproved speech. The new phrases are her own sentences, normalised, numbered and printed back at her. No one at the post writes in the book and no rule is broken — R3 holds, R4 holds, and the mechanism is a returns cycle, not an ink that moves.
  - Planted: Chapter 1, where Vasko reads a phrase off a page she has known for eleven years, notices its cadence is a cadence she recognises before she recognises the words, and enters the crossing without mentioning it.
  - Lands: Chapter 2, where she opens Lenk's old ledger volume beside the open phrasebook and matches printed, numbered right-column phrases against unlisted utterances logged in Brandt's hand with dates.

- **Why are the new phrases always about her, and what does being printed do to her?**
  - True: the compiled phrases come back inverted. What she said as the counter's voice is reissued as the traveller's column, so the book now supplies the words for questioning the person who holds it. By page 212 her name is set as an approved term — that is, she has become a numbered item of the inventory rather than its reader, and under R5 a document reads as printed until Form 9 returns countersigned. She files Form 9. It does not return within the book. She is therefore, officially and for as long as the story lasts, what the phrasebook says she is, and she works the rest of the shift anyway.
  - Planted: Chapter 1, where the changed phrase is in the right-hand column and addresses the person behind the counter rather than the one at the window.
  - Lands: Chapter 3, where she finds her own name printed as approved usage, fills out Form 9, watches it go into the district bag, and lifts the barrier for the night's last crossing.

- **What happened to Dora Lenk?**
  - True: eleven years ago Lenk filed Form 9 about the same changes, was never sent a countersigned return, and was transferred to a post that appears on no district list. Her name now stands on page 212 as an approved term of address, one line above where Vasko's appears. The book does not explain what a transfer to an unlisted post is, and Vasko never learns; what is canon is only that the Service absorbed the report into the inventory instead of answering it, and that filing Form 9 is therefore the last thing Lenk is recorded doing.
  - Planted: Chapter 2, where Vasko takes the old ledger volume from the cupboard and finds struck corrections in another hand, initialled, ending mid-quarter.
  - Lands: Chapter 3, where page 212 carries Lenk's name as printed usage and the district's non-answer is the only account of her the post will ever hold.
