# Chapter 1 — Page Seventy-Four, Phrase Six

At 01:40 the stove pipe was the warmest thing in the building, and Irena Vasko stood with her palms half an inch off it, close enough to feel the heat lift the fine hairs on her wrists.

"It shuts," Brandt said. "It shuts, and then it opens again on its own, and I am not permitted to wire it, because wiring it is a modification." He toed the damper. It rang. "A modification requires a request."

Light came up the pass then, two cones swinging across the block wall, and Brandt was at the ledger before the engine was audible. He uncapped the pen. He wrote the time in ink, the figures square and unhurried, and only then put his hand to the barrier control. Eleven years, and she had never once seen him do it in the other order.

The card came through the window in a gloved hand. Valid, current, the photograph a younger version of the woman now bending to the gap. Vasko drew the chained phrasebook towards her by its cloth spine, the links sliding on the counter, and opened it flat.

"Page seventy-four, phrase one." She read the left column, then the right. Purpose of travel.

"Collection," the woman said. "Ana Petrik. There's a consignment at the depot on the far side and I've been told it's been sitting since Thursday, which I doubt, but I'm told it anyway."

"Page seventy-four, phrase three. Expected duration."

"Two hours there, two back, longer if the road has iced. Call it five." Petrik smiled at the numbering, the way people did, as though it were a game the post had invented for its own amusement.

"Page seventy-four, phrase four. Goods carried."

"Nothing outbound. Paper in the glovebox."

Vasko's eye went down the column to six, and stopped before it read.

She knew the shape of it first. The cadence — the particular short lift at the end, the hesitation built into a clause — was one she recognised the way you recognise a footstep on stairs. She had never read this phrase aloud. It was not on this page. It was on this page now, set in the right-hand column, the traveller's column, and it was phrased to ask something of the person standing behind the counter.

Nothing else was printed there for her to say.

"Page seventy-four, phrase six."

Petrik answered it fully, and a little more than asked, and did not seem to find it strange.

Brandt wrote the numbers into the ledger. The barrier went up, and the tail lights pulled away, and Vasko watched them until the pass took them, and added it to the count of what she was not allowed to say aloud.
