# Chapter 2 — The Cupboard Volume

At 03:10 the headlights came up out of the dark the way they had gone down into it, and Ana Petrik put the same card on the counter with the same unchanged purpose written on it in the same hand.

Vasko turned to page seventy-four. Four more phrases in the right-hand column that had not been there, and not only on seventy-four: on eighty-one, on ninety-three, on pages she could have set out from memory. Every one of them addressed the person holding the book. She counted them privately and read them as printed, page and phrase number first, in the flat cadence the licence required.

Petrik answered each in full. She answered the repeated ones too, patiently, unhurried, as though repetition were not an error but the whole of the exercise. Brandt wrote the time, the name, the purpose and the phrase numbers in ink, blotted it, and only then put his hand to the barrier control. The arm went up. The engine noise thinned out along the pass and the post went quiet again, with the wind coming across the block wall in long unbroken pushes.

Vasko opened the counter cupboard. The ledger volumes stood in there in their order, and she took out one from four years back and laid it open beside the chained phrasebook, which stayed on its chain, as it did.

Unlisted utterances, in Brandt's square careful hand, dated. *Translator stated, off page: it is not far now.* She found it printed in the traveller's column of page ninety-three, numbered, tidied — *the distance remaining is small* — the shape of it kept, the roughness taken out. She went back a volume and found another. Then four more. Eleven years of things she had not been able to stop herself saying, standing in the book as approved phrasing for questioning her.

Deeper in the oldest volume the hand changed. Another writer altogether: struck corrections, one line through each, initials pressed so hard the paper had given under them. The entries ran on and then stopped mid-quarter, no closing entry, nothing signed off.

Brandt spoke from the stove without turning round. The quarterly returns went down to the district in the bag, he said, and came back as reissued phrasing. That was how it had always worked. He said the damper wanted seeing to.

No one had written in the book. Nobody here had touched it. She had fed it, accurately, every quarter for eleven years, because Brandt logged her honestly, and Brandt logged her honestly because he believed accuracy was a kindness. She closed the volume and counted that, too.
