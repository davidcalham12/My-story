# Chapter 3 — Page Two Hundred and Twelve

The stove had gone down to embers and the road held nothing. Vasko turned the phrasebook to its last page because there was nowhere else left in it to turn.

Page 212 was headed *Approved Terms of Address*, and the terms were numbered like everything else. She read down the column with her finger not touching the paper. 212.4: *Lenk.* 212.5: *Vasko.*

She counted the entries above and below. Nine in all. Two of them were people she could name.

"Brandt."

He looked up from the stove door.

"The book." Her own words came out the way they always did, short, costing more than they carried. "My name is in it. Printed. As a term of address."

He was quiet a moment. Then: "That's a Form 9. There's no other form for it." He went to the drawer. "It's filed here, it goes down in the bag with the quarterly returns, and it comes back countersigned by the district. Or it doesn't." He set the blank on the counter beside her, square to the edge. He did not offer to recopy it. He did not suggest they settle it between them.

She filled it in in ink. *Nature of irregularity: an issued document reads other than as printed.* Under the old ledger volume in the cupboard there was a carbon of the same form, eleven years soft at the folds, the hand on it small and even, and Vasko understood while her pen moved that the district had never written back, and that its not writing back was the whole of what the post would ever hold of Dora Lenk. There was nothing else. There was a name on page 212, one line above her own.

Headlights came up the pass at twenty past five.

Same card, same purpose, patiently given. Brandt entered the crossing in the night ledger, time and name and purpose, in ink, before he touched the barrel switch. Vasko put her hand flat on the open book and read the counter questions from page 212, by number, in the flat cadence, including the ones that began with her own name set as the approved way of addressing the woman holding the book. Petrik answered all of them, and more than all of them, and seemed to find the repetition the point.

The barrier lifted. The tail lights went over the crest.

Vasko folded the Form 9 into the district bag with the quarterly returns and drew the cord. Brandt brought the barrier down. She worked the last hour of the shift, and kept the count.
