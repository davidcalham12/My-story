## Promises
- Who is putting new phrases into a book that nobody at the post is permitted to write in, and by what means, if no rule has been broken?
- Why do the changed phrases keep turning toward the woman who holds the book, and what happens to her once she is printed?
- What became of Dora Lenk, the translator before, whose only remaining trace is a struck correction and a Form 9 that never came back?

## Chapters

### Chapter 1 — Page Seventy-Four, Phrase Six
- **POV:** Irena Vasko
- **Tension:** 4/10
- **Promise advanced:** Who is putting new phrases into the book; why the phrases turn toward the holder
- **Beats:**
  1. At the counter at 01:40, Vasko warms her hands on the stove pipe while Brandt complains about the damper, and headlights come up the pass; Brandt opens the night ledger and takes down the time in ink before touching the barrier control.
  2. Ana Petrik hands in a valid card at the window, and Vasko reads the counter questions off the chained phrasebook by page and phrase number, stating purpose of travel, expected duration, goods carried; Petrik answers each one fully and a little more than asked, amused at the formality.
  3. Turning to page seventy-four, a page Vasko has worked from for eleven years, she finds a phrase at position six whose cadence she recognises before she reads the words — it is not one she has ever read aloud, and it sits in the right-hand column, the traveller's column, phrased to question the person standing behind the counter.
  4. She reads it anyway because R2 leaves her nothing else to say, Petrik answers it, and Brandt writes the phrase numbers into the ledger and lifts the barrier; Vasko watches the tail lights go and does not tell him, adding the thing to the private count of what she is not allowed to say aloud.

### Chapter 2 — The Cupboard Volume
- **POV:** Irena Vasko
- **Tension:** 7/10
- **Promise advanced:** Who is putting new phrases into the book (lands); what became of Dora Lenk (planted)
- **Beats:**
  1. At 03:10 Petrik's headlights return inbound, the same card, the same unchanged purpose, and Vasko finds four more phrases altered in the right-hand column of pages she knows, every one of them addressed to the person holding the book rather than the person at the window.
  2. She reads them, Petrik answers them patiently as though the repetition were the point, Brandt enters the crossing in ink and lifts the barrier, and the post goes quiet again with the wind on the block wall.
  3. Vasko opens the counter cupboard and takes out the old ledger volume that predates her, lays it beside the chained phrasebook, and finds pages of unlisted utterances in Brandt's hand — her own sentences, dated, the ones she could not keep from saying across eleven years — now standing printed and numbered in the traveller's column, tidied into approved phrasing.
  4. Deeper in the volume the handwriting changes to another hand entirely: struck corrections, single lines, initials pressed hard into the paper, ending mid-quarter with no closing entry; Brandt, looking over her shoulder at the stove, says only that the quarterly returns go down to the district in the bag and come back as reissued phrasing, and that this is how it has always worked.
  5. Vasko understands that no one has written in the book, that R3 and R4 hold entirely, and that the mechanism is a returns cycle she has been feeding accurately for eleven years because Brandt believed accuracy was a kindness.

### Chapter 3 — Page Two Hundred and Twelve
- **POV:** Irena Vasko
- **Tension:** 6/10
- **Promise advanced:** Why the phrases turn toward the holder (lands); what became of Dora Lenk (lands)
- **Beats:**
  1. Before dawn, with the stove down to embers and nothing on the road, Vasko turns the phrasebook to its last page and finds page 212 set as approved terms of address, and on it, one line above another, the name Dora Lenk and then her own — numbered, printed, an item of the inventory rather than its reader.
  2. She tells Brandt in her own unnumbered words, which come out short and reluctant, and he answers by describing the paperwork rather than the thing: Form 9, filed and sent down, countersigned or not at all; he fetches the blank, lets her fill it in, and does not offer to recopy anything or settle it below the district.
  3. She fills out Form 9 in ink, reporting that an issued document reads other than as printed, and understands while writing that Lenk filed this same form eleven years ago and that the district's silence is the entire account of her the post will ever hold.
  4. Headlights: Petrik's third crossing, outbound again, same card, same purpose; Brandt enters it in the night ledger in ink before the barrier lifts, and Vasko reads the counter questions from the page exactly as printed, her own name among them, because until Form 9 returns countersigned the book reads as printed and so does she.
  5. The form goes into the district bag with the quarterly returns, the barrier comes down, and Vasko works the last hour of the shift, keeping the count.
