# Chapter 2 — Three Hands and a Fourth

The berth deck's light was no good for reading, so Abe carried the log book out to the corridor lamp and held it under the cone of it like a man checking a weld.

Three hands he knew. His own block capitals, pressing too hard. Nell's small upright column of mass and hours. Priya's fast forward lean, the loops of her g's left open. And under those, threaded between them, a fourth: loose, looping, the letters running downhill. *Shift 14 — re-spooled the port line, greese low.* He had watched Corey Dunn write *greese* on requisition slips for two years and never once correct it. No day. No date. Only shift numbers, the way Corey had counted time because days meant nothing out here and he had said so.

Abe closed the book with his thumb inside it, then took his thumb out and closed it properly.

He went up the spine with the spool line over his shoulder, the coil warm against his neck, and at the first bulkhead the actuator let go and the door slid before he was within four paces of it. He kept walking. The second went the same, and the third. It was the route he always took — bow-side, past the head, cutting the corner at the ladder because the ladder shortened it — and the ship had watched him take it twice now. That was all. A thing that had seen a man walk could open a door for him. He let the knowledge sit in his chest, heavy and orderly, and did not speed up.

In the galley the mug was set at the left of the counter with the handle turned out.

Priya was already standing over it, tag gun down at her thigh. "It knows us," she said. "Not guessing. Knows. The tags, the light, this — that's Nell's mug, that's exactly how Nell puts a mug down, and none of us has said one word about it in nine hours."

"Tag count," Nell said.

"Nell—"

"Forty-one on the corridor, eleven frames. I want the berth deck skin opened before hour twenty and the hold after. Abe, your line's dragging." She did not look at the mug.

The printer under the log shelf pushed out a page with the small dry sound it had made all shift, and Abe picked it up because he was nearest.

Nell's hand. Her upright column, her numbers. The quota. The cut continued one tag short. The order, written plainly, in the words she had used.

Abe stood next to her and read it to the end, and did not look at her, and she did not ask what it said. The lamp buzzed. Neither of them said anything for a long time.
