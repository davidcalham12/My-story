# Characters

The tug is the **Grebe**: four berths, three of them slept in. The fourth bunk tag
aboard the Grebe still reads **Corey Dunn**, fourteen months after he died, because
nobody has been willing to pull it. The derelict's fourth berth carries the same
name in the same place.

Canon on the crew's shared limit: none of them can check anything the derelict
writes against an outside record (R4), and none of them will say the obvious thing
first (Texture).

- **Nell Varga** — tug master; files the claims, keeps the mass quota, exacting and quietly superstitious
  - **Wants:** a clean claim, a beacon on the hull, and the fee logged before anybody asks her what the log books say.
  - **Fears:** that the one thing she has only ever said aloud to an empty galley is now in print in her own handwriting, and that Abe will read it before she can cut the page out.
  - **Speaks:** in work-orders and numbers — mass, hours, tag counts. Short declaratives, no hedging, no metaphor. When she is frightened she gets more procedural, not less, and gives an instruction instead of an answer. She does not ask questions she cannot stand the answer to.

- **Abe Whitlock** — cutter; runs the torch, the spool line and the rig, slow-spoken and stubborn
  - **Wants:** to cut the hull down in the order he planned it, finish the shift, and be paid by mass like every other job.
  - **Fears:** that the rules he works by — a cut stays cut, nothing is salvage until it is tagged — stop being enough to stand on; and that he was the one holding the line the day Corey went.
  - **Speaks:** long pauses, flat delivery, mostly about equipment and sequence. Makes his arguments by describing what he is going to do next rather than by disagreeing. Says Corey's name plainly when the others won't, and lets it sit.

- **Priya Sen** — engineer and tagger; youngest aboard, quick, unwilling to pretend
  - **Wants:** to name what the derelict is doing out loud, in front of both of them, and be told she is not imagining it.
  - **Fears:** being the only one who keeps a record — that she will end up the crew's sole witness to something no relay will ever confirm.
  - **Speaks:** fast, concrete, testing. Thinks by listing observations and asking what would prove it. Uses the tag gun's numbers the way other people use punctuation. When the others go quiet she fills the silence, then hears herself and stops.

- **Corey Dunn** — dead crewman; present only as a bunk tag, a handwriting sample and a hole in the conversation
  - **Wants:** nothing; he is dead fourteen months and the book does not resurrect him. What persists is the crew's habit of leaving his tag, his mug and his berth where they were.
  - **Fears:** n/a — but he is the pressure point for all three living crew, and the derelict handles his name the way a person handles a thing they know is fragile.
  - **Speaks:** never in the present. His register exists only in documents — a loose, looping hand, misspellings he never corrected, the habit of dating entries by shift number instead of day. Any writing in that hand is canon-Corey and must never be dialogue.
