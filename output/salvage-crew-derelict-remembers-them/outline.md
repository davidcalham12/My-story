## Promises

1. **How can a hull that has drifted longer than any of them have been alive already know their names?** — The bunk tags are right before anyone speaks aboard the wreck. The book will show the reader exactly where the knowing stops, and will never say where it came from.
2. **Whose name is on the fourth bunk tag, and what did Nell Varga leave out of the filing?** — Corey Dunn is dead fourteen months and still berthed. The thing Nell said only to an empty galley is aboard a ship she has never boarded, in her own handwriting, and Abe Whitlock is standing next to her when it prints.
3. **Do they cut it up and file the claim anyway?** — They are paid by mass and fined for walking away. The question is not whether they finish the job but what the job costs them to finish, and what they destroy on the way out.

## Chapters

### Chapter 1 — Right Berths
- **POV:** Priya Sen
- **Tension:** 5/10
- **Promise advanced:** Promise 1 (planted), Promise 2 (planted), Promise 3 (planted)
- **Beats:**
  1. The Grebe hooks an unpowered hull at the edge of the drift — decades cold, no beacon, four berths laid out exactly like their own — and Priya Sen reads the drift figures aloud and says the hull has been out here longer than any of them have been alive.
  2. In the berth deck Priya finds four bunk tags in the right berths: Varga, Whitlock, Sen. Nobody has spoken a word aboard the wreck yet.
  3. The fourth tag reads Dunn. None of the three touches it; Abe Whitlock says Corey Dunn's name plainly, out loud, and lets it sit while the other two look at the bulkhead.
  4. Abe is already rigging the torch before the tags are finished being read — he talks through his cutting order instead of arguing about the tags — and Priya shoots the first numbered tag into a bulkhead plate, because nothing is salvage until it is tagged.
  5. A cabin light comes up over a berth nobody is standing in. Nell Varga gives the hours remaining on the shift and tells them to keep cutting.

### Chapter 2 — Three Hands and a Fourth
- **POV:** Abe Whitlock
- **Tension:** 8/10
- **Promise advanced:** Promise 2 (lands)
- **Beats:**
  1. Abe carries a log book out to better light and finds it written in three hands he recognises as his own, Nell's and Priya's — and a fourth, loose and looping, misspellings uncorrected, entries dated by shift number instead of day.
  2. Walking the spine corridor with the spool line over his shoulder, Abe watches the door actuators open ahead of him, one compartment at a time, before he reaches them.
  3. In the derelict's galley a mug sits set down at the left of the counter with the handle turned out — where Nell sets hers — and Priya says out loud what all three of them have been working around; Nell answers with a work-order and a tag count.
  4. A printed page comes up in the log in Nell's hand: the quota, the cut continued one tag short, the order given. Abe reads it standing next to her and does not look at her, and neither of them says anything for a long time.

### Chapter 3 — Blank Paper
- **POV:** Nell Varga
- **Tension:** 6/10 rising to 9/10
- **Promise advanced:** Promise 1 (lands), Promise 3 (lands)
- **Beats:**
  1. Hour twenty-six. Nell files the claim on the tug's terminal and sends Priya out with the beacon, because walking away forfeits the fee and draws a fine, and she says so in those words rather than any other.
  2. Back aboard the wreck, Nell tests the limit: she writes out by hand a question about something that was never said or done aboard either ship, and leaves the book open.
  3. The page comes back blank — not a lie, not a refusal, just blank paper — and Nell closes the book without telling either of them what she asked.
  4. She gives Abe the page reference and the instruction, and Abe puts the torch through the page carrying her confession. It stays cut. Priya watches and writes the tag number down in her own record instead of saying anything.
  5. The Grebe undocks with the mass, the claim filed and the beacon lit behind them; the relay's answer is nine days out and Nell logs the hours worked.
