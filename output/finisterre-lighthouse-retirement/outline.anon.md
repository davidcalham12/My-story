# Outline — Finisterre Lighthouse Retirement

## Acts

The first Sunday after his retirement, [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] moves through the rituals that have held his life: the morning climb to the lighthouse, the tending of the garden, the gathering at the family table, and the passing of the key. Each ritual carries the weight of forty-four years, and in this single day of endings and continuations, he learns what it means to belong to a place and a people beyond the measure of duty.

## Promises to the reader

- **Q1.** Why does [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] still climb the lighthouse steps the morning after his retirement?
- **Q2.** Why is the Sunday joke about the octopus and the accountant told in exactly the same words, decade after decade?
- **Q3.** What does [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] mean to do with the lighthouse key?
- **Q4.** Why does [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] save seeds from the tomatoes in labelled jars, year after year?

### Chapter 1 — The Key

From before dawn to dusk on the Sunday his retirement watch formally ends, [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] climbs the tower, reads the storm in the log, tends the garden, tells the unchanged joke at the table, and at the lamp room hour, passes the key to Manuel.

**Beats:**

1. Before dawn, [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] wakes and climbs the ninety-two steps to the gallery and the fourteen more to the lamp room, following the rhythm his body has known since he was twenty-one, unthinking—then stops at the lamp room door and knows he will not need to come back. He winds the rotating optic by hand anyway, feeling the machinery respond, and stands in the pre-dawn dark watching the automated light turn.

2. In the keeper's office below the lamp room, [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] opens the watch log to write the morning entry. He turns back through decades of pages in his own careful handwriting—entries from October 1987 when the storm took the power lines and left him alone in the dark. He reads what he wrote then: factual, precise, unbothered. The memory sits in the ink, not to be spoken of.

3. In the greenhouse behind his house, [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] moves through the tomato plants in their late-season growth. He selects the ripest fruits, cuts seeds free, washes and dries them on cloth, and labels the glass jars in brown ink with variety and year. The work is methodical, practiced, the same as every year—his proof that he makes things grow.

4. At the Sunday table, with Rosa and Manuel seated, the meal prepared, [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] waits for the moment when the conversation settles into a familiar pause, then tells the joke about the octopus and the accountant in exactly the same words he has told it every Sunday for decades. The punchline lands as it always does; Manuel laughs; Rosa smiles; the ritual holds.

5. When the meal is finished, [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] and Manuel walk together up the lighthouse path at dusk. In the lamp room, as the rotating optic begins to turn in the gathering darkness, [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] removes the key to the lamp room door from where it hangs beside the lamp and places it in Manuel's hand. He does not speak. Manuel closes his hand around it and understands: this is the witnessing that someone kept the light.
