For forty-four years, Tomás tended the lighthouse at Cabo Fisterra, at the edge of the Costa da Morte where the Atlantic meets the last stone of Galicia. On the morning of his retirement in September 2026, the key to the lamp room passes to his son Manuel. But the habit does not leave him: the climb up the stone steps, the ninety-two to the gallery, the fourteen more to the light, the pull of the tower above the village that turns its back to the wind.

Rosa, his wife of forty years, has built a life within the life the lighthouse demanded. Manuel, born the year after the great Atlantic storm of October 1987, watches his father with the particularity of a man who has never understood the silence between them. That storm—when Tomás wound the rotating optic by hand through the night and held five fishing boats in his beam while the mainland could not reach him—lives unspoken in the house, a weight carried without words.

In the greenhouse behind his stone house, Tomás saves seed from his tomatoes in labelled jars, year after year. And now, with the watch formally ended, these small, precise habits of a lifetime take on a different weight. This is a novel about the quiet work of love, about lighthouses and storms, about what we keep and what we carry in silence.

For readers of *The Light Between Oceans*, *The Remains of the Day* and *Beside the Ocean of Time*.
