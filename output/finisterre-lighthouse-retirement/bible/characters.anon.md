# Characters

- **[NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO]** — Retired Lighthouse Keeper, 65; duty, precision, quietness

Kept the lamp burning at Finisterre for 44 years without complaint. His life was the lighthouse, measured in watches, log entries, winds of the rotating optic. Now retired and learning what it means to have a day that does not begin with the climb. What he wants: the light to still burn, someone to remember the keeping. What he will not say: anything true about the October night in 1987 when the storm took the power lines and left him alone in the dark. How he speaks: Rarely, always as fact. At the Sunday table, the octopus joke, unchanged in forty years. He is the center; everyone else is arranged around what he does.

- **Rosa** — [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO]'s Wife, 62; steadiness, knowing, wry

Married him when he was the young keeper who would not smile, when she was 22. Has built a life inside the life the lighthouse demanded: seasons of the garden, the rhythm of the Sunday table, the careful art of saying nothing about the things he does not say. What she wants: the continuation of routine, the knowledge that his retirement will not unmake the home she built. What she will not say: how many times she waited on the stone steps for him to come down from the tower. How she speaks: Warmly and directly, with the practicality of a woman who has learned to say important things quickly. The anchor of the household; she knows him in all his silence.

- **Manuel** — Adult Son, 38; observant, careful, dutiful

Born after the great storm. Works away from the coast, building a life at distance from the lighthouse. Comes home to the Sunday table and watches his father with the particularity of a son who has never quite understood the silence, and loves him anyway. He is the one buying this story as a gift. What he wants: to know his father through the quiet act of giving him a book about his own life. To understand what the keeping meant. What he will not say: how much his own distance is built on the weight of his father's attachment. How he speaks: More freely than his father, but with a caution learned from a childhood of listening to what was not said. The son who bridges the lighthouse world and the world beyond.

- **Xabier** — Fisherman and Village Elder, 52; weather-wise, direct, loyal

Born to the sea and the village. Has known [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] for forty-four years, since boyhood. Watched him keep the light through the great storm and has never forgotten it. What he wants: the light to stay burning, the village to hold together, the old ways to mean something. What he will not say: how afraid he was in 1987, how close the village came to losing boats. How he speaks: In stories when he can, in few words when he must. The voice of the village; he knows what people think before they think it. A friend and witness to the keeper.

- **Carmen** — Village Shopkeeper, 58; observant, practical, perceptive

Sold bread and milk and news from behind her counter for thirty-five years. Knows every person in the village and what they do not tell. Has watched [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] buy the same supplies in the same order for thirty-five years, since she opened the shop. Old enough friends with Rosa that certain conversations have happened in the spaces between other conversations. What she wants: the village to continue, the lighthouse to stand. What she will not say: that she knows more about the 1987 storm than anyone but [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO], but she will not tell. How she speaks: Practically, with the dark humor of a woman who reads what people do not say. The keeper of village memory and unspoken knowledge.

- **Carlos** — Lighthouse Service Inspector, 48; bureaucratic, well-meaning, skeptical

Visits every six months from the mainland to check the automation, the logs, the light. Has known [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] for twenty years, long enough to respect him but not long enough to understand what the light meant before the machines. What he wants: the light maintained, the automation working, regulations satisfied, the old keeper finally at rest. What he will not say: that he admires [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO]'s refusal to retire, though it has been an administrative difficulty. How he speaks: In the language of procedures and documents, with occasional moments of genuine warmth. The official from away, who does not understand that some things do not retire.
