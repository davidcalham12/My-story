# Outline — Baruna Reach

## Acts

- **Act I — Chapter 1.** The arithmetic. Nayla Wiryawan reads a free scene index over the induction link and finds four days of her own life itemised under a stranger's name; the cable maths fits four clinic callbacks logged in her own handwriting. Inciting fact established, ninety-day clock started.
- **Act II — Chapter 2.** The paperwork. Tower-side, dry, quiet. An honest bench assay contradicts a certificate, the provenance chain resolves to a debt at Estuary Seven, and the buyer who wears Lot 4417 learns what is decaying inside her skull. Deliberate trough: nobody raises their voice, everything gets worse.
- **Act III — Chapter 3.** The skull. Night, rationed power, stated battery capacity. Seventy-two seconds of cable against a 04:00 overwrite; the fragment comes out assayed and witnessed, and the hand that opened the port is named by the person who taught Nayla to log it.

## Promises

- **P1 — The four hours forty-eight minutes.** Twenty-four recorded hours is 8,640 MB of unbroken cable. Who held Nayla Wiryawan's port open that long, in a life where she was never out of a chair for more than seventy-eight minutes? *Asked Ch 1, circled Ch 2, paid off Ch 3.*
- **P2 — The ninety-day edge.** Her own implant has already overwritten what was taken. One 36 MB fragment from Day −88 survives and expires at the 04:00 pump siren on Day +2. Can she get it out of her head in seventy-two seconds of cable, with the rig above the off-grid ceiling? *Asked Ch 1, paid off Ch 3.*
- **P3 — Generation 1, detail 0.94.** The certificate on Lot 4417 says first-hand. Why does a calibrated field assay read generation 2 at 0.88, and whose connectome accounts for the missing transcode? *Asked Ch 1, paid off Ch 2.*
- **P4 — The debt at Estuary Seven.** A courier boat lost 194 GB of client archive in the Ledge channel. What did Oskar Maas accept in place of drive stock, and who on that pontoon was in a position to pay him in first-generation trace? *Asked Ch 1, paid off Ch 2, named Ch 3.*
- **P5 — Juno Halim's forty-one days.** Forged agency markers rated for thirty to sixty days, and the lot is already thinning into somebody else's. When it re-flags inside her own skull, does she surrender the certificate or spend it first? *Asked Ch 2, paid off Ch 3.*

## Chapters

### Chapter 1 — Four Days, Itemised

- **POV:** Nayla Wiryawan — Estuary Seven pontoon clinic, eastern districts, Day 0, morning, tide coming up the stairwell two flights below
- **Setting:** The chair, the assay bench with its certificate taped to the housing, the induction link running at 9 kb/s — text only, no images, no preview
- **Tension:** 6/10
- **Promise advanced:** P1, P2, P3, P4 (all opened; P1 and P2 driven hardest)
- **Beats:**
  - Between clients she pulls the Ninth Floor Exchange's free scene index down the induction link to price a competitor's stock, and reads four days she recognises: a dawn on the Ledge channel, a burn on her left wrist, the specific order in which she solders a mastoid lead — itemised under the originator name Juno Halim, certificate generation 1, detail 0.94.
  - She does the arithmetic aloud because she cannot leave a number wrong: twenty-four recorded hours is 8,640 MB, 0.5 MB/s, four hours and forty-eight minutes of unbroken cable — and she has never in nine years been out of a chair that long at a stretch.
  - She opens the clinic's paper log and finds it broken into four: Days −117, −108, −99, −88, each entered "thermal-fault callback, 78 min chair, 4 × 90 s hard-shut, 72 min cable, no charge," each in her own handwriting, each a date she remembers arriving at and not leaving.
  - She queries her own Lattice index and finds nothing before Day −90 — rolling overwrite, ninety days — except one 36 MB salience fragment stamped Day −88: cable tension behind the ear, a rig fan, a pair of hands. She calculates its export at 72 seconds of cable and its expiry at the 04:00 siren on Day +2.
  - Ilham Basri comes aboard with fuel cells he should not have been able to afford and asks, mildly, what she is counting. She says the tide. She does not show him the log.
- **Must establish:** That memory is legal to sell and only the unconsented port is a crime; that Nayla's proof exists solely as a 36 MB fragment with a stated expiry; that the clinic log incriminates her own handwriting, which means someone with her trust wrote the cover story.
- **Must leave open:** Who paid, who cut, and whether the fragment can survive its own deadline.

### Chapter 2 — The Bench Says Two

- **POV:** Juno Halim — Ledge Tower, tower-side, dry floors, mid-afternoon Day +1
- **Setting:** A buyer's room above the waterline; Ratri Sundoro's calibration bench open on the table; Oskar Maas present, courteous, with a provenance folder
- **Tension:** 4/10 (deliberate trough — low voices, contractual language, catastrophic content)
- **Promise advanced:** P3 and P4 (paid off), P5 (opened), P1 (narrowed)
- **Beats:**
  - Juno has called the assay herself, because for eleven days she has known how to splice a mastoid lead without ever having learned it, and this week the knowledge has stopped feeling like hers — she can state the burn on the left wrist as a fact and cannot find the pain in it.
  - Ratri Sundoro runs the bench on Lot 4417 and reads generation 2, detail 0.88, to within one percent, and says so flatly: the file has passed through one more connectome than the certificate admits, and the agency markers are forged and forty-one days into a thirty-to-sixty-day decay.
  - Oskar Maas, asked directly, does not lie — it is not illegal. He opened the provenance file under a buyer's name rather than a seller's because there was no seller willing to be one, and he took first-generation trace on two courier drives at the dock on Day −86 in settlement of 194 GB of drive stock lost in the Ledge channel, owed by a technician at Estuary Seven.
  - Nayla Wiryawan is announced downstairs, having come up out of the water district with a paper log and no certificate, and Ratri agrees to stay only because a generation number is at stake and not because a life is.
  - Left alone with the certificate — generation 1, detail 0.94, originator Halim, worth more than the memory it describes — Juno does not hand it over. She puts it in her coat.
- **Must establish:** The missing transcode is inside Juno's own skull; Maas's settlement points unambiguously back to Estuary Seven and to a debt only Ilham Basri carried; the honest number (2 / 0.88) now exists on a calibrated bench with a witness.
- **Must leave open:** What Juno intends to do with the certificate, and whether the 36 MB fragment can be got out before the siren.

### Chapter 3 — Seventy-Two Seconds of Cable

- **POV:** Nayla Wiryawan — Estuary Seven pontoon clinic, Day +1 22:00 through Day +2 04:00, power ration on, everything off-grid
- **Setting:** Batteries of stated capacity, the 500 W ceiling, a transcode rig that draws 900 W and therefore cannot be used; the export head and Ratri's bench, together, under the ceiling
- **Tension:** 9/10
- **Promise advanced:** P1 and P2 (paid off), P4 (named), P5 (resolved)
- **Beats:**
  - Ratri Sundoro sets her calibration bench on the pontoon at 22:00 and states the terms: she will certify the fragment's generation and hour, nothing more — an assay cannot say whose life a memory came from, only how many brains it has crossed, so the fragment has to be generation 1 out of Nayla's own port or it proves nothing.
  - Ilham Basri arrives to stop it and instead explains it, because he is meticulous about cover stories and has run out of one: he kept her connectome map from the day he fitted her at nineteen; he wrote the thermal-fault protocol; he sedated her four times, seventy-eight minutes of chair and seventy-two of cable each, and copied her handwriting into the log because hers was the only hand the log ever held. He owed Maas 194 GB and had nothing else at first generation to pay with.
  - A courier boat comes alongside at tidal speed with Juno Halim's certificate aboard — surrendered, not spent — and Juno with it, because the markers have finished decaying and she would rather be nobody than be Nayla; the certificate's claim of generation 1 now contradicts Ratri's bench at generation 2, which makes it fraud in the Reach and puts Maas's provenance file into an evidentiary chain.
  - At 03:57 Nayla plugs her own mastoid port, alone, with Ratri reading and Ilham watching, and exports 36 MB in seventy-two seconds: cable tension behind the ear, a rig fan, and Ilham Basri's hands, timestamped Day −88, assayed generation 1. The 04:00 siren fires and her Lattice overwrites the original mid-breath; everything before that hour is now somebody else's copy, and the only surviving instance of it is on a drive on Ratri's bench.
  - Ilham does not run, because the crime was never in the market. Nayla logs the transfer in her own handwriting — time, megabytes, seconds of cable, no charge — and this time it is true.
- **Must establish:** The port was opened by Ilham Basri, four times, without consent, to service a drive-stock debt to Oskar Maas; the fragment is out, certified, and beat the overwrite by three minutes; Juno Halim gave up the certificate and with it the only version of herself she had been sold.
- **Must leave open:** Nothing new. Only the cost — twenty-four hours of her life exist solely as someone else's second-generation copy, and no process restores what generation loss takes.

## Tension Curve

- Chapter 1 — 6/10. Cold arithmetic on a quiet morning; the horror is a number that fits, not a threat.
- Chapter 2 — 4/10. Trough. Dry floors, courteous men, a bench reading two decimal places; the room breathes while the ground gives way.
- Chapter 3 — 9/10. Rationed power, a stated battery, seventy-two seconds against a 04:00 siren, and a confession from the hands that fitted her.
