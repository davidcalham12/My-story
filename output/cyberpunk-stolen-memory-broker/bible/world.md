# Baruna Reach — World Bible

Baruna Reach is what remains of the north Java coast after two metres of sea rise and ninety years of groundwater subsidence: a megacity of eleven million living above its own drowned ground floor, on stilt decks, pontoon rafts and the upper storeys of towers whose lobbies are now tidal caves. Mean high water stands above the second storey. Everything moves by boat, catwalk or courier. The city's one growth industry is memory: since cortical implants became cheap enough to fit in a back-alley clinic, recorded experience has become a commodity that can be copied, graded, degraded and resold — and, because experience is not property under Reach law, resold legally. The story takes place across three chapters in the flooded eastern districts, where the clinics are, and in the dry towers of the exchange, where the money is.

## Factions

- **The Kanal Authority** — wants the pumps running and the population countable; gets it by owning the power ration, licensing every implant clinic above waterline, and treating unlicensed surgery as a utilities offence rather than a medical one.
- **The Ninth Floor Exchange** — wants a liquid, graded market in recorded experience; gets it by buying first-generation rips at the dock, grading them by measured fidelity, and refusing to trade anything without an Attestation certificate, which makes the certificate worth more than the memory.
- **Attestation Mutual** — wants to be the only body whose fidelity assay anyone believes; gets it by running the calibration standards, publishing generation tables, and prosecuting forged provenance as fraud even though the underlying memory is legal to sell.
- **The Slipway Clinics** — an unlicensed technicians' trade, wants to keep fitting and servicing implants below the waterline; gets it by working from boats during the pump window, taking payment in fuel cells and drive stock, and never asking where a client's port has been.

## Technology

- **The Lattice** — a hippocampal-cortical implant that records the encoding pathway, not sight and sound: what comes back is the trace of an experience being laid down, re-run through the host's own sensory cortex. Cheap, common, fitted in a chair.
- **The Mastoid Port** — the only bulk data path in or out of a Lattice: a physical connector behind the ear, 4 Mb/s, requiring an unbroken cable. A slow induction link exists for indexes and text at 9 kb/s.
- **Transcode Rigs** — clinic hardware that re-fits a recorded trace from one connectome to another. Every rig is a lossy encoder; none is a copier.
- **The Attestation Assay** — a benchtop test that reads a memory file and returns its generation number and recoverable-detail score to within one percent. It cannot say who originated a memory, only how many brains it has passed through.
- **Courier Drives and the Reach Microwave** — line-of-sight relays over the flooded districts carry 20 Mb/s shared across forty thousand people. Anything larger than an index travels as a physical drive in a boat.

## Rules

- No wireless deep read. Bulk memory leaves a Lattice only through the mastoid port at 4 Mb/s, and one recorded hour is 360 MB — twelve minutes of unbroken cable. Theft requires a victim who is unconscious, restrained or paid, for a measurable and stateable length of time.
- Thermal ceiling. An implant may dissipate 40 mW before tissue rises 1 °C; past that it hard-shuts for ninety seconds. Nobody records continuously; the Lattice captures on salience triggers, averaging six hours of trace per day.
- Storage is 90 days, rolling. The implant overwrites its oldest trace. Any memory older than ninety days exists only as an exported copy in someone else's hands, and is therefore second-generation or worse. A full archive is 194 GB — four and a half days of continuous port time, so nobody steals a whole life in one sitting.
- Generation loss is monotonic. Each transcode into a new connectome costs 8–14% of recoverable sensory detail, and no process restores it. Fourth-generation material is a plot summary with weather in it. Prices follow the assay, not the story.
- Agency cannot be transcoded. A memory played in a foreign brain is known as fact but never felt as *mine*. Forged agency markers exist, cost more than the memory, and decay within 30–60 days, after which the file re-flags as somebody else's.
- Experience is not property. Selling a memory you did not originate is legal in the Reach; opening a port without consent is felony assault on tissue. Every case is proved at the skull, never in the market.
- Power is rationed 22:00–04:00 to the pump grid. During the window, nothing above 500 W runs off-grid, and a transcode rig draws 900 W. Night work happens on batteries with stated capacity, or not at all.
- No cloud. The microwave relays cannot move a memory file across the city in useful time; bulk data travels by courier boat, at tidal speed, and can be physically intercepted.

## Texture

The Reach smells of diesel, wet concrete and the chlorine they dose the pump outfalls with. Water is always two flights below and rising twice a day; you learn a building by its tide line. People wear their ports covered — a bared mastoid is an unlocked door — and check the time by the pump sirens. In the clinics, everything is secondhand and calibrated: drive stock, fuel cells, the assay bench with its certificate taped to the housing. The city runs on the quiet knowledge that anyone's past can be taken, copied, sold four times over, and handed back thinner than it went out — and that the law will only care whether the wire was plugged in without asking.
