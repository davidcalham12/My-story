# Chapter 2 — The Bench Says Two

The burn on Juno Halim's left wrist was a fact she owned the way she owned the room: four centimetres, pale, from a rig housing that ran hot because someone had bypassed the fan interlock. She knew the smell of it. She could not find the pain.

Ratri Sundoro set the calibration bench on the table and unrolled its certificate, taped to the housing, the way assayers did. "I'll take a sample, not the lot. Twenty-four recorded hours is four hours forty-eight minutes of cable and neither of us has that afternoon. Generation and detail come off six hundred megabytes as well as off the whole file."

"Then take the sample," Juno said.

The bench drew it across the mastoid lead at four megabits, and Juno sat still for it, because six weeks ago she had known how to splice such a lead without ever having been taught, and because that knowledge had begun this week to feel borrowed. Twenty minutes for the sample. Ratri watched the read like a woman watching a kettle.

"Generation two," she said. "Detail zero point eight-eight. Within one percent, and I'll sign it."

Oskar Maas did not move. He had a provenance folder open on his knee and the courtesy of a man who has never once been caught lying.

"The certificate says one," Juno said. "Zero point nine-four."

"The certificate is wrong by one connectome." Ratri turned the display. "Agency markers forged. Rated thirty to sixty days. You're forty-two days in. They're thinning — that's not an opinion, that's the decay curve."

Juno looked at her wrist. Somebody's burn.

"Mr Maas."

"Day minus eighty-six," he said. "Two courier drives at the dock. Generation one, both of them." He turned a page without hurrying. "I opened the provenance under a buyer's name because there was no seller willing to be one. That is permitted. Experience is not property."

"Who brought them?"

"Settlement of a hundred and ninety-four gigabytes of drive stock lost in the Ledge channel. The liability was a technician's, at Estuary Seven." He closed the folder. "I took trace instead of fuel cells. He offered."

The room was very quiet. Two flights below, the tide was doing what it did.

The desk chimed. "A Nayla Wiryawan downstairs," the floor said. "No certificate. Carrying a paper log."

Ratri began, unhurriedly, to repack the bench, and then stopped. "I'll stay," she said. "There's a generation number in dispute."

"There's a woman in dispute," Juno said.

"That isn't assayable."

They went down to meet her, Maas first, the folder under his arm. Juno stayed a moment. On the table lay the certificate: generation 1, detail 0.94, originator Halim. Worth more than the memory it described. Worth more, now, than the memory was true.

She picked it up and put it in her coat.
