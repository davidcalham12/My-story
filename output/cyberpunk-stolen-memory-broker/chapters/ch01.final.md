# Chapter 1 — Four Days, Itemised

The index came down at nine kilobits a second, which meant text and nothing else: no thumbnails, no preview trace, just a list building itself a line at a time while the tide worked up the stairwell two flights below.

Lot 4417. Certificate: generation 1, detail 0.94. Originator: Halim, J.

Nayla Wiryawan read the scene titles the way she read a parts manifest, and stopped at the fourth.

*Dawn, Ledge channel, outbound.* *Burn, left wrist, flux spatter.* *Mastoid lead, solder sequence.*

She turned her wrist over. The scar sat where it had always sat, pale against brown, from the morning she had let the iron roll. Nobody else had been on the pontoon. And no one solders a mastoid lead the way she does — shield first, then signal, then the strain loop, because Ilham had taught her that and Ilham had learned it from a man who was dead.

Four days, itemised, under a woman she had never met.

"Twenty-four recorded hours," she said out loud, because a number left unspoken was a number left wrong. "Three-sixty megabytes an hour. Eight thousand six hundred and forty. At half a megabyte a second—" she did it on the bench edge with a fingernail "—four hours forty-eight minutes of cable. Unbroken."

In nine years she had never been out of a chair for four hours and forty-eight minutes at a stretch. Nobody was. The port was a wire behind the ear; a wire behind the ear is a thing people notice.

So it hadn't been one sitting.

The paper log lived under the assay bench, beside the certificate taped to the housing. She went back through it and found the shape of it in four entries, spaced nine days, nine days, eleven.

Day −117. Day −108. Day −99. Day −88.

*Thermal-fault callback. 78 min chair. 4 × 90 s hard-shut. 72 min cable. No charge.*

Identical to the minute. Seventy-two minutes at half a megabyte a second was 2,160 megabytes — six recorded hours, four times over. Her own handwriting, four times over. Dates she remembered arriving at, and not one of them a date she remembered leaving.

Selling the memory wasn't the crime. Nothing about a sold memory had ever been a crime here. The crime was the cable, and the cable was proof, and proof was the one thing she owned.

She queried her own Lattice index over the induction link. Nothing before Day −90; the rolling ninety had eaten it. One fragment survived, stamped Day −88, 36 megabytes. Seventy-two seconds of cable to export. Tension behind her ear. A rig fan. Hands.

Ninety days from Day −88 fell at the 04:00 siren, two days out.

The hull knocked. Ilham Basri came aboard with four fuel cells he had no money for.

"What are you counting?"

"The tide," Nayla said, and closed the log.
