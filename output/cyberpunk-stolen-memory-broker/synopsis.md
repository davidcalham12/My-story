# Four Hours Forty-Eight Minutes of Cable

Nayla Wiryawan pulls the Exchange's index one ordinary shift and finds four days of her own life for sale under someone else's name. Lot 4417: twenty-four hours, certified generation 1, detail 0.94, originator Juno Halim — a stranger who now owns four hours forty-eight minutes of memory that came out of Nayla's own skull. The clinic's paper log, written in her own hand, records four "thermal-fault callbacks" she has no memory of authorizing. Her implant holds only ninety days of anything, and the originals are already overwritten; all that survives is a single 36-megabyte fragment, expiring at the next pump siren whether or not she understands what it shows her. In Baruna Reach, where eleven million people live on stilts above their own drowned city and a cheap cortical port is the one thing everyone can afford to sell, experience isn't property — so what was taken from Nayla is, technically, legal. Proving otherwise means proving it happened inside her body, not on a ledger, before her own memory of it disappears for good. Between a dock buyer who signs provenance he doesn't ask about, an assayer who trusts only her bench, and a boat partner who owes debts he can't explain, Nayla has one night and thirty-six megabytes to find out who held her port open — and why. A hard-edged, unsentimental mystery of memory, debt, and drowned infrastructure.
