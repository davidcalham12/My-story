# Chapter 1 — Store and Forward

At 22:00 Roth signed on, alone, and at 22:15 she keyed the first post check of the shift: *0459 / POST CHECK / DISP ROTH / NORMAL*. The hut ticked as it cooled. Below the window there was nothing to see but the col and the weather coming over it.

The standing notice was clipped to the panel at eye height, where the Observatory liked its notices. OSTGRAT→SATTEL: MAINTENANCE WINDOW 18:00 TUE — 06:00 WED. DATA CHANNEL PRIORITY. Snowpack telemetry. She read it the way she read everything, once, all of it, and left it clipped.

She scrolled INSLOG back. Line 0447: *WENGER, KARL / RECOVERED / 16:20 TUE / CTRL BAUER*. Two confirmations behind it, field callsign and tag, twenty-three minutes apart. Below it, Voss, still carried overdue; search suspended 20:40 for want of a live fix.

22:30. 22:45. 23:00. She logged each one on the quarter hour, and between them the channel hissed its flat open hiss, and nothing came down from above the col.

At 02:44 the Observatory batch cleared. The data indicator on the Ostgrat panel dropped out of priority and sat amber, then green.

At 02:52 a voice landed in the room.

"Col post, col post. Wenger, Karl. Registered party of two." A pause for the chain. "I'm above the north couloir. I'm unhurt. I'm staying at my last fix."

Roth's hand was already on the log key. Procedure first, and procedure said: "Station calling, give me a fix with its tolerance."

He read the position back in degrees and minutes, unhurried, the way a man reads who has written it on his hand. "Plus or minus one hundred twenty metres. I'm under rock."

She entered it as line 0463. The header stamp came up against the line and she looked at it, and then looked at the wall clock, and then at the stamp again.

23:12.

The wall clock said 02:53.

"Station, confirm the time at your end."

The hiss opened. It took long enough that she counted it.

"Twenty-three fourteen," he said.

Then the hiss again, level, uninterrupted, the sound of a channel with nothing on it. She said his callsign twice and got the hiss back both times.

Roth sat with her hands flat on the desk for a moment that she would afterwards be able to measure exactly, because the next thing she did was log the 02:45 post check, and it went in at 02:53.

Then, in the annotation field beside line 0463, she typed what she had and nothing she did not have: *CALLER NAMES HIMSELF AS CASUALTY ON LINE 0447. FIX ±120 M UNDER ROCK. HEADER STAMP 23:12, RECEIVED 02:52. CALLER'S OWN CLOCK 23:14. VOICE UNIMPAIRED.*

She did not write what it meant. She did not know what it meant. She keyed it in, and the log took it, and the hiss went on.
