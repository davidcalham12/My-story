# Chapter 2 — Two Independent Confirmations

The confirmations sat behind line 0447 where Bauer had entered them: field callsign BERG 4, 15:55, one casualty in the north couloir. Tag read 7-1142, 16:18. Twenty-three minutes apart.

Roth counted it twice on her fingers against the card. Thirty-minute window. Twenty-three was inside twenty-three was inside thirty. The line was correctly set. Whatever was wrong with the night, it was not that.

She opened the tag register. 7-1142, rented Wenger, Karl. 7-1150, rented Voss, Lena. Both activated Monday 16:10 at the valley station, one issuing clerk, one minute, two signatures.

She queried the beacon feed for 7-1150 because the feed was there and Voss was overdue and nobody had looked at it since the search was suspended at 20:40. The screen filled. It kept filling. Bursts stacked from 18:14 onward, unread, every one of them carrying a fix and a clock stamp and a battery figure, all of them flagged nothing-to-do because the log said Voss was the one still out.

Roth read the cadence down the column. Thirty-minute intervals from 18:14: 18:44, 19:14, 19:44, 20:14, 20:44. Then the gaps shortened — 20:48, 20:52, 20:56, on through 21:12. Then thirty minutes again, and thirty, and thirty, out to the edge of the screen.

She wrote it on the desk pad in pencil, because the pad did not have to be append-only. *4-min cadence = moving.* Under it: *20:44 − 16:20 = 4 h 24 min.* She looked at that for longer than she looked at most things.

She keyed the valley station on voice. It took two tries. A night clerk answered, chewing something, and told her the duty controller was off the hill, down in the valley, asleep since nine. Did she want him raised? She said no, because she had nothing to raise him with. She had one call, taken by herself, and the card in front of her said in nine words that she could not confirm it.

She tried the channel above the col. *Post to Wenger, Karl. Confirm your tag number. Confirm which beacon you are wearing.* The channel hissed back at her, open and empty, and the Ostgrat status line said QUEUED, 17 of 64.

Sixty-four messages. Retry every twenty minutes. Seventy-two hours, then discard. And — she read it off the card twice, the way she had read the twenty-three minutes — *a queued transmission arrives carrying its original clock stamp and no mark of the delay.*

02:52 minus 23:12 was two hundred and twenty minutes. Two hundred and twenty divided by twenty was eleven.

Eleven retries. The mountain was three and a half hours behind her desk, and the desk was the only place where that was true, and nothing she sent up there would arrive for hours yet.

At 03:00 the post check fell due. The log key was under her hand. She sat with the burst count on the screen — twenty-two, twenty-three — and did not press it.
