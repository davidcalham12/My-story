# Outline

## Promises

1. A dead man's voice arrives on a live channel at 02:52 — who is speaking, and how did the call cross a mountain that was closed?
2. If the man on INSLOG line 0447 is alive above the couloir, then the body carried out of it at 15:55 Tuesday belongs to someone, and the log does not say who.
3. Roth is forbidden to confirm a call she took herself, and the only man who may correct a status line is asleep in the valley — so what procedure is left to her?
4. Tag 7-1150 has been bursting all night and no one thought it strange; what does its cadence prove that no voice on the channel can?
5. Line 0447 is permanent. What does it cost to be right in a system where nothing is true until it is logged, and nothing logged is ever deleted?

## Acts

A single night shift, told at the desk, in the order the desk learns things. Chapter 1 is a quiet watch broken by a routine-sounding voice call that names a recovered man; chapter 2 is Roth reading backwards through the confirmations behind line 0447 and finding a tag number where a person should be, while the rule that forbids her to confirm her own call closes every door; chapter 3 lets the lapsed post check do what she is not permitted to do — the automatic callout fires, Bauer wakes, the queue flushes twenty-four bursts, and the correction is written by the only hand allowed to write it.

### Chapter 1 — Store and Forward

**Tension:** 5/10 — procedure holding steady, then one line of the log that cannot be true.

**Beats:**
1. Roth takes the desk at 22:00 Tuesday, alone, and logs her first post check on the quarter hour.
2. She reads the standing notice: Observatory maintenance window on the Ostgrat→Sattel hop, 18:00 Tuesday to 06:00 Wednesday, data channel priority.
3. She scrolls INSLOG to line 0447 — WENGER, KARL / RECOVERED, 16:20 Tuesday — and below it Voss, still carried overdue, search suspended 20:40 for want of a live fix.
4. Post checks at 22:15, 22:30, 22:45, 23:00; the channel hisses, nothing comes down from above the col.
5. At 02:44 the Observatory batch clears and the data indicator on the Ostgrat panel drops out of priority.
6. At 02:52 a voice call lands: callsign given, name given — Wenger, Karl — reporting himself above the north couloir, unhurt, staying at his last fix.
7. Roth asks for a fix with its tolerance; the voice reads back a position and says ±120 m, under rock.
8. She logs the call as line 0463, and the header stamp on it reads 23:12, not 02:52.
9. She asks the voice to confirm the time at his end; he answers with a time three and a half hours behind her wall clock, and the channel goes back to hiss.
10. Roth logs the 02:45 post check late, at 02:53, and writes beside line 0463 that the caller names himself as the casualty on line 0447.

### Chapter 2 — Two Independent Confirmations

**Tension:** 8/10 — every check she runs makes the impossible more documented and leaves her with no one to tell.

**Beats:**
1. Roth pulls the confirmations behind line 0447: field callsign BERG 4 at 15:55, tag read 7-1142 at 16:18, twenty-three minutes apart.
2. She checks the twenty-three minutes against the rule and finds the entry valid — inside the thirty-minute window, the line correctly set.
3. She reads the tag register: 7-1142 rented to Wenger, 7-1150 rented to Voss, both activated Monday 16:10.
4. She queries the beacon feed and finds 7-1150 bursting all night, timestamps stacked and unread because the log carries Voss as the one still missing.
5. She counts the cadence in the stacked bursts: thirty-minute rest intervals from 18:14, then four-minute intervals from 20:44 to 21:12, then rest again.
6. She writes the arithmetic on the desk pad — four-minute cadence means moving, and 20:44 is four hours and twenty-four minutes after line 0447 was set.
7. She raises the valley station on voice to ask for a second confirmation and is told the duty controller is off the hill; the rule forbids her to confirm a call she took herself.
8. She tries Wenger on the channel to ask him which tag he is wearing, and gets no answer — the hop above the col is still queueing, and nothing she sends arrives.
9. She reads the mast rule off the card: sixty-four messages queued, retry every twenty minutes, arrival carries the original stamp and no mark of delay — and understands that 23:12 reached her eleven retries late.
10. The 03:00 post check falls due; Roth sits with the beacon count in front of her and does not log it.

### Chapter 3 — A Correction Is a New Line

**Tension:** 6/10 rising to 9/10 — the system does her work for her, then hands her the bill.

**Beats:**
1. At 03:05 the automatic valley callout fires on the lapsed post check, twenty minutes after 02:45, and Roth lets the tone run.
2. The callout raises Tomas Bauer in the valley; he comes up on voice and asks first why there is no post check at 03:00.
3. Roth reads him line 0463, the 23:12 stamp, and the Ostgrat queue rule, in that order and without conclusion.
4. Bauer defends line 0447: BERG 4 at 15:55, tag 7-1142 at 16:18, both confirmations logged and valid.
5. The Observatory hop flushes and twenty-four queued bursts from 7-1150 land on the desk at once, the last stamped 02:42.
6. Roth reads him the last fix: ±120 m under rock, 410 m above the box that was searched for Voss and suspended at 20:40.
7. Bauer plays the 23:12 voice tape at 03:14 and takes it as his first confirmation — a voice, not a tag.
8. At 03:29 he completes the tag reconciliation: 7-1150 on the live man, 7-1142 on the casualty recovered Tuesday, and says aloud that the harnesses were swapped at the Sattelhaus.
9. At 03:31 Bauer sets line 0471 — WENGER, KARL / LOCATED, ALIVE, and VOSS, LENA / DECEASED, RECOVERED 15:55 — fifteen minutes after his first confirmation and inside the window; line 0447 stays where it is.
10. Team launched 03:40; Roth logs the launch, logs the lapsed post check as a fault against her own name, and starts the 03:45 check on the quarter hour.
