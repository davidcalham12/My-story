# Chapter 1 — Six Hundred and Eleven

Berth 1180 had written eleven lines. Noor Sandoval read them at the pace she had held for fifty days, because a testament read fast is a testament summarised, and she did not summarise.

Her voice had gone on day thirty-one and come back wrong. The reading floor was hull deck, 0.82 g, cold enough that the mineral wool sweated. Her water ration for the day sat beside the lectern in its metered cup, and she had not touched it, because touching it meant stopping.

She finished. She turned the paper over and read the running count aloud from her own hand.

"Of one thousand one hundred and eighty testaments, six hundred and eleven contain a best-available-data clause. They instruct this ship to use the sky it can actually see, and not the spectra of the 2190s."

Twelve people. On day 154 there had been hundreds.

Hanne Vollmer went on polishing the vault door, the weekly cloth going panel to panel, and did not contest the number. "Five hundred and sixty-nine say the opposite," she said. "The Trust has quoted those for two hundred years. A guild whose whole claim is fidelity cannot be the mouth that announces the founders already let go."

Someone behind her said *the frozen*.

"Signatories," Vollmer said, and kept counting panels.

Oksana Brill came off the stairs without raising her voice. "Article 9 counts votes. Three thousand five hundred and twenty-seven of five thousand two hundred and ninety. A testament is not a chit, and a frozen signatory cannot cut one." She looked at the thin audience. "Ivers, deck 9, four litres. Sunna, deck 14, three point five. Ottoline, deck 22, four." No list. She had never needed one.

Teo Marchetti spoke from her own deck. "One hundred and ninety berths. Four thousand one hundred and ten awake plus one hundred and ninety is four thousand three hundred exactly, which the loop sustains. The biomass reserve is never opened."

"Day sixty-one," Brill said. "A betrayal from my own deck."

Evening, and Ansel Duong came down from the Long Ear with a fragment nobody had asked for, logged that day, at least 11.9 years old.

"It speaks of the beam consortium in the past tense. It calls the *Vantage* the first of three. The other two were never built." He waited. "The Epsilon Eridani water spectra came from that same source. If anyone wants to ask Sol which of these is still true, the round trip is 23.8 years."

At the eighth hour word came down the stairs that the counting hall had been cleared for day 210, and the twelve went up to see it.

Sandoval filed berth 1180 and found she had read one thousand one hundred and eighty promises to an empty floor.
