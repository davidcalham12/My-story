# Chapter 2 — Seventy-Four

The hall opened at seventh bell with Juno Katsaros reading her own disqualification aloud, because the Charter required it and because she liked the sound of a rule that cost her something.

"Helm officers are struck from the roll while serving. I do not vote. Say it back."

The witnesses said it back. Forty of them, one from every deck, standing at the long tables under lights scheduled for the occasion. No terminal in the room was powered. Since Year 96 the ship's computers could model this to four decimals and were forbidden to touch a single chit, and Katsaros had always thought that was the most civilised sentence in the Charter.

The chits came up from the boxes in paper handfuls, still smelling of the press. Two questions each: Wake or Keep Sealed; and if Wake, Limited or Full. She counted aloud in blocks of fifty. The hall repeated each block. It took nine hours and it was, she thought, the least frightening nine hours she had spent in a year.

Twice the tables were challenged. Oksana Brill's witness objected to a chit creased across the fold; Katsaros admitted it, the mark being legible. An hour later Noor Sandoval's witness objected to a chit creased identically; Katsaros admitted that one too, on the same grounds, and neither faction thanked her. Good. Indifference was the only thing she was permitted to own in public.

What she was not permitted to own stood behind her sternum all day, cold and numbered, and said: *wake them.* She did not spend it. There was nowhere to spend it.

Idris Falk stood at the back with the ledger open on his forearm and said, to nobody, that each revival was nineteen megawatt-hours and forty days of a technician's attention, that nine in a hundred failed irreversibly, that the ship held 41.2 kilometres per second and a diversion would spend 8.7 of it before flight year 292 day 1. He offered no metaphor. He looked at her once, a half-second too long, and went back to his kilograms.

At the twenty-third hour she read the result and made them give it back to her.

"Chits cast, three thousand four hundred and ten of three thousand seven hundred and forty-eight. Wake, one thousand seven hundred and forty-two. Keep Sealed, one thousand six hundred and sixty-eight. Margin, seventy-four. Of the wake chits: Limited, one thousand four hundred and forty-one. Full, three hundred and one."

The hall gave it back. She sealed the doors herself. No recount.

Afterwards Falk waited by the empty tables. "You could say it now."

"No," said Juno Katsaros, "I couldn't," and signed the certificate in a clean hand.
