# Chapter 3 — The Denominator

The door took wax the way it always had, in small circles, sixteen years of Thursdays in her wrist. Hanne Vollmer polished while the warming clock on berth 0901 ran down its last hour. Behind her the sunward deck hummed at 0.82 g and smelled of cold metal and chlorophyll, and beyond the hull the *Vantage* went on at 0.0341c toward Tau Ceti, as she had since 2211.

Flight year 209: nine days of rewarming on a failed power bus, logged as a thermal excursion, never tested. Flight year 271: sixty-four trickle signatures that did not match the other 1,116, sealed inside the only complete enrollment record aboard, which was hers. She had never been able to test them. The only test was a forty-day warming that could not be undone. In flight year 262 she had signed a death on warming day 31 with her own hand, and after that the nine percent was not a statistic to her. So she had waited for a ballot. On day 210 the ship gave her one by seventy-four chits, and chose the limited thaw — Teo Marchetti's 190 berths, the exact number the loop could absorb.

0901 opened at the scheduled minute. The technicians worked the procedure they had rehearsed for fifty years without performing it once: drain, lavage, sternal leads, the checklist read aloud. The body was warm. Nothing else about it was.

"Note the time," Vollmer said. "Signatory. Not remains. Signatory."

0902 came eleven minutes later, the same.

Noor Sandoval stood at the rail with her hands folded. "Berth 0901," she said. "Berth 0902." She had no other way to name them that she was willing to use.

Idris Falk arrived with the ledger and did not look at the tables. "Article 9 wants 3,527 of the chartered 5,290. The roll produces 3,748 chits. That was already 94.1 percent of everyone who could vote. Add 190 berths — sixty-four of them dead since 209, about eleven more to the nine percent — and the roll is roughly 3,863 and the bar is roughly 91.3 percent, and flight year 292 day 1 arrives regardless. Only the full thaw ever moved it. Three hundred and one people voted for that."

"Say what it is," said Oksana Brill, and then did not say anything else.

"It is mass and it is arithmetic," Falk said. "I won't call it anything grander."

Vollmer entered both deaths in the enrollment record, in ink, and wrote beneath them that the Charter's denominator had counted sixty-four corpses since flight year 209 — that every threshold the ship had ever computed had been computed against the dead.

Then she picked up the cloth. The door still needed polishing.
