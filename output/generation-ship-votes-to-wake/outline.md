# Outline — *The Vantage*

## Acts

**Act I — The Grocery Bill with 1,180 Lines.** The *Vantage* is eight years from a deceleration burn that ends all argument, and the ballot on whether to wake the 1,180 frozen signatories is fifty days out. Noor Sandoval is midway through reading all 1,180 testaments aloud, a labour the Charter Trust never expected to be honest about; Teo Marchetti's 190-berth audit has split his own bloc; Oksana Brill is counting corridor decks by name. The act establishes the arithmetic — 3,748 chits, 94.1% needed for any diversion — and plants the thing nobody says aloud: Hanne Vollmer polishes a door with sixty-four berths behind it whose trickle signatures have not matched since flight year 271.

**Act II — The Tally.** Day 210. A hand-counted, air-gapped ballot in a cleared hall, run by a Returning Officer struck from her own roll. Ansel Duong's eleven-year-old fragment about the beam consortium — spoken of in the past tense — lands the evening before and is too old to be an argument, which is exactly Duong's point. The count runs to a margin of 74 for Wake, with Limited outvoting Full six to one. Juno Katsaros certifies a result she wanted and will never admit wanting, because saying so would poison every count she has ever run.

**Act III — Day 254.** The thaw the ship authorised is a limited one, which means the diversion was arithmetically dead before the chits were cut, and only Falk and Katsaros are equipped to see it. Vollmer names the first cohort and puts berths 0901 to 0964 at the head of it. Forty days of warming end with two berths opening on people who died in flight year 209. The Charter's denominator has counted sixty-four corpses for seventy-eight years, and the ship sails on to Tau Ceti exactly as the 2190s data said it would.

## Chapters

### Chapter 1 — Six Hundred and Eleven

- **POV:** Noor Sandoval
- **Place and date:** The reading floor outside the sunward vault door, *Vantage*, flight year 287, day 203
- **Tension:** 6/10
- **Promise advanced:** The testaments will be read in full, and we will learn what the Trust has been hiding behind selective quotation

- **Beats:**
  1. Sandoval is on day fifty of reading all 1,180 testaments aloud, and she is on the last one — she cites by berth number, never name, and has refused since day 154 to summarise any testament she has not finished. Establish the physical grind: 0.82 g at the hull, metered water, her voice gone, an audience that has thinned from hundreds to a dozen. She finishes berth 1180 and states the running count she has kept on paper: 611 of the 1,180 contain a best-available-data clause instructing the ship to use the sky it can actually see rather than the 2190s spectra that chose Tau Ceti.
  2. Hanne Vollmer is present, polishing the vault door as she does weekly, and does not contest the number. Establish through her that the Trust quoted the other 569 for decades because those read as instructions to honour the original choice, and a guild whose entire claim is fidelity could not be the one to announce that the founders had already released the ship. Vollmer calls them signatories and corrects someone who says "the frozen." She says nothing about berths 0901 to 0964, but she is counting the door's panels while she talks and the reader should feel a woman carrying something.
  3. Oksana Brill arrives off the stairs, never raising her voice, and demolishes the revelation on procedure: Article 9 counts votes, not wishes, and a frozen signatory's testament is not a chit. She names three people in the thin audience by their water rations without a list. Teo Marchetti, her own deck steward, contradicts her in public with his 190-berth audit — 4,110 awake plus 190 is exactly the 4,300 the closed loop sustains, the biomass reserve never touched — and Brill calls it what she called it on day 61: a betrayal from her own deck.
  4. Evening. Ansel Duong comes down from the Long Ear with a Sol fragment logged that day — at least 11.9 years old — that speaks of the beam consortium in the past tense and of the *Vantage* as the first of three, the other two never built. Nobody asked for it. He notes the thing nobody wants to hear: the Epsilon Eridani water spectra everyone is arguing over came from the same silent source, and no question sent to Sol can complete a round trip in under 23.8 years.
  5. Word comes that the counting hall has been cleared. Sandoval files the last testament and finds she has read 1,180 promises to an empty floor.

- **Must establish for later chapters:** The ship is the *Vantage*, flight year 287, coasting at 0.0341c, 4,110 awake and 1,180 frozen; the ballot is day 210 on whether to wake the signatories; Article 9 requires two-thirds of 5,290 — 3,527 — and frozen members count in the denominator but cannot vote; the published roll is 3,748 chits; the Tally is hand-counted paper chits, air-gapped, no recount after sealing; shipboard computers are legally read-only in governance since the Year 96 lockout; Vollmer's unspoken burden exists; Marchetti's 190-berth ecology-neutral number exists; Duong's fragment exists.
- **Closing line's intended effect:** Fifty days of honesty, delivered to nobody, on the eve of a count that will not consider it.

### Chapter 2 — Seventy-Four

- **POV:** Juno Katsaros
- **Place and date:** The counting hall, *Vantage*, flight year 287, day 210
- **Tension:** 4/10
- **Promise advanced:** The vote will be counted, cleanly, and we will see the number; and we will learn whether Juno Katsaros, forbidden a preference, has one

- **Beats:**
  1. Katsaros opens the hall as Returning Officer, struck from the ballot roll while serving, as all Helm officers are. Establish the mechanics as ritual and as relief: paper chits, public tables, witnesses from every deck, the computers legally barred from touching it. She counts aloud and makes every witness repeat each number back to her. Deliberately low-key — this chapter breathes. The tension is procedural patience, not crisis.
  2. The chits ask two things: Wake or Keep Sealed, and if Wake, Limited or Full. Brill's witnesses and the Trust's witnesses watch the same tables. Katsaros refuses a Continuance objection and a Trust objection in the same hour on identical grounds, and is publicly indifferent in a way that costs her. Interior only, never spoken: she wants the signatories woken, and the one preference she holds is the one preference she is structurally forbidden to spend.
  3. Idris Falk stands at the back with the mass ledger and quotes the count in kilograms — every revival is 19 MWh and 40 days of a technician's attention, 9% of revivals fail irreversibly, remaining delta-v is 41.2 km/s and any diversion must spend 8.7 of it before flight year 292 day 1. He refuses metaphor. He looks at Katsaros once too long. Establish, lightly, that Falk knows what she wants and will never say.
  4. The count closes: 3,410 chits cast of 3,748. Wake 1,742, Keep Sealed 1,668 — a margin of 74. Of the wake chits, Limited 1,441 and Full 301. Katsaros reads the numbers aloud and has the hall repeat them. She seals the hall; there is no recount.
  5. Alone afterward, Falk offers her the chance to say it. She does not — a Returning Officer who admits a preference retroactively poisons every count she has ever certified, including this one.

- **Must establish for later chapters:** The exact result (3,410 of 3,748; Wake 1,742 / Keep Sealed 1,668, margin 74; Limited 1,441 / Full 301) and that the hall is sealed with no recount; that the ship authorised a *limited* thaw, i.e. Marchetti's 190 berths; Falk's commit deadline of flight year 292 day 1 and the 41.2 km/s figure; that Katsaros's preference is known to Falk and to no one else and is never confirmed aloud.
- **Closing line's intended effect:** The cleanest count in the ship's history, certified by the only person aboard who is not allowed to be glad about it.

### Chapter 3 — The Denominator

- **POV:** Hanne Vollmer
- **Place and date:** The sunward vault deck, *Vantage*, flight year 287, day 254 — the fortieth and final day of warming for the first thaw cohort
- **Tension:** 9/10
- **Promise advanced:** We will find out what is wrong with berths 0901 to 0964 and why Hanne Vollmer has never said; and we will learn whether a diversion to Epsilon Eridani was ever actually possible

- **Beats:**
  1. Context the writer needs in-scene: the ship is the *Vantage*, flight year 287, a spinning cylinder coasting at 0.0341c toward Tau Ceti with 4,110 people born aboard and 1,180 signatories frozen in sealed berths on the sunward endcap. On day 210 the ship voted by a margin of 74 to wake them, and chose the *limited* thaw — Teo Marchetti's ecology-neutral 190 berths, the exact number the closed loop can absorb without touching the biomass reserve. Vollmer, First Custodian of the vaults, named the first cohort on day 214 and put berths 0901 to 0964 at the head of it. Revival is a 40-day warming that cannot be reversed; 9% of revivals fail. Today is day 40. Open with her polishing the vault door, as she has every week for sixteen years.
  2. The confession, carried in her head as the warming clock runs out: a power-bus fault in flight year 209 let berths 0901 to 0964 rewarm for nine days before trickle power was restored; the Trust logged it as a thermal excursion and never tested it. In flight year 271 she found that those sixty-four berths' trickle signatures did not match the other 1,116, sealed the finding inside the enrollment record — the only complete copy, hers — and told no one outside the guild. She could not test them, because the only test is a warming that cannot be undone: opening them to prove they were alive would have been the act that killed them if they were. She waited until the ballot had already authorised a thaw. Establish that in flight year 262 she personally signed the death of the only signatory ever revived aboard, who died on warming day 31, and that the 9% stopped being a statistic for her then.
  3. Berth 0901 opens. Then 0902. Two people who have been dead for seventy-eight years. Play it as vault technicians doing procedure on bodies — warm, not living. Vollmer says the word she has always insisted on: signatories. Noor Sandoval, who read all 1,180 testaments aloud between day 154 and day 203, is present as Reader and cites the two by berth number rather than name because she has no other way to honour them.
  4. Idris Falk arrives with the ledger and closes the other door. Article 9 needs 3,527 of the chartered 5,290 for a diversion; the roll can produce 3,748 chits, so the requirement was already 94.1% of everyone who could vote. The limited thaw adds 190 berths — sixty-four of them already dead, and roughly eleven of the remaining 126 lost to the 9% — lifting the roll to about 3,863 and lowering the bar only to about 91.3%, while the flight year 292 day 1 commit deadline runs out. Only the full thaw could ever have brought Epsilon Eridani within reach, and the full thaw was what 301 people voted for. Falk states it in kilograms and refuses to call it a tragedy. Oksana Brill, whose bloc won the argument it did not know it had already won, has nothing to say and says it by name to no one.
  5. Vollmer records the deaths in the enrollment record and states the consequence plainly: the Charter's denominator has counted sixty-four corpses since flight year 209, which means every threshold the ship has ever computed, including Article 9's, was computed against a number that included the dead. She resumes polishing the door. The *Vantage* continues to Tau Ceti, arrival flight year 349, exactly as the 2190s data said it would.

- **Must establish for later chapters:** Nothing — this is the final chapter. It must introduce no new promise, resolve the 0901–0964 mystery as death, resolve the diversion question as arithmetically foreclosed before the ballot, and leave Katsaros's preference unconfirmed if she is mentioned at all.
- **Closing line's intended effect:** A lie ends in her lifetime, as she wanted, and costs exactly what she knew it would cost — the ship goes where it was always going, and the door still needs polishing.
