# The *Vantage* — Flight Year 287

The *Vantage* left Sol orbit in 2211 under a laser beam that pushed her to 0.0341c and then, ninety-six years later, went dark without explanation. She has coasted ever since: a spinning cylinder 1.4 km long, 310 m across, carrying 4,110 people born aboard and 1,180 frozen founders who signed the Charter and chose Tau Ceti before any living crewman existed. In eight years the deceleration stage lights and the last of the reaction mass is spent; after that the destination is arithmetic, not opinion. A faction of the awake wants to divert to Epsilon Eridani, whose recent spectra suggest a wetter world than the one the founders picked from 2190s data. Charter Article 9 makes that change impossible without the founders' votes, and founders cannot vote while frozen. So the ship must first vote on whether to wake them. The ballot is scheduled for day 210 of flight year 287, and the counting hall has already been cleared.

## Factions

- **The Helm** — wants the vote to be procedurally unimpeachable so that whatever it decides survives the next two centuries; gets it by controlling the ballot rolls, the counting hall, and the mass ledger, and by refusing to state a preference in public.
- **Continuance** — the born-aboard majority bloc; wants the course left to the people who will actually walk on the planet, which means keeping the vaults shut; gets it by campaigning on the thaw's cost in biomass, power and dead sleepers, and by turning out the corridor decks.
- **The Charter Trust** — the custodial guild that maintains the vaults and the founders' testaments; wants the sleepers woken, both because Article 9 requires it for a diversion and because they believe a promise to 1,180 people is not a line item; gets it by controlling vault access, revival scheduling, and the only complete copy of the enrollment record.

## Technology

- **Cryostasis vaults** — 1,180 sealed berths on the sunward endcap, stable for centuries on trickle power. Revival is a 40-day warming under continuous supervision and cannot be reversed: a thawed body cannot be refrozen.
- **Closed ecology** — 26 hydroponic decks and an algal loop recovering 98.6% of water, carbon and nitrogen per cycle. Losses are made up from a finite reserve of stored biomass.
- **Deceleration stage** — a deuterium fusion motor plus a 40 km magnetic sail that brakes against the destination star's wind. It throws mass to change velocity; there is no other way to steer the ship.
- **The Tally** — a hand-counted, air-gapped ballot: paper chits, public tables, witnesses from every deck. The ship's computers may model the outcome but are legally barred from touching it.
- **The Long Ear** — a 12 m dish that still receives Sol traffic. Everything it hears is at least 11.9 years old and nothing can be asked.

## Rules

- No faster-than-light travel and no FTL signalling. The *Vantage* coasts at 0.0341c; Tau Ceti arrival is flight year 349. Any message to or from Sol crosses 11.9 light-years each way, so no exchange with Earth can complete in under 23.8 years.
- Remaining delta-v is 41.2 km/s, all of it committed to the deceleration burn beginning flight year 295. Any manoeuvre before then subtracts from that figure permanently. There are no reactionless drives: every velocity change throws propellant overboard.
- The hull cannot survive above 0.04c against interstellar dust, so the ship cannot accelerate further under any circumstance.
- Each revival consumes 19 MWh and 40 days of a vault technician's attention, and 9% of revivals fail irreversibly. Waking all 1,180 sleepers takes a minimum of 640 days at maximum vault throughput and is statistically certain to kill roughly 106 of them.
- The closed ecology sustains 4,300 people indefinitely. Above that number the deficit is drawn from a reserve of 11,000 person-years of stored biomass. Waking every sleeper puts 5,290 aboard and exhausts that reserve in eleven years.
- Charter Article 9: a change of destination requires two-thirds of the chartered complement — 3,527 of 5,290. Frozen members count in the denominator and cannot vote. Therefore a diversion decided by the awake alone would need 3,527 of 4,110 ballots, or 85.8%.
- Suffrage is one person, one chit, age 16 and above; no proxies, no absentee votes, no recount after the hall is sealed. Helm officers are struck from the roll while serving.
- Since the Year 96 lockout, shipboard computers are legally read-only in governance: they may advise and model, but an order originating from a machine is void and any officer executing one is removed.

## Texture

The *Vantage* smells of wet mineral wool and chlorophyll, and every corridor has the faint ozone tang of a spin section that has been patched too many times. Gravity is 0.82 g at the hull and thins as you climb inward, so arguments happen on stairs. Light is scheduled. Water is metered by name. People know the mass ledger the way farmers know weather — everyone can tell you what a kilogram of anything costs in air — and this makes the coming vote feel less like philosophy than like a grocery bill with 1,180 lines on it. The vaults hum on the sunward end, cold and numbered, behind a door the Trust polishes weekly, and nobody who works that deck calls the sleepers anything but "the signatories."
