Leo is ten years old today, and he lives in Cadaques, a real seaside town on the Costa Brava where the Mediterranean breaks against stone. This summer, on his tenth birthday, he is about to have an adventure that will need everything he has: his dog Bruno asleep at the foot of his bed, his cardboard observatory built with his grandfather in the garden, and the fossil he found on the beach—the one he named Captain Crunch. The adventure will take him to places he has never been, at times he has not prepared for, and will ask him to be brave in ways he does not yet know he can be.

## Factions

- **The Summer Families** — They arrive each July and August with suitcases and cameras, wanting the town to be a perfect postcard: sun, beaches, ice cream on the promenade. They want to take it with them when they leave. They are wrong about how much it belongs to them, and they are wrong about how the sea behaves on schedule.

- **The Year-Round People** — Fishermen, shopkeepers, families rooted here across generations. They want the town to move by the seasons and the tides, as it always has. They know which hours are safe and which are dangerous. They are impatient with outsiders who have not learned the rhythm yet.

- **The Children of Cadaques** — They have mapped the town in a way no adult's map shows: the best tide pools, the safest routes in the dark, which gardens have walls to climb and which streets are too open. They want their town to belong to them. They judge newcomers by whether the newcomers understand the rules.

- **The Grandparents** — They remember when the harbour was different, when rocks were less visited, when people knew more about the tides and the dark. They keep the old knowledge. They want the young to learn it before forgetting makes the sea more dangerous.

## Things

- **Captain Crunch** — The fossil Leo found on the beach at Cadaques. It is real, it is his, and naming it made it real in a way that matters. It fits in a pocket. It cannot move, and it cannot tell the future, though Leo sometimes hopes it can.

- **The Stone Collection** — Leo collects smooth stones from the beach and arranges them by size, colour, and smoothness. Each stone tells where it came from. The collection can be counted and rearranged. It cannot be sold or replaced. It can comfort him, but it cannot change what is coming.

- **The Cardboard Observatory** — Built in the garden with his grandfather, with a roof you can open. On clear nights you can lie inside and see stars through the hole at the top. It can show the sky. It cannot see beyond the garden wall, and it cannot protect you from what happens outside it. It is fragile and has never been moved.

- **Bruno** — A scruffy terrier who sleeps at the foot of Leo's bed. He is loyal and brave, and he will follow Leo almost anywhere. He will not cross water deeper than his legs. He will not go out in heavy rain. He barks at strangers but forgets them quickly.

- **The Torch** — Small, with fresh batteries. It makes a circle of light on the ground in front of you. It can be seen from far away if someone is looking. It cannot light all the darkness, and it makes shadows deeper where the beam does not reach.

- **The Tide Table** — A printed chart pinned to the kitchen wall. It shows the exact times of high and low tide for every day. The times never change. Everything else in Cadaques moves around them.

## Rules

- **R1.** The tide comes in and goes out twice a day on the Costa Brava, and the times change by about 50 minutes each day. The tide table shows the exact times. No character can stay on the beach when the tide is coming in unless they can reach safe ground before it cuts them off. This is not optional.

- **R2.** Leo can go to the beach, the rocks at the far end of the beach, the harbour, the town's streets, and other families' gardens. He cannot cross the road that leads inland toward other villages. The rocks are inside what is allowed. The inland road is the boundary.

- **R3.** The fear: Leo is afraid of the dark but will not admit it—not to anyone, not to himself. He will go out alone after dark only if something matters more than the fear. The darkness: In summer, the dark does not arrive until after nine o'clock. But out on the rocks, away from the town's street lamps, it is real darkness, complete and deep, deep enough that the torch becomes necessary.

- **R4.** Bruno will not cross water. Not the stream by the fountain, not the channel between beach and rocks, not the deep tide pools. He will go around or wait.

- **R5.** The cardboard observatory is in the garden and has never been moved. It can show the sky but cannot see beyond the garden wall. Anything happening outside the garden cannot be seen from inside it, even with the roof open.

- **R6.** His grandfather is old and does not move quickly. When Leo's grandfather is with him, their speed is his grandfather's speed. Leo cannot leave him behind to move faster.

- **R7.** The stone collection matters to Leo more than almost anything else. If the collection is threatened, he will act. If he loses a stone, he feels it deeply.

## Texture

Cadaques in summer is a place where the sun is honest and the stone is warm. The sea smells like salt and possibility. The streets are narrow and make sense only if you have grown up here, or if someone who has grown up here shows them to you. The light lasts a long time, and people stay out late. Everything is real here—the rocks do not move, the sea does not lie about the tides—and that is both comforting and terrifying to a boy who is just turning ten and learning that the world has rules that do not care if he is ready.
