# Chapter 2 — What's Beyond

[NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] sat on the edge of his bed with his stone jar open beside him and a canvas bag on his lap. The morning was already hot. Cicadas were sawing away in the olive trees beyond the garden, and through his window he could see the dry grass shimmering where his mother had stopped watering it three weeks ago.

He looked down at the forty-three stones.

The grey one with the white stripe he picked up first, turning it over in his palms the way he had been doing since yesterday at supper. There was no question about this one—it was his best thinking stone, worn smooth by a river that was probably miles away by now. That had to come. He set it gently in the bag.

The yellow one from the beach at Cadaques was next. Small, almost perfectly round. He had found it the same summer as Captain Crunch, which seemed important, though he could not have said why. He added it to the bag.

Three more smooth stones—one black, one speckled, one the color of old cream—went in after that. He was not sure what he needed them for exactly. Some things, he was learning, did not need a reason before you packed them. They just seemed to belong.

The fossil he lifted last, turning it in the light from his window. The thin ridge along one side caught the sun just right, and for a moment it really did look like the edge of a shell. Captain Crunch had been somewhere. It had been in the ground for longer than anyone could count, or lived in the sea, or both. If anything in this bag was going to be lucky, it would be that.

He wrapped the fossil in his spare handkerchief and set it in the center of the bag, where it would not knock against anything else. Then he took the new leather-bound notebook from the shelf—the one with the question about the fossil's shape already written inside in his own careful printing—and slid it down beside the stones.

When he lifted the bag to feel its weight, it seemed perfectly right. Not too light. Not too heavy.

Bruno was waiting by the kitchen door, leash in mouth.

[NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] stopped. He had left the leash coiled on the hook above the coat stand. He had not told Bruno anything about going anywhere.

His mother was at the kitchen table with her coffee, and she looked up with a smile that said nothing—the kind of smile that meant she knew exactly what was happening and was choosing not to make a big thing of it.

Bruno dropped the leash at [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO]'s feet and circled three times before lying down exactly where it had fallen. His tail was up. His eyes followed [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO]'s face.

"Well," [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] said. "All right then."

He picked up the leash and clipped it to Bruno's collar. The dog did not whine. He did not press himself against [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO]'s leg the way he sometimes did in thunderstorms. He simply stood and waited, his whole shabby terrier body saying yes.

[NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] picked up his canvas bag with one hand and held the leash with the other. He was not sure what he had expected to feel before this moment, but he was surprised by how ordinary his own feet looked on the kitchen tiles. The same feet that had walked to school every day since he was five. His mother took a slow sip of her coffee.

"Home before the sun touches the horizon," she said, which was not a question.

"I know," [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] said.

"Not when it is starting to. When it touches."

"I know, Mamma."

She nodded and smiled that same small smile. "Then go."

The village fountain was in the center of the plaza, under a dusty linden tree that had been old when [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO]'s grandmother was young. He sat on the stone rim and let Bruno lap at the water while he waited for his eyes to adjust after the bright white heat of the walk.

Someone tapped his shoulder.

Rosa was standing over him with flour on both her sleeves, even though it was hours past the morning baking. Her dark hair was stuck to the back of her neck, and she was grinning the way she did when she was about to say something she had been thinking about for a while.

"Where are you going?" she said.

"The hill," [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] said.

"With those stones?"

He felt his face go hot. "Maybe I need them."

"You don't need them," Rosa said, sitting down beside him without being asked. "But you're bringing them anyway, which means you think they're important, which means—" She stopped and brushed flour off her left sleeve, not looking at it. "Which means this is something. What's really on the other side? I've asked my father like five times and he just says 'less interesting than you think' every time, which is such an obviously wrong answer that I know something is there."

Bruno lifted his head from the water and looked at Rosa, his whole face going happy. She leaned over and ruffled his ears.

"Did you ask Marcos?" [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] said.

"I haven't seen Marcos. Did you?"

"No. I thought—" [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] turned a stone over in his pocket, one he had taken from the jar at the last second. "I thought maybe he would tell me if I asked him."

"And would he?"

[NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] shrugged. "Maybe in a story. He does that. He circles around the answer until you stop listening and then you know what it was."

Rosa laughed, a quick bright sound. "That's the worst way to tell someone something. It's brilliant." She brushed flour off her other sleeve, still without noticing she was doing it. "It's why you need me. You're good at thinking about things, and listening to old people talk around answers, but I'm good at asking questions that make people stop being mysterious. And also—" She looked directly at him. "Also you shouldn't go alone. What if something happens?"

"Like what?" [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] said, but he already knew the answer, which was nothing and everything, all at once.

"Like you fall. Like you get lost coming back. Like you need someone to laugh if you do something stupid." She stood up and brushed her apron, which did have flour on it, and this time she actually saw it and was surprised. "Also I want to go. I want to see what's there."

She crossed her arms and waited.

[NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] looked at Bruno, who was looking at Rosa with his head tilted, waiting too.

"Then I'm coming too," Rosa said.
