Day 4. [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] and Bruno set out after breakfast with the whole day ahead and climbed until his legs burned and the village below looked like something painted on paper. Past the shepherd's markers the path came and went, and in a stretch of deep shade Bruno pressed against his leg and whined. That is the warning, and he took it seriously: he stopped, and he said aloud, with his voice cracking, that he did not know if he could find the way back down from here, and that Bruno was trusting him. It was not the dark he was afraid of. It was being lost, and not being able to get Bruno home. Then he went on anyway.

The ground levelled without warning and the fear drained into something he could carry. Below the crest lay a whole valley nobody in the village had ever mentioned: a stream, fields, and a stone house with a wall around it. A girl stood in the yard with her hand over her eyes, looking up at him. She called "Who are you?" and her voice arrived both nearer and farther than she was, the way voices do on that hill.

He took his first step down toward her, and the sun touched the horizon. He understood the rule differently then: it does not say run, it says stop, and stopping where you are is obedience, not failure. So he stopped.

Her name is Elena. She is twelve and lives there with her grandmother. She climbed the slope to him, took Bruno by the collar, and led them down to the lit house — warmth, shelter, and food cooking.
