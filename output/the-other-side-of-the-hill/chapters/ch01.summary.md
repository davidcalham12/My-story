Day 1. [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] turned ten. Marcos came to the house with a leather-bound notebook wrapped in brown paper and told him it was for the questions he could not ask anyone else, not for answers. [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] put the notebook on the shelf beside Captain Crunch, looked at the fossil properly for the first time in a while, saw a thin ridge along one side like the edge of a shell, and wrote his first question in the notebook: "What shape was it?" He did not write an answer.

At supper his father told him he is old enough now to go to the hill, on one condition: daylight only, home before the sun touches the horizon, because once the sun is fully down the paths change and distance stops being reliable. [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] said he was not afraid of anything. Nobody at the table believed him and nobody said so. He sat turning a grey river stone with a white stripe over and over in his hands.

Who knows what: the whole family and Marcos know he means to go to the hill and that he has permission. Nobody has crossed it. Nobody in the book has mentioned what is on the other side, and Marcos has not been asked.

Still open: what is over the hill; why the fossil matters so much to him; what he is actually afraid of, which is not the dark.

Bruno slept at the foot of the bed and circled three times before lying down in the cool corner of the room.
