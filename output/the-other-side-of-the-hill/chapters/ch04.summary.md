Day 3, afternoon. Rosa caught [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] at the edge of the plaza, apron white with flour, and told him she cannot come tomorrow: the ovens are going for the feast, someone has to mind the counter and the market rush, and she is someone, apparently. "Bread is ruining my life." She laughed at her own rambling and was disappointed underneath it, and made him promise — absolutely promise — to tell her everything he sees. The colour of the stones, whether it is steep, whether there are birds, what it smells like. He promised. She said quietly that she cannot ask her father about the hill any more, because he only says it is less interesting than she thinks and they both know she does not believe him, so now they do not talk about it at all.

Then [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] and Bruno walked out past the last houses to the foot of the hill and started up the shepherd's path, which is marked with small pale stones laid by somebody, years ago, to say: this way. They climbed past the first pines, through the smell of bruised wild thyme. Bruno's ears stayed up the whole way; he was enjoying himself.

Where the path narrowed into loose shale and the pines closed over it and the light went dim, [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] stopped and did the arithmetic himself: the afternoon was turning, and if he went on he might not be home before the sun touched the horizon. So he turned back, and Bruno followed without hesitation. He goes alone tomorrow.
