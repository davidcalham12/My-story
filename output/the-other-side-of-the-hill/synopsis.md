Leo is ten years old today and finally old enough to climb the hill behind his house that he has never crossed—but only if he is home before the sun touches the horizon. What he is really afraid of, though he will not admit it, is not the dark itself, but getting lost, and whether he and Bruno, his scruffy terrier, will make it home.

He packs his fossil and his stones. He walks the path through deep shade where Bruno presses against his leg and whines. He names his fear aloud, voice cracking, and goes on anyway. And when the sun touches the horizon at the crest, he finds what no one in the village has ever mentioned: a valley with a stream and fields, a stone house with a wall, and a girl standing in the yard with her hand over her eyes.

One kitchen, one warm meal, one fossil on a shelf that looks almost like his own. By morning Leo is walking home changed. His grandfather, asked straight out, finally answers plainly. And Leo writes in his notebook: Elena has a fossil too. We are the same kind of person. Then he asks whether he can go again.

A warm, funny summer quest about bravery—not the absence of fear, but the choice to move forward despite it—where the greatest wonders are real and a loyal terrier understands more than anyone expects.

Comparables: *Because of Winn-Dixie* by Kate DiCamillo, *The Wind in the Willows* by Kenneth Grahame
