Day +24. Brede closed the thirteen-day series — fifteen vials, five days' outlet samples skipped and logged as skipped, four vials left — and regressed: loop D/H rising 2.38 ± 0.30 ppm/day, 7.9 standard errors from zero, which she rounded aloud to eight. She signed it with the word *cause* deliberately absent.

Then she inverted it for Pike. The predicted rate is 2.3 × 649 / L, so the measured slope fixes L: 627 kg, and the 12.6% error on the slope carries through to ±80. The design figure 612 sits inside it, and 612 plus the 25.3 kg Marchetti rounded away — 637.3 — sits closer to her centre. It is the first instrument estimate of the loop inventory ever made aboard Bittern. The water was always in the loop; what vanished was the record.

Vance opened the Day −40 bag for the first time. Stack B's membranes came out white and crusted, not grey and worn; the swab ran as sodium carbonate, not wear. He took Stack A off the line a full day for its own swab — 3.1 kg, the strip re-cut from eighty-nine to eighty-seven, 127.7 kg standing — and Stack A showed the same white, thinner, forty days behind.

Brede's one line: same pipe, same vapour, one source. Closing the valve stops the scale and the water together.
