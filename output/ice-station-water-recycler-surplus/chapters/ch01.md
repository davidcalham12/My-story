# Chapter 1 — The Second Strip

The 0600 handover smelled of carbonate, the way every hour aboard did, sweetish and faintly mineral, worked so far into the collar of Pike's coverall that she only noticed it when Salas came close enough to hand her the strip.

He had signed it. The signature sat in the box at the foot of the printout, and above the signature the numbers ran in two columns, inlet and outlet, hour by hour through the night watch. Salas had not read them. Pike knew this the way she knew the pitch of the number three circulation pump, which had been running a half-tone flat since she came on rotation and would run flat until someone replaced a bearing nobody had: by long exposure, without needing to prove it. He signed what he was handed. That was not a failing in a relief hand. It was the whole job description.

"Quiet," Salas said.

"Quiet's fine."

He went aft to sleep. Pike stood under the sodium strip in the recycler alcove and read the printout properly, which took ninety seconds, and then she folded it in three and put it in her breast pocket with the others.

There were ten in there already. Day −11 through the night before last, the fold lines soft now from being carried, each one annotated in her own hand in the margin where Cold Harbor's schema had no field. The eleventh made eleven. Inlet 61.0 kg through the pass. Outlet 63.3. Difference plus 2.3, and it was 2.3 the way the previous ten had been 2.3 — not 2.28 rounded up, not 2.31 shaded down, but the same figure walking in every morning wearing the same coat.

She did the accumulation in her head because she had done it every morning for six days and the answer arrived before the arithmetic did. Eleven days at 2.3 was 25.3 kg. On 61 kg of throughput, 2.3 was a shade under 3.8 percent. The tolerance on the loop, entered in the standing instruction on the day the station was commissioned and never argued with since, was half a percent over any twenty-four-hour window. She was running seven and a half times tolerance and had been running it for a week and a half, and the totalizers themselves counted to two hundredths of a kilogram per pass, which meant the instrument had no room to be lying at that magnitude. A totalizer could drift. It could not invent 2.3 kg eleven mornings running and hit the same figure each time.

Nothing was being created. That was the part she kept coming back to. In a loop sealed downstream of the slurry isolation valve, with one metered way in, more out than in was not luck. It was a door somebody had not drawn on the plan.

At 0640 she went forward to the command module and took the binder off its clip.

The logged strips lived in order, tabbed, the paper stiffer than hers because it had never been in anyone's pocket. She pulled Day −11 and worked forward. Marchetti's hand was small and level and he wrote the same way every day, which was the quality Cold Harbor had trained into him and the quality they had picked him for.

Day −11: *loop inventory 612 kg. Δ within tolerance.* Signed, G. Marchetti.

Day −10: *loop inventory 612 kg. Δ within tolerance.*

She went through all eleven. Every one read 612. Every one read inside tolerance. Six hundred and twelve kilograms was the number the previous watch had entered, and the watch before that, and it had never in the station's life been an instrument reading; it was a figure that lived in the schema and got carried forward because the field required something. And for the last six days — since her own strip and the logged strip had quietly stopped agreeing — nobody had said a word about it, including her.

That was the piece she had to hold. She had not raised it either. She had carried the paper in her pocket for six days waiting for the accumulation to become loggable under station rule, five days minimum before a totalizer difference could be entered as a finding, and the rule had been satisfied on Day −6 and she had gone on carrying it for five more. There was a version of this where she had been careful. There was another version, and she was not yet certain which one she was in.

At 0900 the watch took the wardroom for the morning brief. Pike laid the eleven logged strips in a row down the table, and beneath them, aligned by date, her own eleven. She sat down and did not speak.

Vance got it in four seconds. She counted them, because she wanted the number.

"Yours say plus two-three," he said. "His say six-twelve and fine."

"They do."

"Every day?"

"Eleven days. Twenty-five point three kilos we did not put in."

Vance did not go for the summary screen on the bulkhead. He turned his hand over and read off the back of it, where he had pencilled the morning count of the reserve dewars, the way he did at 0500 before anyone else was up and had done every morning since Stack B went into its bag.

"One hundred eighty point zero," he said. "Six of us at zero-eight-five is five point one a day. Stack A at sixty percent derate gives me three point one. That's a two-kilo shortfall a day, every day, and at two kilos a day one-eighty is ninety days. Ninety. And that's if nothing else breaks." He tapped the pencil marks. "Every hour Stack A is off the line costs me point one three of a kilo off that. Not off the day. Off the ninety."

"I know."

"Then declare. Seal the modules, put us in suits, sit the rotation out on the bottles and let somebody come and get us. Nora, we don't know what's crossing into that loop. Stack B's membranes fouled, they didn't age. Stack A is downstream of the same water."

"If I key it, it's transmitted," Pike said. "Not discussed. Thirty-one minutes to Cold Harbor and then it's irrevocable, and the claim opens to the first hull that reaches us. That's not a safety action. That's a property transaction I can't take back."

"And ninety days is ninety days."

"Ninety days is ninety days of time to spend," she said. "I'm spending five of it."

She opened the log and wrote the finding, because eleven days of accumulated totalizer difference was loggable and a tank level was not, and she had never in two rotations signed a level.

Okoye to the slurry line: valve position, physically felt, and the line temperature the full three hundred metres. Brede to the spectrometer: D/H on loop water, on seam ice, on bottled feedstock, error bars attached. Marchetti to produce every schema entry since Day −11, unrounded, with the source of the 612.

She signed it. Five days.

Then she stood, and left her own strip lying on the table where she had put it, instead of folding it back into her pocket.
