# Chapter 8 — What Goes in the Entry

The 0600 handover pad came up from Salas with four lines on it and nothing else, which was correct. Marchetti set it beside the four voltage strips he had printed the night before and laid them out on the mess table in order, left to right, the way he laid out everything: Day +36, Day +44, Day +52, Day +60.

2.01 V. 1.94. 1.85. 1.79.

He read the gaps rather than the numbers. Eight days from the first to the second was seven hundredths; eight days to the third was nine; eight days to the last was six. A rise that had run four millivolts a day for seventy-six days had stopped rising somewhere in the eleven days after Okoye's plate went on, turned over, and come down harder than it had ever gone up. This morning's read was 1.79 V. On Day −40, with Stack B still a working machine in the next frame, Stack A had stood at 1.82.

Below where it started. That was the thing he kept coming back to, pen uncapped over the margin, not writing yet. Vance had measured 2.05 on Day +17 and drawn the line forward to 2.10 on Day +30 and told them all that Stack A was going to quit on a Tuesday. Stack B had quit at 2.10. Stack A had gone to about 2.02 and turned around, because the scale had stopped arriving and the membrane had begun, slowly, in the way of membranes, to clear.

He wrote in the margin of the Day +60 strip: *climb flattened ~D+44, reversed. 1.79 V vs 1.82 V at D−40. Below commissioning-era working point.* Then he capped the pen, because the next number was not his to produce.

Vance brought it at 0620, in his own handwriting on a torn sheet, as he had brought it every morning for sixty days: the reserve dewars counted down by hand, valve by valve, gauge by gauge, no display trusted.

67.3 kg.

Marchetti did the subtraction on the pad where anyone could follow it, because the two terms were the entire chapter of the last four weeks.

109.3 standing at 1140 on Day +33, when the valve went shut. Three days after that at the old shortfall, 2.0 kg/day, because nobody had re-read the stack yet and the log does not get to assume: 6.0 kg. Then twenty-four days from the Day +36 re-read to this morning at 1.5 kg/day, because from the re-read forward the recovery was a measurement and not a hope: 36.0 kg.

109.3 − 6.0 − 36.0 = 67.3.

It closed to the tenth against a hand count made with a torch and a wrench in a cold compartment. That almost never happened.

"It agrees," Marchetti said.

"I know it agrees." Vance had not sat down. "It agrees because the stack is making three point six."

"Five point one demand. One point five short. Yes. Three point six."

"The log carries three point one."

"Since Day minus forty."

"Then the log is wrong and I'm not signing it." Vance's hands were at his sides and open, which with him meant he had already thought it through. "Gil. Put three point six in that field and Cold Harbor reads it as headroom. They read it as the stack being fine, and the next thing in the manifest is a tonnage figure that assumes it. I have spent this whole finding refusing to sign anything that says Stack A can be run harder. I'm not going to sign it on the day it finally looks good."

"You counted the dewar."

"I counted the dewar. I'll sign the dewar. Sixty-seven point three, my hand, my name." He put the torn sheet down on the table and squared it, once, with two fingers. "I won't sign the rate that produced it."

So Marchetti had, laid out in front of him, a measured number and a signed number half a kilogram a day apart, and one field in the schema to put one of them in.

He worked it both ways in the margin, because that was the only honest thing to do with it. 67.3 divided by the signed 2.0 was 33.6 — thirty-three days, rounding the way the schema rounded, down. 67.3 divided by the measured 1.5 was 44.9 — forty-four days. Eleven days between a number that was true and a number that was signed. On Day −11 he had rounded 2.3 kg/day to nothing eleven times and it had cost five days. He wrote both figures. He left both on the pad.

Brede came through at 0740 with the rack under her arm and set it down so he could see into it. One vial. She had gone in with four.

"Three on the post-closure loop," she said. "Day plus thirty-four, plus forty-seven, plus fifty-nine. Regressed against the thirteen-day series."

"And?"

"Zero point one, plus or minus zero point three, parts per million per day." She said it the way she said everything, the error bar welded to the number, refusing to be quoted without it. "Which is flat. It is not zero. It is indistinguishable from zero at the precision of this instrument, and I will sign it as flat within error and nothing stronger."

Before Day +33 the loop had been enriching at 2.38 ± 0.30 ppm/day for thirteen consecutive days. After the plate went on the fifteen-millimetre tie-in, it was doing nothing at all.

"It stopped on the day," Marchetti said.

"It stopped on the day and, as far as three vials can tell, within the hour." She picked the rack back up. "That's the confirmation. It's also the cheapest number anybody has produced on this station."

Kestrel called at 0210, four minutes of lag now, close enough that the pauses felt like manners rather than distance. Salas took it on the 0200 and wrote it out on the pad in block capitals, the way Salas wrote everything, and signed underneath:

KESTREL ALONGSIDE D+61 APPROX 1500. ASKS AGAIN IF BITTERN WISHES TO DECLARE PRIOR TO ARRIVAL. SAYS NO PRESSURE. SAYS STACK IS IN THE HOLD EITHER WAY.

Salas had no opinion about it, and had written none, and had gone off watch.

Pike gave him the entry at 0900 and left the compartment, which was its own instruction.

The schema opened with the field pre-populated: LOOP INVENTORY 612 KG, provenance DESIGN, carried forward three hundred days and every day before that back to commissioning. Marchetti selected it and typed over it.

**627 ± 80 kg — measured.** Provenance: INSTRUMENT, D/H regression, thirteen-day series, Brede.

Under DISPOSITION he filed 25.3 kg of seam-origin water as product recovered — not yield, not surplus, product — flagged against an assay that could no longer stand, and entered the requirement: VINALIA SEAM FOUR ASSAY TO BE RERUN, BOUND WATER IN CARBONATE FRACTION NOT PREVIOUSLY COUNTED.

Then he opened Day −96 and amended Okoye's entry from a face change to an assay change, and let the schema do what it did with an amendment that old, which was to raise a flag on every entry between.

In the production field he put 3.6 kg/day, provenance INSTRUMENT, and beneath it Vance's 2.0, provenance SIGNED, and the two air figures, forty-four and thirty-three, side by side with the divisors shown.

He signed it. His name went at the bottom of that one too.
