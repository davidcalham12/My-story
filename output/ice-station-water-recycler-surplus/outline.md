# Outline — Bittern Station

## Promises

- **Where the +2.3 kg/day comes from, and what single number proves it past an error bar.** Eleven days of surplus on a sealed loop, 25.3 kg accumulated, and an instrument that reads ±10 ppm against a shift of the order of tens of ppm. The book owes the reader the measurement, not the hunch.
- **Whether the thing handing them water is the thing that killed Stack B — and whether Stack A is next, on a date they can calculate.** Stack B died on Day −40, twenty-nine days before the surplus appeared. The book owes the reader the mechanism that makes those two facts one fact, and the day Stack A reaches the same voltage.
- **Who loaded a replacement stack onto Kestrel before anyone aboard asked for one.** No human transmitted anything. The book owes the reader the timestamp, and an answer to whether a form can betray a crew, and whose name is at the bottom of it.
- **Where 25.3 kg went while the tanks read nominal.** The water is aboard. The record is not. The book owes the reader the discovery that 612 kg was never an instrument reading, and the price of the five days that cost.
- **Whether Pike keys distress.** Declaring buys fourteen days of Kestrel's arrival and loses the Occator claim irrevocably, thirty-one minutes after the key. Not declaring bets six lives on a slope measured in millivolts per day. The book owes the reader the moment the key is live and the choice is made.

## Chapters

### Chapter 1 — The Second Strip
- **POV:** Nora Pike
- **Tension:** 5/10
- **Promise advanced:** Where the +2.3 kg/day comes from; whether Pike keys distress
- **Day:** Day 0 (180.0 kg LOX, 90 days of air — exhaustion Day +90)
- **Beats:**
  1. Pike takes the 0600 handover from Salas, accepts the printed totalizer strip he has signed without reading, and adds it to the ten folded in her pocket since Day −6: eleven consecutive days of outlet over inlet, +2.3 kg on 61 kg throughput, 25.3 kg accumulated, against a ±0.5% daily tolerance.
  2. She pulls the logged strips from the command module binder and finds every one of Marchetti's eleven entries reads loop inventory 612 kg and every one reads inside tolerance; her strips and the station's strips have not agreed for six days and nobody has said so.
  3. At 0900 she puts both sets on the wardroom table, side by side, and says nothing until someone else names the difference; Vance names it in four seconds and argues immediately for declaring, sealing the modules and sitting the rotation out in suits.
  4. Vance reads the burn-down off his own hand count rather than the screen: 180 kg of reserve at a 2.0 kg/day shortfall is ninety days, Stack A at 60% derate makes 3.1 against 5.1 demand, and every hour Stack A spends off the line costs 0.13 kg of the ninety.
  5. Pike refuses to key and opens a five-day finding instead — Okoye to the slurry line, Brede to the spectrometer, Marchetti to produce every schema entry since Day −11 — writes the tasking in the log, signs it, and leaves her pocket strip on the table instead of putting it back.

### Chapter 2 — Inside Tolerance
- **POV:** Gil Marchetti
- **Tension:** 6/10
- **Promise advanced:** Where 25.3 kg went; who loaded the stack onto Kestrel
- **Day:** Day +2 (176.0 kg LOX, 88 days of air — exhaustion Day +90)
- **Beats:**
  1. Marchetti prints eleven days of Cold Harbor schema entries and finds his own name at the bottom of all eleven, each carrying loop inventory 612 kg in a mandatory field the form will not let him leave blank or flag.
  2. He chases 612 backward through the record: handover entry Day −300 carried forward from the previous watch, which carried it from the watch before, back to commissioning — there is no ultrasonic reading of the potable loop anywhere in the station's history, and the number the whole watch has been reasoning from has never been measured by anything.
  3. He reconstructs his own rounding day by day and totals what he entered as zero: 2.3, 2.3, 2.3, eleven times, 25.3 kg, every day of it defensible under his reading that a discrepancy inside instrument tolerance is not yet a fact.
  4. In the transmission log he finds an automatic Cold Harbor schema pull at 0412 on Day −29 carrying the stack-fault flag he himself set on Day −40, sent by no one, received thirty-one minutes later, and within the day standing on a Kestrel loading manifest.
  5. He takes the 612 problem to Pike and keeps the pull to himself; then he opens the Day +2 entry and finds the inventory field already pre-populated with 612.

### Chapter 3 — A Valve You Can Feel
- **POV:** Sam Okoye
- **Tension:** 7/10
- **Promise advanced:** Where the +2.3 kg/day comes from
- **Day:** Day +6 (167.6 kg LOX after two airlock cycles, 83.8 days of air — exhaustion Day +89)
- **Beats:**
  1. Okoye suits with Salas tending and spends two of the twelve budgeted airlock cycles to reach the mine head — 1.8 kg of atmosphere, 0.4 kg of it oxygen — and the command module strip is re-cut that evening from ninety days to eighty-nine.
  2. He walks all 300 m of heated line with a glove on every jacket section and finds the setpoint he raised on Day −14 to clear a slug still sitting fourteen degrees high, where he left it, unlogged as anything but a slug.
  3. At the isolation valve he confirms by hand what the log claims — full open, stem out, felt through the glove — and then finds what no drawing aboard has ever drawn: a flash-vent tie-in downstream of the inlet totalizer, warm to the touch, carrying slurry-line vapour straight into the recycler's vapour-compression stage.
  4. He chips two samples for Brede and labels them by location only — one from the fresh carbonate lens he advanced into on Day −96, one from the worked-face product on the billet rail — refusing to write on the label what he thinks either of them is.
  5. Back inboard, still in the undersuit, he gives the watch the sentence that reframes everything: the inlet totalizer counts litres of liquid, the tie-in carries steam, and nothing aboard Bittern has ever counted steam as anything at all.

### Chapter 4 — Without a Baseline
- **POV:** Hannah Brede
- **Tension:** 5/10
- **Promise advanced:** Where the +2.3 kg/day comes from
- **Day:** Day +11 (156.8 kg LOX after a six-hour Stack A outage, 78.4 days of air — exhaustion Day +89)
- **Beats:**
  1. Brede runs Okoye's two chips on two of her twenty-two vials: worked face 171 ± 10 ppm D/H, bound water from the new lens 820 ± 10 ppm — two reservoirs six hundred and fifty ppm apart, which is the only piece of luck in the book.
  2. She runs the potable loop at 213 ppm and refuses to report the figure to the watch, because no archived pre-surplus loop sample exists anywhere aboard and an offset without a baseline is not a measurement, it is a number with a story attached.
  3. Vance breaks Stack A for six hours so she can swab the feed side clean of carryover before sampling; the outage costs 0.78 kg against the reserve and Vance writes the figure on the strip in his own hand before he opens the breaker, not after.
  4. She designs the only experiment her instrument can win: abandon the offset, measure the slope — paired loop and outlet samples every day, regressed against day number, because a rate of change needs no baseline and 2.3 kg/day of 820 ppm water into a 612 kg loop must climb at 2.49 ppm/day or the whole theory is wrong.
  5. She tells the wardroom she will have nothing for thirteen days, will not be asked for a preliminary opinion in the interval, and starts the series with six vials — fourteen left, and Vance does the division out loud.

### Chapter 5 — Cell Voltage, Morning
- **POV:** Theo Vance
- **Tension:** 4/10
- **Promise advanced:** Whether the thing giving water killed Stack B; who loaded the stack onto Kestrel
- **Day:** Day +17 (144.8 kg LOX, 72.4 days of air — exhaustion Day +89)
- **Beats:**
  1. Vance does the thing he has done every morning since Day −40: dewar ullage and pressure by hand at 0530, both reserves, written on paper before he permits himself to look at the summary screen — 144.8 kg, and the screen agrees, and he trusts the paper.
  2. He pulls Stack A's forty days of cell-voltage record for the first time since the derate was set and finds it is not a derate at all: 1.82 V at Day −40, climbing 0.004 V per day, a straight line through forty points.
  3. He extrapolates to the voltage at which Stack B quit and gets Day +30 — after which Stack A produces nothing, the draw becomes the full 5.1 kg/day, and the 115 kg standing on that date is twenty-two days: dead on Day +53, eight days before Kestrel.
  4. Kestrel calls at nineteen minutes of light-lag, patient and generous, to confirm the replacement stack is in the hold, to ask after the watch by name, and to mention without pressing that they can burn early the instant Bittern declares.
  5. He does not carry it to Pike that night; he taps it through the bulkhead to Marchetti, who says the word *tolerance*, and Vance says the word *slope*, and they lie in adjacent compartments listening to each other's pumps until Salas's 0200 relief.

### Chapter 6 — The Bag
- **POV:** Hannah Brede
- **Tension:** 8/10
- **Promise advanced:** Where the +2.3 kg/day comes from; whether the same mechanism killed Stack B; where 25.3 kg went
- **Day:** Day +24 (127.7 kg LOX after a full-day Stack A outage, 63.8 days of air — exhaustion Day +87)
- **Beats:**
  1. Brede closes the series at fifteen vials and regresses: slope 2.38 ± 0.30 ppm/day, non-zero at eight standard errors, and she signs that sentence with the error bar attached and the word *slope* in it and the word *cause* deliberately absent.
  2. She inverts the arithmetic for Pike: 2.3 kg/day of 820 ppm water entering a loop of L kilograms gives 2.49 ppm/day at L = 612, and her measurement puts L at 640 ± 80 kg — the first instrument estimate of the loop inventory ever made aboard Bittern, and it sits exactly on 612 plus the 25.3 kg Marchetti rounded away.
  3. Vance carries the Day −40 bag from the workshop to the med bay and opens it for the first time since he sealed it; the membranes come out white and crusted rather than grey and worn, and the swab runs as sodium carbonate, not as age.
  4. Stack A comes off the line for a full day for its own feed-side swab — 3.1 kg gone, the strip re-cut from eighty-nine days to eighty-seven — and Stack A's membranes show the same white, thinner, exactly forty days behind.
  5. Pike asks for the one-line version and Brede gives it with the error bars in it: the fresh lens has been handing them water and scale down the same tie-in, scale since the face changed on Day −96 and water since the setpoint went up on Day −14, and closing the valve stops both, and stopping both stops the free water they are now living on.

### Chapter 7 — Thirty-Three
- **POV:** Nora Pike
- **Tension:** 9/10
- **Promise advanced:** Whether Pike keys distress; who loaded the stack onto Kestrel; whether Stack A is next
- **Day:** Day +33 (109.3 kg LOX after two more airlock cycles, 54.6 days of air — exhaustion Day +87)
- **Beats:**
  1. Pike works the whole decision onto one sheet in pencil: key distress and Kestrel burns early for Day +47 and the Occator claim passes to the first hull alongside thirty-one minutes after transmission; do not key, and Kestrel is Day +61, which is eight days after the date Vance's voltage line kills them — and today is the last day the early burn is still reachable.
  2. Marchetti puts the 0412 Day −29 pull in front of her and offers to stand down; Pike does not accept it, because the pull carried a field he was required by the schema to fill, and the same schema is why 612 stood for a decade unmeasured — the form did both, and his name is at the bottom of both.
  3. Okoye spends two more cycles, closes the isolation valve by hand at 1140 and blanks the flash-vent tie-in with a plate he cut that morning, stopping the slurry, the billets, the contract tonnage and the surplus in the same motion — 0.4 kg gone, 109.3 kg standing, six cycles left in the budget.
  4. Cold Harbor answers the previous day's query sixty-two minutes late with an instruction to keep the face cut and book the surplus as yield; Pike logs the instruction verbatim, logs beneath it that she has disregarded it and why, and signs both entries.
  5. At 2300, alone in the command module with the key live and the declaration composed on the pad in front of her, Pike does not send it — she writes instead that the watch is held to a Stack A voltage re-read on Day +36, and then enters eleven days of her own pocket strips into the permanent record, backdated and initialled, for the first time.

### Chapter 8 — What Goes in the Entry
- **POV:** Gil Marchetti
- **Tension:** 7/10
- **Promise advanced:** Whether Stack A is next; where 25.3 kg went; whether Pike keys distress
- **Day:** Day +60 (67.3 kg LOX, 33 days of air at the signed 2.0 kg/day — 44 days at the measured 1.5)
- **Beats:**
  1. Marchetti stands the 0600 with four voltage re-reads laid out — Day +36, +44, +52, +60 — and reads the shape of them: the climb flattened eleven days after the valve closed and then came down, 1.79 V this morning, production 3.6 kg/day against the 3.1 the log has carried since Day −40.
  2. Vance hands him the hand-counted dewar figure, 67.3 kg, seven kilograms ahead of where the ninety-day line says it should be, and then refuses to sign the 1.5 kg/day shortfall that produced it, because signing it would imply Stack A can be run harder — leaving Marchetti a measured number and a signed number 0.5 kg/day apart and one field to put one of them in.
  3. Brede spends three of her last five vials on the post-closure loop and reports the slope flat within error at 0.1 ± 0.3 ppm/day: the seam stopped giving on the day and the hour Okoye's plate went on, which is the confirmation the whole finding was always waiting for and the cheapest number in the book.
  4. Kestrel calls at four minutes of lag, alongside tomorrow, still kind, asking once more and very gently whether Bittern would care to declare before they arrive; Salas takes the call on the 0200, writes it on the pad in block capitals, signs it, and has no opinion about it.
  5. Pike gives Marchetti the entry and leaves him alone with the schema: he overwrites the pre-populated 612 with 640 ± 80 kg and the word *measured*, files 25.3 kg of seam-origin water as product against a Vinalia Seam Four assay that must now be rerun, appends Okoye's Day −96 face change as an assay change, and signs it — his name at the bottom of that one too.
