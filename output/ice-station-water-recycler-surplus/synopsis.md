Bittern Station has ninety days of air, and the loop that is supposed to be sealed is handing back water it never took in — 2.3 kilograms too many, every day, for eleven days running. Nobody aboard can make water without spending oxygen or hydrogen already spoken for, and forty days before the surplus began, the station's second electrolysis stack died, leaving six people breathing down a reserve that empties on a date Nora Pike can already estimate. She is the only one who can key the distress call that would end the mystery and the mission both, and she will not sign for numbers she has not watched accumulate herself. Around her, a mine engineer who trusts nothing with a screen, a medic who will not report a figure without its error bar, a life-support tech counting reserve oxygen by hand each morning, and a recycler technician guarding a logbook whose founding number turns out to have never been a reading at all — five people working the same shaft from five directions, one pipe at a time, one sample at a time, toward a source that may also be what killed the first stack. A tug is sixty-one days out, already loaded with a replacement part nobody aboard requested. Someone, somewhere, already knows what is wrong. This is a mystery told in gauge readings and logbook entries, where the clue that saves six lives is a number nobody wanted to be right.
