# Bittern Station — World Bible

Bittern Station sits on the western rim of Occator crater on Ceres, forty-one metres of regolith and sintered brick over a habitat of six pressurised modules, working a face of hydrated sodium-carbonate brine ice that the survey called Vinalia Seam Four. It is a water mine. Everything it does is water: it cuts slurry, boils it, freezes the clean product into 900 kg billets, and stacks the billets on a launch rail for a tug that comes twice a year. Six people hold the standing watch on a fourteen-month rotation. Surface gravity is 0.028 g, which is enough to keep coffee in a cup and not enough to weigh anything, so nothing aboard is measured by how heavy it is — only by flow, level, pressure and time. Forty days ago the number two electrolysis stack failed. Eleven days ago the potable loop began coming back richer than it went out.

## Factions

- **Cold Harbor Consortium (the operator, 31 light-minutes away)** — wants the seam worked to contract tonnage and the surplus booked as yield, not as a fault; it pays per billet railed and loses the Occator claim if the station is logged uninhabitable. It gets its way by owning the logbook schema, the resupply manifest and the authority to refuse an unscheduled burn.
- **The standing watch (the six aboard)** — wants to still be breathing in ninety days, and disagrees internally about whether that is best served by finding the cause or by declaring and sitting in suits. It gets its way through the duty log, the valve positions and who is willing to sign a reading.
- **Kestrel Tug Line** — wants the seam. Its tug is sixty-one days out with the replacement electrolysis stack in the hold, and under registry law the first vessel to reach a station that has declared distress inherits the working claim. It gets its way by arriving, slowly, on schedule, and by being very helpful on the radio.

## Means

- **The recycler (vapour-compression distillation plus multifiltration, loop inventory 612 kg)** — takes crew grey water and metered mine slurry, returns potable. Inlet and outlet totalizers count to ±0.02 kg per pass.
- **The oxygen plant** — electrolysis Stack A at 60% derate, plus 180 kg of liquid oxygen in two reserve dewars. Stack B is a sealed brick of failed membranes in a bag in the workshop.
- **The benchtop mass spectrometer** — medical-bay instrument, reads deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio in a 5 ml sample to ±10 ppm. Seam ice, crew loop water and bottled feedstock hydrogen each have a different D/H signature, and mixtures resolve by proportion.
- **The mine head and slurry line** — a cutting head in Seam Four, 300 m of heated line, one isolation valve, and a seam whose carbonates carry bound water that no assay ever counted as product.

## Rules

- The potable loop is sealed downstream of the slurry isolation valve. Nothing enters it except metered flow through the inlet totalizer, and over any 24-hour window outlet must match inlet within ±0.5%. The loop is currently running +2.3 kg/day on 61 kg/day throughput — about +3.8% — and has done so for eleven consecutive days.
- The ninety-day air figure is derived, not stated: six crew at 0.85 kg oxygen per person per day is 5.1 kg/day demand; Stack A makes 3.1 kg/day; the 2.0 kg/day shortfall is drawn from 180 kg of reserve LOX. Every full day Stack A is offline costs an additional 3.1 kg and shortens the ninety.
- Water is eight parts oxygen to one part hydrogen by mass. Nothing aboard can make water without spending oxygen or hydrogen already on the inventory, and no process aboard can make oxygen except electrolysis, which consumes water and power.
- The station cannot weigh anything. Tank quantities come from ultrasonic level at ±1.5% — ±9 kg on a 600 kg tank — so a 2 kg discrepancy is invisible in a day and undeniable over a week. Only totalizer differences accumulated across five days or more may be entered in the log as findings.
- A distress declaration is transmitted, not discussed: once keyed it is irrevocable, it reaches Cold Harbor 31 minutes later, and it opens the claim to the first arriving vessel. Only the officer of the watch may key it, and only from the command module.
- Each airlock cycle vents 0.9 kg of atmosphere, of which 0.2 kg is oxygen. The consumable budget allows twelve more cycles before the ninety becomes eighty-nine. Every trip to the mine head costs two.

## Texture

Bittern is warm, loud and damp: pumps you stop hearing on day three and notice the instant they change pitch, condensate beading on the cold side of every bulkhead, the sweetish carbonate smell that gets into clothing and never washes out. Work is shift-shaped and written down — six-hour watches, handwritten annotations in the margins of printed totalizer strips because the crew stopped trusting the summary screens years before anyone aboard now was born. The dark outside is not dramatic; it is simply where the numbers come from. And the numbers are good news formatted as a threat. There is more water than there was. Nobody put it there. In a system this closed, generosity is a symptom.
