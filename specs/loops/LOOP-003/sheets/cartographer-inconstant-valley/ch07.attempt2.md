OPEN — Continuity (2 findings) and Science (1 finding), worst first
  1. [high] A fragment of editorial apparatus has got into the prose
     Quote:        "But it was signed, every walk of it, by the incumbent of the day, and R and the rule stood plain in the printed instructions in his own bag: no field outside the walked bound could be numbered, and the walked bound was what the incumbent signed."
     What is wrong: "and R and the rule stood plain" is broken. "R" is a stray label that has leaked out of a rule reference and into the sentence, and it leaves the grammar in pieces. This is not a matter of taste: a reader who meets a loose letter of apparatus in a finished book stops believing the book was read by anybody.
     Against what:  nothing in canon — this is the sentence contradicting itself. The substance it is trying to state is correct and comes from bible/world.md, the last rule: no field outside the walked bound can be numbered, and the bound is the one walked at Rogationtide and signed by the incumbent.
     How it should read: The same sentence with the stray letter gone — "and the rule stood plain in the printed instructions in his own bag". Nothing else about it changes; the law it states is right.

  2. [medium] The instrument is given an intention the world never claims for it
     Quote:        "It was not a warning. He had left it with a sight unfinished and it had settled, the way water settles into the beck, onto the one direction that was not in dispute. It had been telling him where to look. It had been trying to tell him for a week, and he had been standing over it asking it how far to a wall."
     What is wrong: The passage is beautiful and its reading is the right one. The trouble is who is saying it. As written, the narration asserts as plain fact that the instrument was *telling* him something and had been *trying* to for a week — a purpose, an intention, a thing that wants. The world says only what happens: an instrument left out is found at dawn turned to the church tower, no mark in the grass, and it offers no motive at all. Put the same understanding in the surveyor's head, where it belongs and where it is far more frightening, and the passage keeps everything it has.
     Against what:  bible/world.md, the rule about instruments left out after dark — it states the phenomenon flatly and gives it no cause, no malice and no intent.
     How it should read: Keep every image, including the water settling into the beck. Change only the standing of the claim: he understood it as an instruction; it was easier to think it had been telling him where to look than to think there was no reason in it at all. He is the one reaching this, and the chapter is about him reaching it. **Do not hedge away the understanding itself** — the outline commissions him understanding that it was not a warning but an instruction, and if that disappears the chapter loses a beat. Anchor it to him; do not remove it.

  3. [medium] One entry in the parish book can be turned into a distance
     Quote:        "From Hird's thorn, by the gate, a quarter of an hour at a man's pace to the sheepfold that was Wilfrid Tatham's."
     What is wrong: A quarter of an hour is a clock, and "at a man's pace" is a rate, and a clock times a rate is a distance. Every other passage the book keeps is tied to a recitation — the length of a psalm, the Creed, the Lord's Prayer said twice — which cannot be converted by anybody. This one can, and it is the single line in the chapter a reader could do arithmetic on, three paragraphs before the narration says that not one of those hands wrote a number to say how far. Worth knowing: the world critic read all four quoted passages and did not call this a breach of the speech rule, so this is not being sent back as unlawful. It is being sent back because it is out of the book's own idiom and it hands away the one thing the book has spent a century refusing to hand away.
     Against what:  bible/world.md, the rule about what dale-born hands write — the perambulation book keeps the bound in durations and the names of dead men, and nothing they have written holds a distance to read.
     How it should read: A recitation, not a clock, and no rate attached — the same shape as the others in the passage. A psalm, a creed, a prayer said so many times. Keep Hird's thorn, keep the gate, keep Wilfrid Tatham's sheepfold; change only the measure between them.
