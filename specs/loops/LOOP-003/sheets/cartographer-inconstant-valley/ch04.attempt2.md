OPEN — Continuity (3 findings) and Science (1 finding), worst first
  1. [high] The surveyor has been moved into the house of the interested party
     Quote:        "By candlelight in the room at Hollins Hall, Hale turned back to Monday's page and read his own margin note through twice."
     What is wrong: Hale does not lodge at Hollins Hall. He lodges at Nether Scarrow, at the inn, and nothing has moved him. Hollins Hall is the estate that is paying for this survey and wants the bound drawn where it wants it, and putting the surveyor's bed inside it — silently, in a subordinate clause — gives away something the story has not decided to give away yet. This is the worst kind of small error: nobody notices it and it changes the book.
     Against what:  bible/timeline.md, Day 1 — "Hale comes in over Sile Ford, measures the ford stone as a check, and lodges at Nether Scarrow."
     How it should read: The same sentence, the same candle, the same turning back to the page — but in his room at the inn at Nether Scarrow. Nothing else about the closing passage changes.

  2. [medium] Two figures are struck out and one of them survives
     Quote:        "He struck out the longest and the shortest and the two that were left were eleven forty and ten ninety-six"
     What is wrong: Of the four figures, the longest is twelve eleven and the shortest is ten ninety-six. Strike those two and what is left is eleven seventy-two and eleven forty. The sentence keeps ten ninety-six, which is the very figure it has just said was struck out. This is the one place in the chapter where a reader following the numbers catches the surveyor's own arithmetic being wrong, and it must not be, because his arithmetic is the last thing he has.
     Against what:  the four figures the draft itself states, and bible/characters.md, Edmund Hale — "methodical, proud of his arithmetic".
     How it should read: The two left standing are eleven seventy-two and eleven forty. The point of the sentence survives untouched: even the middle two do not agree, and there is no honest way to call them one line.

  3. [medium] The leading man is drawing the arrows he ought to be setting
     Quote:        "Coulter held; Fowler drew the arrow and went on"
     What is wrong: Fowler is on the leading end. The leader carries the arrows and STICKS one in the ground at each chain-length as he goes; it is the man behind who pulls them up as he comes to them. As written the leader is pulling up an arrow and walking forward, which is not how a chain goes out, and it contradicts the picture of the work the chapter otherwise gets exactly right.
     Against what:  bible/world.md, ## Means — the Gunter's chain and its ten arrows, worked by two chainmen.
     How it should read: Fowler, leading, sets the arrow and goes on; Coulter, following, is the one who draws it as he comes to it. Half a line, and only this one clause — the later moment where Fowler gathers the arrows at the end of the run and puts them into Hale's hand is CORRECT and must not be touched. See the note below.

  4. [medium] The ford stone is claimed to be stable in a way the rule does not grant
     Quote:        "He had measured to it and from it eleven times in five days. It was the one thing in Scarrowdale he no longer had to think about."
     What is wrong: What is guaranteed constant is the ford stone itself, and the ford stone taken in relation to the other two constants — the church tower and the boundary oak. A chain distance run from some ordinary point on the ground to the stone is not covered by that, and would wander like anything else. As written the passage promises a reliability the rule does not extend to it, which will cost the book later, when the whole plan is rebuilt on exactly this guarantee and the reader needs it to have been stated correctly the first time.
     Against what:  bible/world.md, R2 — the three measure the same for everyone singly and in relation to one another, and angles taken directly among those three close identically; everything else measures differently each time.
     How it should read: What he has taken eleven times is the stone against the church tower — one constant to another, which is precisely the thing that holds. Keep the beat exactly as it is: he rides past without looking down, because this is the one thing he no longer has to think about, and he is right about it for reasons he does not yet understand.
