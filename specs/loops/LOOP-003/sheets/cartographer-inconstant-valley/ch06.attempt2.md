OPEN — Continuity (1 finding)
  1. [high] The lantern cannot be lit on both passes and useless on the second
     Quote:        "He saw Mr Brock pass. Then he saw him pass again. Before the light, both times, and he had a lantern with him going, and he had it still lit coming back, which is a queer thing, because by then he'd no use for it."
     What is wrong: The sentence says two things that will not both stand. It says both sightings were before the light, and it says that on the second one he had no use for the lantern — and the only reason given for having no use for a lantern is that it has got light. Worse, it collides with what is already fixed about that morning: when the agent reached the surveyor on the fell top he came **out of the dark** and the flame of his lantern was **already just killed**. A lit lantern on a later pass will not square with a dead one at the end of the journey. The son's sighting is the single piece of evidence the next two chapters are reconstructed from, so it has to be something a reader can lay alongside the rest and have it fit.
     Against what:  bible/timeline.md, the ninth day — the agent comes up the fell before the dale's dawn carrying the surveyor's field book, with his lantern already out, and says the light will reach Raven Hause shortly; the entry is then taken with the light standing on the fell.
     How it should read: Keep everything that makes the testimony valuable — the lad up all night with the ewe, two passes, both of them at an hour when a man has no business being abroad, and her flat refusal to say what it means. Fix only the lantern's logic. The straightforward repair is to let the sky have greyed between the two passes without the light yet being on Raven Hause: he went by with it lit when it was black, and came by again with it still lit when it had greyed enough that a man could see his feet, and that is what struck the lad as queer — not that dawn had come, but that a careful man was still burning oil he did not need. Then it sits easily beside a flame killed a little later, at the top. However you phrase it, the son must be able to have seen both passes and the lantern must be able to be dead by the time it reaches the fell top.
