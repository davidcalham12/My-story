  1. [high] The third entry is made outside the hour the rule allows
     Quote:        "Nell Harker's petition of the fourteenth lay where she had set it two years' worth of refusal ago, the scrap of net float still knotted through it, and she copied it out at last within the hour of taking it up: *Marigold, ketch, sou'-sou'-west.* The third."
     What is wrong: The entry is the hinge of the whole book and as written it breaks the first rule. A petition is a report of a vessel's passing, and the hour runs from the moment the keeper receives it — which was two days earlier, when it was left on the step. Calling the moment she picks the paper up again "taking it up" does not restart the hour. An entry made outside the hour is not a valid entry, and an invalid entry cannot be the third one, and if it is not the third one the name never reaches the Drowned Page and the book has no ending.
     Against what:  bible/world.md, first rule — only within the hour of the sighting, and a sighting is any report of a vessel's passing that reaches the keeper, the hour counted from the moment the keeper receives it. Also bible/characters.md, Nell Harker — she leaves petitions on the seventh step **at every low water**, and has done for nine years.
     How it should read: There is a petition on the step this morning. Nell leaves one every low water and always has; the one Ada copies out is the one that came up at this morning's low water, received within the hour, with the same name on it and the same scrap of net float as all the others. That makes the entry valid, and it costs the moment nothing — the weight is not in which piece of paper it is, it is that after two years of the same request arriving and being refused, this is the morning she writes it. Keep "two years' worth of refusal"; attach it to the asking, not to that one sheet of paper. Keep the line she writes exactly as it is.

  2. [medium] The reading aloud is attributed to a rule that does not require it
     Quote:        "At the turn of the tide Ada read both aloud in the lamp room as the rule required, the hour on the page and the hour on the return, and the sound of them went out into the salt and did not come back with anything."
     What is wrong: "as the rule required" is false. The rule that puts two lines into the air at the turn of the tide is about a correction written beneath an original **in the Register**. What Ada is holding against the page here is a Commissioners' return, which is not a Register line and not a correction. No rule obliges this reading at all.
     Against what:  bible/world.md, second rule — no entry may be struck out; a correction is written beneath the original, and both lines are read aloud in the lamp room at the next turn of the tide. Neither of the two hours being read here is a correction under it.
     How it should read: Take out the claim that a rule required it and leave the act standing. She reads them aloud at the turn of the tide because that is the hour this place reads things aloud in and because there is nothing else to be done with two times that cannot both be true — not because she is obliged to. That is a better reason than the false one, and it is three words shorter.
