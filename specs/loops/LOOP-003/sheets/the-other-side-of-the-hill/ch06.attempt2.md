OPEN — Prose (4 findings, worst first)

  1. [major] The book's one big joke is reported rather than told
     Quote:        "It's called Captain Crunch," he said quickly, as if speed would help. "I named it when I was eight. After a cereal."
     What is wrong: the reader is told he is embarrassed and told the name is silly, and then Elena is kind about it. The kindness lands. The joke does not. A ten-year-old listening will not laugh here, and this is the one place in the whole novel where a laugh was available.
     Against what:  The prose characteristic, and the tone this book was commissioned in — warm, funny, a little brave.
     How it should read: let the comedy be in the specifics and in the pause. He does not have to explain the cereal; a twelve-year-old either knows it or asks. Elena's reply is where the laugh is: she can take the name completely seriously, which is funnier than any joke he could make, or she can ask one flat simple question about it. Keep her kindness — do not have her mock him — but let the exchange be funny as well as sweet. One extra line of dialogue is probably all it needs.

  2. [major] "reading" and "turning over" have worn out by the last paragraph

     What is wrong: "reading" twice in one sentence, after "studying it the way a reader studies a page" and "as if she were reading two stories that fit together" earlier, and "turning something over in her mind" three lines before. The image was specific and characterful when it belonged to her hands and the fossils; stretched over her mind and the hill and the earth, it goes slack.
     Against what:  The prose characteristic — a fact restated, and an image spent.
     How it should read: keep ONE of the two "reading"s in this sentence at most, and prefer none. The last image of the chapter should be concrete — the sun on the stones, her face, the hill — rather than another figure about interpretation.
