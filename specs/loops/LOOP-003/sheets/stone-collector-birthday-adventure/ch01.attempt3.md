OPEN — Prose (2 findings, worst first)
  1. [major] The scene slips out of the story into a general "you"
     Quote:        "when you lay inside it, the garden fell silent beyond the cardboard. It was just you and the heavens, and Pau lay solid beside you."
     What is wrong: The chapter is told in third person about one boy, and this sentence switches to a general "you" as if explaining how cardboard observatories work in principle. A ten-year-old reading aloud loses track of who is in the box. Worse, it puts Pau beside him a full paragraph before Pau lies down, so the order of events breaks.
     Against what:  bible/characters.md — this is one boy's tenth birthday told close to him, not a description of a kind of place. And the paragraph that follows has Pau lie down first and the boy slide in beside him, which this sentence contradicts.
     How it should read: Same picture, same length, but about him specifically, and without Pau arriving early.
     Replacement:   when [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] lay inside it, the garden fell silent beyond the cardboard. It was just him and the sky, and the warm cardboard against his shoulders.

  2. [major] Pau makes a promise the sky does not keep
     Quote:        "It was just the sky, pale blue and simple. But Pau's voice had changed when he said it."
     What is wrong: Pau points, his voice changes, he says that is what he wanted you to see — and what is there is empty blue. The build-up is real and the payoff is nothing, so the biggest moment of the chapter lands flat. The reader is told something mattered instead of being shown what.
     Against what:  bible/mysteries.md — the promise this chapter must fully answer is what Pau prepared for this birthday, and its truth is that the observatory was meant to be a time machine for seeing the past and future of the stars. The pointing should aim at that, so the note in the book two paragraphs later pays off what the finger started.
     How it should read: Leave Pau's spoken line exactly as it is. Replace only the narration after it, so the empty blue becomes the point rather than an anticlimax, and the reader leans toward the book that is coming.
     Replacement:   It was just blue. Empty blue, all the way up. But Pau was pointing at one particular piece of it, the way you point at a friend's house, and [NOMBRE_ANONIMIZADO] understood that something was up there whether he could see it or not.
